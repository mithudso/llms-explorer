## Definitions

- [statement] On AWS, Gen1 Provisioned IOPS and Gen2 High Performance IOPS refer to the same performance class, backed by Provisioned IOPS SSD volumes. Gen2 Extended Standard IOPS uses the Standard class instead; these options have different latency and consistency characteristics. ## Measurements and reference values

- [statement] AWS Gen1 M30+ standard storage starts at 3,000 IOPS. At 1 TB or more Atlas applies a 3:1 IOPS-to-GB ratio, subject to the 16,000 standard-IOPS ceiling. Gen2 also includes a storage-dependent baseline: 3,000 below 1,000 GB and 3 IOPS/GB above 1,000 GB. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-14) — https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/customize-storage.md

## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] EC2 bounds EBS performance by the smaller of the instance limit and aggregate attached-volume performance. Provisioning more volume IOPS than the instance can deliver wastes that extra provisioned capacity; MongoDB explicitly warns of this when discussing combined volumes. (high confidence) — https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ebs-optimized.html · keywords: Gen1 versus Gen2 IOPS provisioning and instance-level EBS/VM IOPS caps · also: https://www.mongodb.com/company/blog/technical/maximizing-mongodb-performance-on-aws
- [statement] Disk and host I/O limits both matter: AWS documents interdependent IOPS and throughput limits, while Azure gives VM and disk limits separately. Increasing disk IOPS alone cannot overcome a lower host limit; the binding limit must be identified before selecting a larger disk or VM. (high confidence) — https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ebs-optimized.html · keywords: Gen1 versus Gen2 IOPS provisioning and instance-level EBS/VM IOPS caps · also: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-machines/disks-performance
- [statement] AWS bounds instance EBS performance by the smaller of the instance limits and aggregate attached-volume performance. I/O size can cause throughput to bind before IOPS. Inference for cost planning: paying for additional disk IOPS cannot resolve an already binding instance limit. ## Comparisons and alternatives
