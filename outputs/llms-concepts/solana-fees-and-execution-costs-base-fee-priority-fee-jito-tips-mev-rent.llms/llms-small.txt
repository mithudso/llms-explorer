## Structure and components

- [statement] The JLP-market price impact fee has two parts. The linear part rounds up to whole basis points, so every trade pays at least 0.01%; at October 2026 parameters SOL adds 0.01% per started ~$375,000 of size. The additive part applies only when a trade enlarges the roughly one-minute net long-minus-short flow beyond a per-asset threshold ($1.5M on SOL and ETH, $750,000 on wBTC). Base, linear and additive fees together cap at 0.50% of trade size, so the price impact fee is at most 0.44%. ## Measurements and reference values

- [statement] Raydium separates price impact from slippage and tolerance. Price impact, impact = (p_before - p_after) / p_before, is a deterministic property of a trade against a specific pool state and can be computed before submission (about 2 x amount_in / reserve_in for small constant-product trades; piecewise on CLMM pools). Realized slippage is (quoted_out - actual_out) / quoted_out. Tolerance is a buffer around the impact: minAmountOut = amountOut x (1 - tolerance), so a 2% impact with 0.5% tolerance puts the floor about 2.5% below pre-trade spot. (low confidence) — https://docs.raydium.io/algorithms/slippage-and-price-impact

## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] Gasless swaps cost extra. When the taker holds under 0.01 SOL and Jupiter fronts the SOL, it raises the swap fee by the USD value of the SOL it pays, converted into the traded token, on top of the normal swap fee. The extra is a fixed amount rather than a percentage, so small trades pay proportionally more; Jupiter says gasless is offered only when the recovered gas is within 10% of the trade (about a $10 minimum). In Swap V2 the extra shows in top-level feeBps but not in platformFee.feeBps (documented example: 5 bps platform plus 7 bps gasless gives feeBps 12). JupiterZ RFQ routes are gasless at no added cost because the market maker pays the fees. (medium confidence) (verified as of 2026-11-06) — https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/trade/swap/fees.md · keywords: Solana fees and execution costs: base fee priority fee Jito tips MEV rent · also: https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap/v2/fees.md, https://developers.jup.ag/blog/ultra-v3
- [statement] Migrating from Swap API V1 changes fee data: V2 route plans report bps instead of percent, and V2 instructions no longer emit fee events, so fee-accounting code written for V1 must change. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-11-06) — https://developers.jup.ag/docs/swap/v2/routing
- [statement] Jito warns that skipped slots produce uncled blocks, and transactions from a bundle can be rebroadcast from them. Such rebroadcast transactions do not respect bundle atomicity or revert protection, so they can land and revert on-chain. Jito advises putting the tip inside the main transaction rather than a standalone tip transaction, and adding pre/post account checks. (low confidence) — https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/
- [statement] BAM (Block Assembly Marketplace) moves transaction sequencing into AMD SEV-SNP TEE nodes that keep transactions encrypted until execution. Validators execute the pre-sequenced result, can fall back to their own TPU, and can run plugins for application-controlled ordering rules. Helius flags the risks as TEE vulnerabilities, node-operator censorship and centralization. It says MEV is relocated rather than eliminated. (low confidence) (verified as of 2025-07-31) — https://www.helius.dev/blog/block-assembly-marketplace-bam
- [statement] Solana has no global public mempool: clients forward transactions straight to upcoming leaders, and unprocessed transactions expire after about 150 blocks (~1 minute). The Solana docs say this shrinks the MEV surface compared with public-mempool chains. It does not remove it, because a sandwich only needs someone with early sight of the victim's order (a leader, an RPC or relay, or an app). (high confidence) — https://solana.com/docs/defi/mev-protection · keywords: Solana fees and execution costs: base fee priority fee Jito tips MEV rent · also: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28115, https://cnitarot.github.io/papers/imc26_solana.pdf, https://chorus.one/articles/analyzing-mev-instances-on-solana-part-1
- [statement] Jupiter's orderbook Beta markets (JUP, HYPE, ZEC, stock perps) drop the base, price impact and borrow fees. They charge taker or maker fees and an hourly funding rate applied on the hour (UTC). Liquidation returns leftover collateral instead of taking all of it. (medium confidence) (verified as of 2026-11-06) — https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/trade/perps/beta-markets · keywords: Solana fees and execution costs: base fee priority fee Jito tips MEV rent · also: https://docs.jup.ag/user-docs/trade/perps/fees
- [statement] On Velocity the taker pays a tiered fee on filled notional and the maker earns a rebate paid out of that fee. The fee is denominated in USDT and applied to the position's cost basis rather than billed separately. A round trip pays the taker fee twice. (medium confidence) — https://docs.velocity.exchange/trading/trading-fees · keywords: Solana fees and execution costs: base fee priority fee Jito tips MEV rent · also: https://docs.velocity.exchange/llms.txt
- [statement] Both Jupiter risk advisers frame cost as more than the headline rate. Gauntlet writes tradingFee = (baseFee + orderBookImpact) x tradeSize. Chaos Labs says a fair venue comparison must include opening fees, slippage and liquidation costs, not only borrowing-versus-funding rates, and that Hyperliquid's open interest may include short hedges that rely on stable low funding. (medium confidence) — https://discuss.jup.ag/t/jupiter-perpetuals-trading-fee-borrowing-rate-recommendations/16122 · keywords: Solana fees and execution costs: base fee priority fee Jito tips MEV rent · also: https://discuss.jup.ag/t/jupiter-borrowing-rate-analysis-recommendations-02-04-25/34842
