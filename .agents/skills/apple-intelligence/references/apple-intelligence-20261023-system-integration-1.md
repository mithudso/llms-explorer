# Apple Intelligence — system-integration-1

## Overview

Siri AI has separate data paths that are easy to conflate. Apple describes request processing through on-device models or Private Cloud Compute (PCC); PCC data is not stored after processing, according to Apple. Siri app conversation history syncs through iCloud, while the optional Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence setting permits Apple to store and review Siri, Dictation, and Translate interaction data, including app conversations, for product improvement and foundation-model training. Apple says opted-in records may be kept for up to two years, with reviewed examples retained longer. Separately, Apple says computer-generated transcripts may be retained and used for Siri and Dictation improvements by default. A user-submitted Report a Concern is another feedback path; Apple’s documents do not explain whether the improvement setting affects such reports.

App Schemas are system-defined contracts in App Intents that map an app’s entities, actions, and enums to categories Siri already understands. Developers should select schemas that match real app capabilities, implement complete schema groups when required, and check schema diagnostics after rebuilding with the OS 27 SDK. Apple’s recommended validation path covers intent behavior, Shortcuts shape, Spotlight entity discovery, and end-to-end Siri. Platform and account eligibility sit outside code adoption and need a separate check.

Apple separates action and parameter resolution from authorization to execute side effects. Siri can infer App Intent parameter values from the conversation or ask the user; that is intent resolution or clarification. App-defined confirmation, system risk checks, and device authentication gate execution. Shared or public entity ownership can inform the system’s confirmation decision. On-screen contextual cues are structured app entities attached to views, allowing references such as “the third one” to resolve to app content; they do not grant consent. Apps can shape dialog and visual results with App Intents, though Apple warns Siri AI may omit some custom result UI. Beta reports show that context-to-action handling can still fail.

This concept covers two distinct integration paths. App entities that conform to IndexedEntity and are donated to Core Spotlight become searchable, with meaning-based matching available in supported App Intents domains. IntentValueQuery and searchInApp provide alternatives for content the app does not pre-index or for forwarding Siri’s search into the app’s own UI. The app must update indexed entries when content changes and delete them when content is removed. Interaction donations describe actions a person already took through the app UI; they inform later system inference and do not themselves execute app code. Apple’s examples and forum guidance also bound expectations: indexing supports discovery and entity resolution, but arbitrary Q&A over every entity property is not guaranteed.

## Core Concepts

### Siri AI personalization and opt-in improvement data lifecycle

- Apple describes Siri AI as processing requests on-device or with Private Cloud Compute (PCC); its privacy page says on-device processing can use personal data without collecting it and that data processed in PCC is not stored. EFF says some on-screen requests may send context to PCC and the interface does not immediately show which route a request took. These statements concern request processing, not persistence of Siri app history or separate opted-in improvement data. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c1-1][^c1-2][^c1-3]
- Apple Support says the dedicated Siri app syncs conversation history across Apple devices with iCloud. Apple’s improvement disclosure includes conversations saved in the Siri app and says it collects follow-up requests and Siri responses when a user continues a conversation. Thus PCC’s request-processing statement does not establish that Siri app history or opted-in copies are ephemeral; Apple’s current Siri AI support page does not explicitly say that this history is end-to-end encrypted. verified-as-of: 2026-10-22[^c1-2][^c1-4][^c1-5]
- Apple’s support guide describes Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence as an optional setting for Siri AI, adjustable at Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements on iPhone and iPad. Apple says the preference is presented during setup and can be changed later; enabling it permits storage and review of sample audio and transcripts and allows use of the data to train foundation models. 9to5Mac reported that Siri AI setup presented “Share Audio and Text” and “Not Now.”. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c1-6][^c1-4][^c1-7]

Apple says it does not collect photos or attachments provided to Siri, notification content, or the information Siri searches on-device, and that it takes steps to remove personal details and some source content from responses. The disclosure therefore distinguishes the request and response record from the underlying personal material Siri used. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c1-4][^c1-6][^c1-7]
- Apple says opted-in audio, transcripts, and related request data use a random device-generated identifier that rotates multiple times per hour and is not tied to an Apple Account or email address. Apple also says it may connect multiple rotating identifiers when a user resumes a conversation. Opted-in data may be retained and used for up to two years; the small reviewed subset may be kept longer. - Apple’s general Siri and Dictation disclosures say audio is not stored by default, but transcripts and related request data may be retained for up to two years and computer-generated transcripts are used to improve Siri and Dictation by default. Apple separately says foundation-model training uses private personal data or interactions only when a user explicitly chooses to help improve the models. ### App Schemas integration for Siri AI

- Apple defines App Schemas as system-defined contracts for app intents, entities, and enums. Developers attach them with `AppIntent(schema:)`, `AppEntity(schema:)`, or `AppEnum(schema:)`; the selected schema tells the system how to interpret that type and its supported structure.[^c2-14][^c2-15][^c2-3]
- Choose schemas that match the app’s actual functionality. Apple says new Siri AI integration requires an app to adopt at least one App Schema, while custom AppIntents and AppShortcuts can coexist for actions outside the predefined schemas. Apple identifies the generic `system.search` schema as an option when an app’s search flow has no closer domain match.[^c2-4][^c2-1][^c2-2]
- Model app content as schema-backed AppEntity types and supported operations as schema-backed AppIntent types. Apple’s calendar sample maps calendar, attendee, and event entities plus create, update, delete, and open actions to the calendar and system domains, allowing Siri to resolve app-specific content and invoke matching actions without app-authored natural-language parsing.[^c2-3][^c2-5]
- Apple defines system schemas for AppIntent, AppEntity, and AppEnum types and maps a type to its selected schema with the AppIntent(schema:), AppEntity(schema:), or AppEnum(schema:) macro. Apple also recommends adopting optional schemas because they increase the number of actions people can invoke with Apple Intelligence and Siri.[^c2-14][^c2-3]
- An AppEntity describes existing app content for the system, while an entity schema tells Siri what category that content represents. An AppIntent schema provides a known action shape and parameters; the app maps those parameters to its own behavior and returns the result, such as the Messages sendMessage example returning the sent message as an entity.[^c2-1][^c2-3]
- Apple distinguishes general App Intent availability across surfaces such as Shortcuts, Spotlight, and Widgets from schema-shaped actions that Siri can execute through natural language; schema domains group related contracts such as Messages, Mail, and Photos.[^c2-2][^c2-1][^c2-3]

### Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues

- The App Intents parameter-resolution step occurs before perform(): the system may infer @Parameter values from the current conversation or ask the person to provide them. Apple documents side-effect confirmation as a separate gate after intent selection and before execution, so resolving an action or value is distinct from authorizing its effects.[^c3-1][^c3-5][^c3-6][^c3-9]
- An app can explicitly request confirmation before performing an App Intent, including with requestConfirmation() and conditional confirmation APIs. Apple also says Apple Intelligence or Siri may request confirmation for destructive or sensitive entity updates; this app-defined prompt is distinct from system-managed risk confirmation.[^c3-1][^c3-2][^c3-6]
- At WWDC26, Apple described Siri and App Intents confirmations as contextual and risk-based: the system evaluates an intent's schema side-effect metadata together with dynamic system state before execution, and actions with higher risk are more likely to prompt. If the person declines, the intent does not run. Apple's OwnershipProvidingEntity documentation separately says the system may request confirmation for destructive or sensitive actions and can include ownership context when an entity is shared or public. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c3-6][^c3-10][^c3-5]
- Device authentication is a separate execution control from confirmation: an App Intent can require the device to be unlocked, schemas carry default authentication policies based on sensitivity and data, and developers may override a schema policy only to make it stricter. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c3-1][^c3-6]
- OwnershipProvidingEntity supplies sharing and ownership context for an entity. Apple says Siri may skip confirmation for private entities by default and uses current ownership state to decide whether to confirm shared or public content; the protocol is documented for Apple OS 27 releases as of 2026-10-23. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c3-2][^c3-5]

### Apple Intelligence semantic indexing and content donations

- An app entity enters this semantic search path when it conforms to IndexedEntity and the app donates it to a CSSearchableIndex with indexAppEntities. Spotlight searches the donated content, and meaning-based matching depends on the App Intents domain; this is Spotlight indexing, not a guarantee that Siri can read every property on every entity.[^c4-1][^c4-3][^c4-5][^c4-8]
- Interaction donations represent an action a person already performed through the app UI. The app donates a schema-conforming AppIntent; Apple describes the donation as a hint stored in a temporary transcript for later system inference. Donation itself does not execute app code: the system can use the recorded intent to construct a similar intent later. Apple advises apps to donate genuine UI interactions accurately and warns that excessive donations may be ignored.[^c4-1][^c4-14][^c4-8]
- Apple's entity-indexing path requires an app to model discoverable records as AppEntity values conforming to IndexedEntity, then explicitly donate those instances with CSSearchableIndex.indexAppEntities(_:priority:) to a named CSSearchableIndex. Conformance alone does not populate the index. Once indexed, the records can appear in Spotlight and become discoverable to Apple Intelligence and Siri.[^c4-3][^c4-5][^c4-1][^c4-2]
- The searchable representation follows the entity metadata the app supplies. DisplayRepresentation contributes default display and index attributes, while @Property(indexingKey:) and customIndexingKey select properties to place under Spotlight keys. indexAppEntities is not recursive, so apps must donate child entities separately when they want them indexed. This lets an app shape which entity fields it exposes to system search rather than indexing every model field automatically.[^c4-13][^c4-11][^c4-1]

- Inferred from Apple’s separate notices: opting out of Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence does not by itself disable all Siri and Dictation processing or local personalization. Apple’s Siri and Dictation notice says audio is not stored unless a user opts in to the older Improve Siri & Dictation setting, while transcripts and related request data may still be stored and used to improve Siri. It also says interaction history is stored on device for personalization and that disabling Siri and Dictation deletes those local transcripts. - Tentative: Report a Concern is a separate, user-initiated feedback flow. Apple says it may use submitted reports to improve products and train models; a report can include user-provided descriptions, photos, videos, messages, or emails plus device and model metadata. Users can review and remove information or attachments before submitting. The cited support article does not give a retention period for these reports. verified-as-of: 2026-09-15[^c1-11] _(Siri AI personalization and opt-in improvement data lifecycle)_
- Some App Schema use-case groups need the full related set for a complete Siri flow. Apple says Xcode warns when an app adopts only part of a group, such as adopting `sendMessage` without the related `draftMessage` schema; treat these diagnostics as integration guidance and verify the full supported flow.[^c2-2][^c2-3] _(App Schemas integration for Siri AI)_
- Tentative: 9to5Mac reported on July 7, 2026 that Siri AI in iOS 27 beta 3 retrieved vehicle battery information from Tessie and a Ford app in its observed examples, while the official Tesla app did not work in those tests. This is a dated beta observation and does not establish which App Schemas each app adopted. verified-as-of: 2026-07-07[^c2-10] _(App Schemas integration for Siri AI)_
- On-screen context is provided through structured app entities attached to the views that display them, using APIs such as appEntityIdentifier, NSUserActivity, and view annotations. These cues can let Siri resolve references such as “the third one”; they add app data and location context beyond text visible in pixels.[^c3-3][^c3-5] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- Context annotations improve reference resolution but do not guarantee that Siri identifies the intended entity: Apple says Siri may ask for clarification or act on another item if it cannot understand the visible entities quickly enough. Lightweight display-representation queries can help Siri resolve them faster. (two sources)[^c3-5][^c3-3] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- Apps can shape spoken and visual results with IntentDialog, entity DisplayRepresentation, and static or interactive snippets. Apple’s snippets documentation warns that Siri AI might not display IntentDialog or ShowsSnippetView for an App Intent, so a custom result view is not guaranteed in every Siri AI interaction. verified-as-of: 2026-10-23[^c3-5][^c3-4][^c3-11] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- An app can add its own confirmation gate with AppIntent.requestConfirmation(). Apple's interactive-snippet guidance shows the intent pausing for the person to review or change ticket details; if they cancel, the intent stops before the follow-on action runs.[^c3-9][^c3-11] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- Apple says an app's onscreen data remains private to that app unless the developer supplies context. Developers can attach matching app entities to views, allowing Siri to ground references such as 'the third one' in structured content; Apple warns not to associate unrelated entities with a view. This app-supplied context is distinct from model inference over an unannotated screen.[^c3-12][^c3-5] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- Tentative: In an October 9, 2026 hands-on review using Siri AI on an iPhone 18 Pro with iOS 27, Nielsen Norman Group observed a drafted message with confirmation before sending. Its comparison also found old Siri selected the wrong contact and literal message content; the reviewer said the confirmation lets a user catch that type of resolution error. This is one reviewer's test, not evidence that every message action always prompts. verified-as-of: 2026-10-09[^c3-13] _(Siri app actions confirmation and contextual cues)_
- The app owns index freshness: donate new records, call indexAppEntities again when indexed properties or display data change, and delete entries when users remove content. Apple documents that indexing an entity with an identifier already in the index updates that entry. Spotlight can also request a rebuild; IndexedEntityQuery supplies that reindex path, while an app already using Core Spotlight reindex APIs does not need to add IndexedEntityQuery as well.[^c4-11][^c4-12][^c4-1][^c4-2][^c4-13] _(Apple Intelligence semantic indexing and content donations)_
- Apple presents three distinct retrieval paths: ahead-of-time IndexedEntity indexing; IntentValueQuery for large, server-side, or frequently changing catalogs, where the app receives structured search input; and .system.searchInApp to route Siri's query into the app's own search UI, even when entities are not indexed. These paths extend content discovery and do not themselves execute a state-changing app action, which requires a separately defined AppIntent. A third-party implementation guide describes the same paths and their different roles.[^c4-1][^c4-8][^c4-9][^c4-7][^c4-2] _(Apple Intelligence semantic indexing and content donations)_
- Apple's examples scope semantic use by domain: the App Intents session describes meaning-based playlist matching, and the Calendar schema demo shows Siri searching event titles and note content. Apple staff clarify that IndexedEntity alone is not an unrestricted property-question interface; arbitrary questions require an explicitly defined answer intent. A developer in the same forum thread reported that their Spotlight-visible campus entities were not used by Siri AI as expected, so treat runtime behavior outside the demonstrated domains as uncertain. (two sources)[^c4-1][^c4-2][^c4-7] _(Apple Intelligence semantic indexing and content donations)_

## Anti-Patterns

- Apple describes semantic matching as dependent on the App Intents domain. Its June 2026 Frameworks forum reply further says that indexed data is searchable and an IndexedEntity parameter gives Siri candidates for entity resolution, but arbitrary answers about a custom entity's properties need an explicit intent. Taken together, indexing should not be treated as a general guarantee that Siri can answer free-form questions about every indexed field; the examples are bounded by supported domain and query behavior.
