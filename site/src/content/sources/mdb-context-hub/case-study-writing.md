# Case Study Writing

## Overview

A case study is a published narrative of one customer's outcome with a vendor's product or approach. It is the highest-conversion content asset in B2B marketing because it does the one thing prospects most need: prove that a buyer like them succeeded with this solution.

**The defining traits of a case study:**
- Specific, named (or carefully anonymized) customer
- Concrete before/after with quantified outcomes
- At least one direct quote from a named customer stakeholder
- A clear narrative arc: Challenge → Solution → Result
- Customer approval in writing before publication
- A non-marketing tone — the customer is the protagonist, not the vendor

## Core Concepts

### 1. Challenge / Solution / Result — the BLUF for customer stories

| Section | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Title + hero metric | Sell the click in five words; promise the outcome | 1 line + 1 stat |
| Challenge | What was broken before, why it mattered | 25% of body |
| Solution | What the customer adopted, how the implementation went | 35% of body |
| Result | Quantified outcomes; customer quote; what changed | 35% of body |
| About + What's next | Context block + forward-looking statement | 5% of body |

**Front-load the result.** Place the hero metric in the title or subtitle, and again in the first paragraph.

### 2. Anonymization decisions

**Name the customer when:** Written marketing approval is signed; customer is referenceable; naming helps, not hurts.

**Anonymize when:** Regulated industry; sensitive admission; customer in quiet period.

**Anonymization patterns:**
- "A Fortune 500 financial-services firm headquartered in the U.S. Northeast" — High trust
- "A large healthcare provider" — Low trust

### 3. The "X% improvement" claim hygiene

- **Specific baseline**: "down from 4.2 hours to 12 minutes" beats "much faster"
- **Specific time window**: "in the first 90 days" beats "over time"
- **Specific scope**: "across 1,200 trades per day"
- **Verifiable source**: the customer signed off on this number

### 4. Quote integration

- Attributed to a named individual with a title
- Sounds like a human said it (not vendor-speak)
- Specific, not generic ("we cut our deploy time in half" beats "Acme has been a great partner")

**Bad quote:** "Acme is a true partner who has helped us leverage our data to drive transformational outcomes."

**Good quote:** "We used to spend two full days every month reconciling reports. Now it's a 20-minute job for one person." — Sarah Chen, Director of Engineering

### 5. Multi-stakeholder voice

| Stakeholder | Quote topic |
|---|---|
| Champion | "Here's why we chose this and what changed" |
| End-user | "Here's what my work feels like now" |
| Finance | "Here's the financial picture" |
| Engineering/IT lead | "Here's how it fits with our other systems" |
| Executive sponsor | "Here's why this mattered to the company" |

### 6. Long-form / mid-form / one-pager derivative pipeline

| Format | Length | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form case study | 1,200–2,500 words | Mid-funnel (active evaluation) |
| Mid-form case study | 600–1,000 words | Mid-to-top funnel |
| One-pager / case study summary | 200–400 words | Sales enablement |

### 7. The written approval workflow

1. Customer success identifies a candidate
2. Verbal pre-approval from a customer executive
3. Discovery interview
4. Draft 1 to the customer
5. Customer Legal and PR review
6. Final draft + approval signature
7. Publication
8. Renewal (annually if case study remains live)

### 8. Tone calibration: the customer is the hero

**Vendor-as-hero (anti-pattern):**
> "Acme's revolutionary platform transformed BigCo's operations, delivering 73% productivity gains."

**Customer-as-hero (correct):**
> "BigCo's engineering team cut deploy time from 4 hours to 22 minutes by replacing their legacy CI system."

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Vendor-as-hero voice | Customer is the protagonist; vendor is the tool |
| Vague hero metric ("transformed operations") | Specific number with baseline, time window, and scope |
| Quotes that sound like the PR team wrote them | Vet quotes: would a human actually say this? |
| Only one stakeholder quoted | Quote 2–3 stakeholders: champion, end-user, finance/exec |
| No written approval | Sign a written approval covering name, quotes, metrics, channels |
