## Definitions

- [statement] Protein redistribution means limiting protein at breakfast and lunch while shifting intake toward dinner. It may improve levodopa response in selected patients with motor fluctuations, but definitions, patient selection and treatment algorithms lack consensus and high-level evidence is limited. (low confidence) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-023-00541-w

## Structure and components

- [statement] In the 2018 pilot, ketogenic diet produced a larger improvement in MDS-UPDRS Part 1 nonmotor experiences than low-fat diet; there were no significant between-group differences in Parts 2 to 4. Within-group motor improvements do not establish ketogenic superiority. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/
- [statement] In an 8-week pilot randomized trial, 47 people were randomized to low-fat or ketogenic diets and 38 completed. Ketogenic participants improved more on MDS-UPDRS Part 1 nonmotor experiences, but there were no between-group differences in Parts 2–4; intermittent worsening of tremor or rigidity was the most common ketogenic adverse effect. These results do not establish motor superiority or long-term safety. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/

## Measurements and reference values

- [statement] Protein redistribution requires nutritional and medication monitoring: the 2023 practical review advises avoiding low-protein diets below 0.8 g/kg/day to prevent lean-mass loss, monitoring weight and dyskinesias, and considering levodopa dose adjustment when bioavailability improves. Its minimum is not a universal optimal protein prescription. (low confidence) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-023-00541-w

## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] The Fox study cannot establish causation or longitudinal slowing of PD: progression was estimated from current symptoms and diagnosis date, and the authors explicitly acknowledged the cross-sectional limitation. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/
- [statement] The Parkinson’s Foundation says targeted nutrition may slow PD advancement, whereas the randomized-trial review describes efficacy evidence as inconclusive. Attribute the optimistic guidance and the evidentiary limitation explicitly; neither establishes disease modification. - [statement] Mediterranean and MIND patterns emphasize plant foods, olive oil, fish and nuts and discourage red meat and sweets. MIND scoring additionally emphasizes leafy greens, berries and whole grains and penalizes butter, cheese and fried foods; different scoring scales cannot be compared as equivalent dose units. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/
- [statement] The MIND/Mediterranean symptom analysis cannot establish causality: its authors describe a cross-sectional method, lack of temporal analysis, a largely white and wealthy US sample, possible co-occurring protective behaviors and unvalidated brief questionnaire scales. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/

## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] In an observational analysis of 1205 participants, higher MIND and Mediterranean scores were associated with fewer patient-reported PD symptoms after adjustment for age, gender, income and years since diagnosis. This was an association with symptom scores, rather than a randomized demonstration of slowed progression. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/
- [statement] The MED-PARK publication is a randomized trial protocol specifying 44 participants and six months of modified Mediterranean diet versus usual diet. Its planned outcomes and enrollment timeline must not be presented as efficacy results. (low confidence) — https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/15/10/e101946
- [statement] The 2018 low-fat versus ketogenic pilot randomized 47 people with PD; 44 started and 38 completed the study. The intervention lasted eight weeks. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/
- [statement] A 2026 critical review concluded that no nutritional intervention had independently replicated, durable cognitive efficacy in established PD. It treats a Mediterranean-style pattern as a reasonable nutritional default rather than an established cognitive treatment. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13708064/

## Facts and statements

- [statement] A varied whole-food diet that supplies adequate energy and protein is practical supportive care in PD; a Mediterranean-style pattern is a reasonable default, while meals should be individualized with a dietitian to nutritional needs and medication timing. - [statement] Dietary protein can reduce or delay levodopa effectiveness in some people with PD. - [statement] A single-center Mediterranean-diet trial randomized eighty people with idiopathic PD to diet or control for 10 weeks; 35 per group completed it.
