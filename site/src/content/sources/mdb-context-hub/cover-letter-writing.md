# Cover Letter Writing

## Overview

The cover letter is a narrative artifact — a focused, one-page argument for why this human should be in this role at this company. Customization per application is non-negotiable.

## Core Concepts

### 1. The three-part skeleton

1. **Why this company.** Show evidence you've done your homework: a product they shipped, a value they've published.
2. **Why this role.** Connect the specific responsibilities to specific skills or experiences you have. Reuse 2-3 keywords from the JD.
3. **Why me.** Demonstrate through evidence and stories. Don't list skills; show outcomes.

### 2. Opener discipline

**Weak openers:**
- "I am writing to apply for the X position at Y."
- "My name is X and I am a [degree/title]."

**Stronger openers:**
- "I noticed your team published the design for the X system last month — the tradeoffs you made on Y are the same ones I worked through at Z."

### 3. Show evidence, don't list skills

**Weak:** "I have strong project management skills and am highly organized."

**Stronger:** "Last year I shipped the X migration on a six-engineer team. The original estimate was four months; we delivered in five because I caught a dependency that wasn't in the original scope."

### 4. Length conventions

- **One page maximum**, no exceptions.
- **250-350 words** is the standard target.
- **3-4 short paragraphs**, block-formatted.

### 5. Customization per application

At a minimum, customize:
- Company name and the "why this company" paragraph
- 2-3 keywords from the JD
- The job title in the opener

### 6. Signature and sign-off

- **Salutation:** "Dear [Name]," when you know the hiring manager's name.
- **Sign-off:** "Sincerely," is the universal safe choice.
- **Capitalization:** Only the first word of the closing phrase is capitalized.

### 7. The "I" parade

Count how many sentences open with "I." If more than half do, the letter reads self-absorbed. Vary openings: lead with the company, the role, the project, the result.

## Template: three-paragraph cover letter

```
Dear [Name],

[Opener — specific reference to the company or role. End with a one-line
thesis: who you are, the role you're applying for, and why you fit.]

[Body paragraph 1 — The strongest evidence story: a specific project,
the role you played, the measurable outcome.]

[Body paragraph 2 — Why this company specifically.]

[Closing — restate the fit. Invite a conversation.]

Sincerely,
[Your name]
```

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The cliché opener.** "I am writing to apply..."
2. **The reused letter.** Generic flattery, no company name in the first paragraph.
3. **The skill list.** "Strong communicator, fast learner, team player."
4. **The resume rehash.** Repeating the resume's bullets in paragraph form.
5. **The two-page letter.** Always cut to one page.
6. **The typo in the company name.** Auto-disqualifying.
