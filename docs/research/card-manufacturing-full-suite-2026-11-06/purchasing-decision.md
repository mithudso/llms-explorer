# Identical game decks: outsource first, qualify ownership later

Version: 1.0.0 — reviewed decision report, 2026-11-06. Delta: promote the source-checked decision, preserve coating-operation findings and correct the MPC specification citation. Final suite status and artifact receipts are tracked in the [companion run report](README.md).

## Recommendation

For this game, start with professionally manufactured samples and an outsourced first run. Consider owning production equipment after actual repeat sales, configured supplier quotes, and a timed production demonstration show that ownership pays for the **complete, acceptable, boxed game**. This is a recommendation from the documented production requirements and cost framework, not a measured financial result for this business. Manufacturers describe separate printing, surface finishing, cutting, collation and packaging operations; relevant-cost accounting requires comparing the costs that actually change between buying and making. [Production workflow](https://printninja.com/card-game-production/), [make-or-buy accounting](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/10-3-evaluate-and-determine-whether-to-make-or-buy-a-component).

The planning case is **138 base cards in two stacks: 114 response cards and 24 theme cards**, before instructions or later contributions. The counts came from a read-only inspection of the game source. Card dimensions, paper, finish and final packaging are still design choices. No supplier has quoted this configured game, and no hardware was tested firsthand during this research.

The first procurement deliverable is one common specification and comparable sample/quote request. The [prepared quote-and-demo brief](quote-and-demo-brief.md) covers that specification; it has not been sent. Select the card size, stock, finish and box, obtain samples and configured outsourced quotes, and then test an owned workflow against the same output. This sequence is my recommendation, not a supplier commitment.

## Are automated card printers any good?

Professional digital presses have useful automation. Canon advertises automatic duplex on the imagePRESS V900 Series up to 350 gsm. Ricoh advertises heavy-media handling and front-to-back registration on its Pro C7500. Those capabilities justify a production demonstration; they do not establish the feel, opacity, registration, durability or complete-game output of the selected stock. Do not combine each model's maximum weight and maximum sheet size into an unrestricted specification. [Canon V900](https://sg.canon/en/business/imagepress-v900-series/main/product), [Ricoh Pro C7500](https://www.ricoh-usa.com/en/production-printing/products/pd/sheet-fed/pro-c7500-color-sheet-fed-printer), [Canon paper limitations](https://oip.manual.canon/USRMA-7565-zz-PS-900-enUS/contents/devu-beginning-dev_spec-papers.html).

A photo printer is a different proposition. Epson limits ET-8550 automatic double-sided printing to specified plain-paper combinations; glossy, thick and certain matte papers are excluded. Its manual two-sided workflow requires dry ink before reloading. I would treat such equipment as a prototyping option requiring hands-on finishing, rather than budget it as an unattended commercial deck line. [Epson duplex restrictions](https://epson.com/faq/SPT_C11CJ21201~faq-00004fb-et8500_8550?faq_cat=faq-8796127602764), [Epson handling instructions](https://download4.epson.biz/sec_pubs/et-8550_series/useg/en/GUID-85133602-1F39-4AF0-9CAB-A0DC7C3C11A1.htm).

Image quality is only part of card quality. LongPack documents that its digital preproduction cards can differ from production stock and color and omit the Poker varnish used in mass production. Approve an appropriate production-equivalent finish sample as well as readable artwork. That supplier's sample limitation is not a general verdict against digital printing. [LongPack card samples](https://help.longpackgames.com/article/105-08-cards).

## What does automation actually cover?

| Stage | Production mechanism or automation | Work to demonstrate on this game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepress and printing | Imposition, stored media settings, automatic duplex and registration controls | Correct front/back orientation; readable text; selected stock feeding consistently; preparation and calibration |
| Coating and surface treatment | A separate finishing operation where required | Ink/stock compatibility, curing and finished handling; do not assume an office-print setting supplies playing-card feel |
| Cutting and corners | Sheet slitters/cutters or dedicated die cutting | Full bleed, cut position, corner shape and clean edges on the actual imposed sheet |
| Deck assembly | Correct imposition can assist collation | Every intended card, no duplicates or omissions, response/theme separation, recovery after an interruption |
| Packaging and inspection | Packing and inspection equipment may cover individual steps | Complete games that meet the agreed specification and fit the chosen box |

This table is a proposed application test, based on manufacturers' process descriptions; it is not a claim that one reviewed machine performs all stages. PrintNinja describes alternative cutting and corner-rounding routes and uses assembly-order imposition to assist collation. [Manufacturing process](https://printninja.com/card-game-production/), [Cartamundi services](https://cartamundi.com/en/cardgame-boardgame-solutions/).

Duplo's DC-618 can recall jobs from barcodes and correct sheet registration. Its headline rate is up to 23 sheets per minute. It is a slitter/cutter/creaser, and that sheet rate is not a measured rate of coated, collated, inspected and boxed games. [DC-618](https://www.duplousa.com/product/dc-618-slitter-cutter-creaser/).

## How much effort will operation take?

**Plan for an operator-dependent production process, not a button that produces boxed games.** That is my operating-burden conclusion from the documented tasks below; the research does not establish a training duration or staffing ratio.

- **Startup:** qualify the stock and artwork, configure printing and finishing, learn registration and acceptance checks, and establish a complete-deck packing method.
- **Each batch:** prepare and load media, calibrate as required, check two-sided registration, move material between machines, set up finishing, inspect output, recover card sequence after interruptions and pack complete games.
- **Maintenance:** replenish consumables, clean the relevant mechanisms, perform the documented operator adjustments and arrange service for faults outside those procedures.

These are tasks to measure, not an estimate of hours per batch. Canon warns that stock characteristics can affect feed reliability and output. Xerox documents customer-replaceable supplies, maintenance sheets and adjustments. [Canon media guidance](https://oip.manual.canon/USRMA-7565-zz-PS-900-enUS/contents/devu-beginning-dev_spec-papers.html), [Xerox Versant 4100 user guide](https://download.support.xerox.com/pub/docs/VERSANT_4100/userdocs/any-os/en_GB/Xerox_Versant_4100_press_ug_en-US.pdf), [production sequence](https://printninja.com/card-game-production/).

If the chosen construction needs an in-house coating step, include its setup and upkeep too. ACTEGA treats drying controls as application-specific and identifies pile pressure and excessive coating as blocking concerns. Graphic Whizard documents lamp/reflector and roller cleaning for its Micro coater. These are process-specific tasks; they do not establish a universal coating recipe or minutes per game. [ACTEGA blocking guidance](https://www.actega.com/emea/en/services/troubleshooting/blocking-printed-sheets.html), [Graphic Whizard coater troubleshooting](https://www.graphicwhizard.com/faq/).

Service response is a separate question from production recovery. Ricoh's advertised service response depends on contract terms; it does not establish the repair duration or this game's output during a failure. Check what the actual agreement includes before counting toner or repair spending again. [Ricoh maintenance coverage](https://www.ricoh-usa.com/en/support-and-download/maintenance-services).

The research does **not** establish overall operator minutes per game, accepted games per shift, reject yield, equipment life or a universal ownership break-even volume. Record attended operator minutes separately from machine-running and drying time. Divide total batch effort and expenditure by accepted complete games, including inspection and rework. This is an application of batch costing and a complete-workflow observation method. [MU Extension batch costing](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g648), [workflow observation case study](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/5036/).

## A useful cost comparison

Compare the same card count, stock, finish, packaging, delivery destination and time period. Use USD quotes for the US planning case. The following is a planning method, not a forecast:

`Outsource cost = configured order + proofs/setup + applicable freight/tax/duties + receiving/rework costs`

`Own cost = incremental equipment/installation/training/space/service + batch materials/clicks/consumables + labor + utilities + finishing/packaging + rework`

Divide both by the number of acceptable complete games. Count a service-included consumable once. Keep customer delivery comparable on both sides. Treat cash outlay, ongoing operating costs and inventory exposure separately. These boundaries adapt relevant-cost and batch-cost methods. [Make-or-buy method](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/10-3-evaluate-and-determine-whether-to-make-or-buy-a-component), [batch costing](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g648).

For a simplified crossover, choose one time horizon first—for example, the first twelve months as a planning assumption. Define `Fown` and `Fout` as each alternative's relevant fixed costs over that same horizon, and `vown` and `vout` as its variable cost per accepted complete game. Keep fixed proof/setup/service charges in the fixed terms rather than also allocating them into the variable terms. Compare `Fown + N × vown` with `Fout + N × vout`. If ownership has higher fixed cost but lower variable cost, the crossover is `N = (Fown − Fout) / (vout − vown)`. If variable savings are zero or negative, that higher fixed investment has no finite payback in this model. Other sign combinations require comparing the totals directly. This is algebra for a stated model, not an observed crossover or supplier offer. [Relevant-cost method](https://openstax.org/books/principles-managerial-accounting/pages/10-3-evaluate-and-determine-whether-to-make-or-buy-a-component).

Use the same cash-cost basis on both sides. Record any documented residual value or financing treatment explicitly; do not silently mix a whole purchase payment with an annual depreciation expense. Recalculate when order tiers, batch frequency or equipment capacity change instead of extending one unit cost across every quantity. No fixed costs or unit costs for this game have been inserted into the model. [Volume and fixed-cost changes](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g648).

Use these **comparison quantities**, chosen for planning rather than demand forecasts:

| Scenario | Compare | Decision it helps resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One physical sample per proposed stock/finish | Configured POD samples and a local specialist sample | Is the production-equivalent card/box specification acceptable? This is an initial comparison, not a sufficient reliability sample by itself. |
| 100 identical games | Configured small-batch/POD quote and a local finished-game quote | What is the total cash commitment while content and demand are still being tested? |
| 500 and 1,500 identical games | Eligible bulk suppliers versus the same POD specification, with landed costs | Does lower quoted unit cost justify inventory and cash exposure? Supplier eligibility still depends on the actual order. |
| Repeat annual production at actual sales volume | Outsourced reorders versus a demonstrated owned workflow | Do measured savings repay the incremental investment, including labor and downtime? |

These quantities do not predict what this business will sell or which supplier will win. A lower manufacturing price can leave more money tied up in unsold stock. [Publisher inventory and cash example](https://stonemaiergames.com/the-math-of-tariffs/).

## Supplier shortlist and the checks that matter

- **The Game Crafter:** useful to evaluate configured small-copy games and direct fulfillment. Specify the two stacks carefully: its guide says same-size cards within a game are not sorted by deck and directs separately packaged decks to its embedded-games option. Whole-sheet billing also affects cost. [Card setup and billing](https://help.thegamecrafter.com/article/399-how-to-make-a-card-game-tarot-deck).
- **MakePlayingCards:** evaluate fully custom paper-card faces, stock/finish choices and fulfillment against the exact game. Verify the selected stock's quantity restrictions rather than assuming every option has the same minimum. [Print routes](https://www.makeplayingcards.com/pops/card-print-types.html), [fulfillment terms](https://www.makeplayingcards.com/mpc-fulfillment-services.aspx), [custom game-card specifications and stock minimums](https://www.makeplayingcards.com/design/custom-blank-card.html).
- **PrintNinja / Panda:** evaluate a repeatable bulk run once the specification and demand support the actual minimums. Retain production-sample approval and freight scope in the quote. [PrintNinja](https://printninja.com/printing-products/card-game-printing/), [Panda](https://pandagm.com/our-process/).
- **A local card-capable commercial printer:** request a finished, rounded, complete two-stack game sample and total cost. A quote for printed sheets alone is not the same deliverable. This is a proposed procurement requirement from the documented full production workflow. [Production stages](https://printninja.com/card-game-production/).

Printify merchandise remains a separate product decision. Approval of Printify for merchandise does not establish that a selected standard custom-back playing-card product supports all unique game faces. Confirm the exact product before treating it as a manufacturing route.

## The purchase test

Before buying equipment, ask the dealer to run the actual artwork and chosen stock through the proposed complete workflow. Agree the card-quality and completeness checks first. Record setup, attended labor, elapsed time, acceptable packed games, rejects, cleaning and jam recovery. Include one repeat run so a good first sample is not the only observation. This is a proposed acceptance protocol, not a standard's prescribed sample size. Use the same output specification for outsourced samples and quotes. [Preproduction/production approval stages](https://pandagm.com/our-process/), [complete-workflow observation](https://repository.rit.edu/theses/5036/).

My buying rule is therefore: **buy only after the demonstrated workflow meets the specification and the measured total cost beats outsourcing at realistic repeat demand**. Until those conditions are established, outsourcing is the more defensible commitment for this project.
