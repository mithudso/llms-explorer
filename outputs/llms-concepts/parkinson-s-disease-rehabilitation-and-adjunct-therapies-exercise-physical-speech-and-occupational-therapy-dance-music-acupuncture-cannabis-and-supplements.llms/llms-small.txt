## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] Two systematic reviews found no significant advantage of acupuncture over placebo acupuncture on the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS): the Lee et al. review in Movement Disorders (eleven RCTs) pooled three placebo-controlled RCTs (n = 96, WMD, 5.7; 95% CI -2.8 to 14.2, P = 0.19) and judged the evidence 'not convincing', and the Noh et al. meta-analysis (42 studies involving 2625 participants) reported that its results failed to show acupuncture significantly more effective than placebo acupuncture in total UPDRS. ## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] Meta-analyses of acupuncture combined with conventional treatment report better UPDRS results than the comparison arm while describing the evidence as limited: one meta-analysis (42 studies involving 2625 participants) found that combined acupuncture and conventional medication showed significant improvements in total UPDRS, UPDRS I, UPDRS II and UPDRS III compared with conventional medication alone and rated the methodological quality of the RCTs low, and Yu et al. (50 RCTs, n = 3,248; acupuncture modalities combined with standard PD therapy) report acupuncture significantly outperforming Western medicine across all UPDRS domains while stating that the overall certainty of evidence ranged from moderate to low across most outcomes. ## Facts and statements

- [statement] Sham-controlled RCTs of acupuncture for PD-related fatigue found no significant difference between real and sham acupuncture: Kluger et al. (ninety-four PD patients, 6 weeks of biweekly treatment) reported significant improvements in both groups at 6 and 12 weeks without significant between-group differences, and Kong et al. (forty PD patients, 5 weeks) reported the same pattern; the Cui et al. review found fatigue showed no difference from sham (SMD 0.10) and graded certainty for fatigue very low. (high confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27028133/ · keywords: Parkinson's disease rehabilitation and adjunct therapies: exercise, physical, speech and occupational therapy, dance, music, acupuncture, cannabis and supplements · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5853635/, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13189933/
- [statement] Authors differ on what sham-controlled acupuncture results imply: Kluger et al. conclude that PD-related fatigue joins the conditions that acupuncture helps primarily through nonspecific or placebo effects, and Kong et al. judge real and sham acupuncture equally effective for PD-related fatigue and likely due to nonspecific or placebo effects, whereas Kwon et al. report that sham acupuncture combined with conventional medicine was superior to conventional medicine alone in their network meta-analysis and suggest that a sham acupuncture-controlled design might underestimate the effect of acupuncture treatment. (medium confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27028133/ · keywords: Parkinson's disease rehabilitation and adjunct therapies: exercise, physical, speech and occupational therapy, dance, music, acupuncture, cannabis and supplements · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5853635/, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8619200/
