# UX Audit — Home Page & Primary Nav (2026-09-07)

Scope: `site/src/pages/index.astro`, `site/src/layouts/Base.astro`, `site/src/styles/global.css`. Lens: psychology + marketing, per `docs/site/00-platform-design.md` §1–2 and the applied-psychology / CTA / VOC skills.

## 1. Who lands here

**A — Agent/tool builder deciding whether to publish `llms.txt`.** Asks "what is this standard, should I adopt it?" The eyebrow and lede assume the reader already knows what an `llms.txt` file is; nothing above the fold defines it plainly. **Not answered** in 10 seconds.

**B — Docs owner wanting a grade.** Asks "how does my `llms-full.txt` score?" The Directory card (`index.astro:126-140`) shows graded sites but frames itself as a showcase ("sites doing it well"), not a self-check. **Partially answered** — the capability is one click away, but the entry point doesn't invite this visitor's action.

**C — Researcher exploring the concept tree.** Asks "what's in this graph, can I query it?" The Tree card renders a live mindmap and concept count. **Answered directly** — the one persona the page already serves well.

## 2. 10-second test

- Eyebrow (exact): *"The llms.txt standard, worked"*
- Lede (exact): *"The concept tree is the map; llms files are the territory; the site keeps the two in agreement."*
- H1 (rendered by `Base.astro:71` from the `title` prop set in `index.astro:8`): *"LLMS-Explorer"*
- First card row: seven visually identical cards — Tree, Directory, Demo, Reference, Examples, Blog, llms.txt (`index.astro:109-198`).

**Can determine:** the site is about `llms.txt` files and a "concept tree"; it feels technical and meta, not a product for one specific job.
**Cannot determine:** what `llms.txt` *is* if unfamiliar; who it's for; what to click first; whether this is a tool to run against *your* site or a reference/showcase; "worked" in the eyebrow reads ambiguously (past-tense success vs. "worked example").

## 3. Findings

**F1 · No self-identification path · impact H · principle: message-to-market match / Five Stages of Awareness (`conversion-copywriting-and-voice-of-customer/SKILL.md` Part 3)** · evidence: lede/eyebrow have no "for docs owners" / "for builders" branch; nav (`Base.astro:9-15`) has no persona entry point · fix: add a one-line "who this is for" branch under the lede, matched to the three personas above.

**F2 · Value prop needs prior knowledge, fails plain-language test · impact H · principle: Yale message clarity (`persuasion-and-influence-psychology/SKILL.md` §6); message hierarchy (`conversion-copywriting-and-voice-of-customer/SKILL.md` Part 2)** · evidence: lede is a metaphor ("map"/"territory") with no literal definition of `llms.txt` above the fold · fix: add one plain sentence — "llms.txt tells AI agents what your site contains" — beside the metaphor.

**F3 · Seven equal-weight cards create choice overload · impact H · principle: Hick's Law; CTA-count effect (`cta-design-technical-audiences/SKILL.md`: 1 CTA → 3.8% CTR vs. 3+ CTAs → 2.4–2.8%)** · evidence: `.card-grid` renders Tree, Directory, Demo, Reference, Examples, Blog, llms.txt as uniform `.card` elements (`index.astro:109-198`, `global.css:266-311`) · fix: give one card (Tree, or a new "check your file" action) visual priority; demote the rest.

**F4 · No primary CTA anywhere on the page · impact H · principle: CTA salience (`cta-design-technical-audiences/SKILL.md`: "CTAs without context get ignored"); salient-default commitment (`behavioral-decision-making/SKILL.md` §6)** · evidence: `global.css:238-256` defines `.btn`/`.btn.secondary`, but `index.astro` uses zero `<a class="btn">` — every action, including "Sign in" and "Downloads," is a plain text link (`index.astro:200-204`) · fix: promote one action to the existing `.btn` style above the fold.

**F5 · Social proof present but visually suppressed · impact M · principle: social proof (`persuasion-and-influence-psychology/SKILL.md` §5); credibility signals (frontend-ui `emotional-design-and-visual-trust`)** · evidence: `{gradeA} of {directory.count} scored files earn an A` and `{concepts} concepts · {roots} roots` render in `<footer>`, styled `color: var(--ink-faint)` at `var(--step--1)` — the faintest, smallest text the system defines (`global.css:279`, `311`) · fix: promote these counts into card body or a stat strip near the top.

**F6 · Nav labels mismatch content and product priority · impact M · principle: Jakob's Law / label clarity; discoverability (research §6, NN/g "increase feature discoverability")** · evidence: `NAV` (`Base.astro:9-15`) is Reference/Examples/Download/Blog/Donate; "Skills" — central per `00-platform-design.md` §1 — lives only in a `<details>` dropdown (`Base.astro:56-62`); "Download" (singular) covers skills-via-npx, the `llmsx` CLI, an npm package, and a terminal browser (`index.astro:202`) · fix: surface "Skills" as a first-class nav item; rename or split "Download."

**F7 · No visible provenance for "researched" claims · impact M · principle: trust & calibration, research §4 (citations adjacent to the claim)** · evidence: the Tree card asserts "Every researched concept," the Demo card a "recorded" run — neither shows an adjacent proof point on the card itself · fix: attach one adjacent, specific proof (date, method link) to each trust claim.

**F8 · Learn → explore → apply isn't sequenced · impact M · principle: awareness-stage sequencing (`conversion-copywriting-and-voice-of-customer/SKILL.md` Part 3); progressive disclosure (research §6, NN/g "educate & inspire")** · evidence: card order mixes learn (Reference), explore (Tree/Directory/Demo), and apply (Skills/Downloads, pushed below the grid as `.signin` paragraphs, `index.astro:200-204`) with no grouping · fix: group into three zones matching the stated funnel.

## 4. What to keep

- Live, honest data in every card (real tree/grade/timing counts, not placeholder copy) — the site's credibility asset; don't replace with marketing prose.
- The "N of M earn an A" framing (`index.astro:139`) states the failure rate too, reading as more credible than a bare success number (Stanford/Fogg credibility: honesty signals trust).
- Every page's `.md` twin and the `/family/` reader for the raw `llms.txt` — the "dog food" principle in `00-platform-design.md` §2.6, worth surfacing, not just structurally present.
- The no-JS `<details>` Skills menu (`Base.astro:56-62`, `global.css:164-165`) — accessible by default; keep this mechanism even while promoting Skills to the main nav row.
- No dark patterns or artificial urgency — matches the documented technical-audience distrust of manufactured urgency (`cta-design-technical-audiences/SKILL.md`); don't fix F3/F4 with countdowns or fake scarcity.

## 5. Constraints observed

- The H1 is rendered by `Base.astro:71` (`<h1>{title}</h1>`) from the `title` prop; changing H1 copy means editing the `title` const in `index.astro:8`, not `Base.astro`.
- The page `description` (`index.astro:9`) is reused verbatim as the lede and must stay one sentence, short enough for the site's own generated `llms.txt` (project convention: MAX_DESC_CHARS=180) — a new lede must keep this dual role or split description from displayed copy explicitly.
- `Base.astro` derives the `.md` twin link from `route` unless a page passes `twin={null}` (used by `index.astro:105`); any homepage restructuring must preserve this pattern.
- Visual tokens (`--accent`, `--paper`, spacing scale, and the already-defined but unused `.btn`/`.btn.secondary`) live in one shared `global.css` imported once by `Base.astro` — new hierarchy elements should reuse these, not introduce new colors.
- The mindmap card falls back through `tree.roots`, then `Object.values(tree.nodes)[0]` (`index.astro:19-27`) so the test suite's fixture trees (lacking the named root) never throw — any Tree-card redesign must keep a null-safe fallback.
- `reference`/`examples`/`blog` picks come from Astro content-collection sorts (`pick`, `newest`, `index.astro:91-103`), not hardcoded copy — changing which example shows requires `order`/`date` front-matter, not prose edits in `index.astro`.
- Google Analytics and AdSense scripts load unconditionally in `<head>` (`Base.astro:33-42`); an above-the-fold redesign should assume future ad-slot competition for vertical space.
