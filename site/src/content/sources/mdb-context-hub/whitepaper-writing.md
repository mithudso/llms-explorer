# Whitepaper Writing

## Overview

A whitepaper is a long-form, authoritative B2B document that educates a business reader on a problem and positions a solution.

- **Lighter than academic**: persuasion is allowed; citation density is moderate; the author has a point of view.
- **Heavier than a blog post**: data and methodology are visible; claims are sourced.
- **Length**: 8–20 pages typical; 8–12 is the sweet spot for engagement.
- **Voice**: third-person company-as-narrator.

Gordon Graham identifies three structural types:
1. **Backgrounder**: explains a technology, methodology, or product in depth.
2. **Numbered-list**: "5 Reasons to Adopt X," "10 Pitfalls of Y."
3. **Problem/solution**: describes an industry-wide problem, evaluates current solutions, presents a new approach.

## Core Concepts

### 1. The problem → solution → proof → CTA arc

| Section | Job | Pages |
|---|---|---|
| Title page + abstract/exec summary | Sell the download | 1–2 |
| Industry context / problem definition | Establish the stakes | 2–3 |
| Why current solutions fall short | Honest review of competing approaches | 2–3 |
| The proposed solution | Vendor-neutral description of the method | 2–4 |
| Proof: case study, data, methodology | Evidence layer | 2–4 |
| Implementation considerations | Real-world adoption guidance | 1–2 |
| Call to action + about + references | Next step, sponsor block, citations | 1–2 |

The arc is **inverted from a sales pitch**: a pitch starts with the product; a whitepaper ends with the product.

### 2. Executive-summary discipline

**Rules:**
- 1–2 pages, no more.
- States the problem, the proposed solution, the most compelling proof point, and the call to action.
- Stands alone.
- Written **last**, after the full paper is drafted.

### 3. Vendor-neutral vs sponsor-disclosed tone

**Vendor-neutral rules:**
- Name the problem before naming the product.
- Discuss competing approaches honestly; their drawbacks should be real, not strawmen.
- Cite third-party sources for problem-scoping statistics.
- Use the sponsor "About" block as the only place where company puffery is acceptable.

A whitepaper that reads as a thinly disguised sales pitch is rejected by 60% of B2B decision-makers.

### 4. Citation density and source hygiene

The right citation density is **2–6 cited claims per page**.

**Source hierarchy (descending credibility):**
1. Peer-reviewed academic research and government data
2. Major analyst firms (Gartner, Forrester, IDC, McKinsey)
3. Standards bodies (NIST, ISO, IEEE, W3C)
4. Industry surveys with disclosed methodology and sample size
5. The sponsor's own anonymized data (acceptable for proof sections)

**The "verify the verifiable" rule:** every claim of the form "X% of organizations report Y" must cite a source the reader can access.

### 5. Three whitepaper types

| Type | Best for | Authority ceiling | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backgrounder | Explaining a new category or technology | Medium-high | 6–12 pages |
| Numbered-list | Top-of-funnel, easy-to-skim | Low-medium | 4–8 pages |
| Problem/solution | Mid-to-bottom of funnel | High | 10–20 pages |

### 6. The "case study within a whitepaper" pattern

Embed a real (or anonymized) customer story in the proof section:
- Challenge → Approach → Result structure (mini case study)
- One direct customer quote, attributed
- Two-to-three quantified outcomes

### 7. Length and design budget

- 8–12 pages: standard problem/solution
- 12–16 pages: technical or research-heavy
- 16–20 pages: research-grade with original primary data
- 20+ pages: split into a series

**Design budget:** Branded title page, table of contents (clickable PDF), 2–4 charts/infographics minimum, pull quotes every 2–3 pages, case-study sidebar boxes.

### 8. Gated vs ungated

**Gate when:** Original research with high differentiation value; mid-or-bottom-funnel audience.

**Ungate when:** Foundational education; high SEO value; technical and form-allergic audience.

**Hybrid pattern (modern best practice):** First 2–3 pages ungated, full PDF gated behind a form.

### 9. Title and subtitle craft

```
Title: The State of Real-Time Data in Financial Services, 2026
Subtitle: Why 73% of risk teams still operate on stale data — and the architectural shift required to fix it
```

### 10. References, appendix, and methodology

**References appendix:**
- Numbered or alphabetized
- Full citation: author, title, publisher, year, URL or DOI
- ~5–25 references typical

**Methodology appendix (for research-based papers):**
- Sample size, geography, role/seniority breakdown
- Survey instrument or interview-protocol summary
- Time frame of data collection
- Disclosure of who funded the research

## Full whitepaper outline (problem/solution, 10–12 pages)

```
Title page
  - Title + subtitle
  - Author name(s) + affiliation
  - Sponsor disclosure
  - Publication date and version

Executive summary (1–2 pages)
  - Problem, Why current approaches fall short
  - New approach, Evidence preview, Call to action

Section 1: The industry problem (2–3 pages)
Section 2: Why current approaches fall short (2–3 pages)
Section 3: A new approach (2–4 pages)
Section 4: Evidence (2–4 pages)
Section 5: Implementation considerations (1–2 pages)
Section 6: Call to action (1 page)

Appendix A: References
Appendix B: Methodology
```

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Sales-pitch tone throughout | Vendor-neutral problem analysis; sponsor visible only in About block |
| Executive summary is just the intro | Write exec summary last; ensure it stands alone |
| Uncited statistics | Every "X% of..." claim cites a verifiable source |
| Strawman competitor analysis | Describe competing approaches with their actual strengths |
| First-person voice ("we believe...") | Third-person company-as-narrator; first person only in About block |
| 24-page paper with no design | 8–12 pages is the engagement sweet spot |
| No case study, no proof | Embed a case study sidebar |
| No references appendix | 5–25 numbered references at the end |
