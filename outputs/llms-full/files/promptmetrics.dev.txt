## About

We work primarily with EU teams where the EU AI Act and GDPR shape every architectural decision.

Our thesis: the bottleneck on AI in real businesses is not the model — it's the shape of the work. Most teams bolt AI onto a workflow that wasn't designed for it, then are surprised when it fails in month three. We redesign the workflow first, then add a governed AI skill on top, with a human gate.

## How it works

**Human-in-the-loop governance.** You stay in charge, at every step. We use a draft-then-review workflow: the system produces a draft, a person reviews it, and nothing ships without sign-off. Nothing is a black box — the skills we build are markdown files that any developer can read and maintain.

**The 4 Ds & 5 Cs.** Two frameworks: the soil, and what we plant.

- The 5 Cs (the people): Creativity, Curiosity, Courage, Compassion, Communication. These human qualities decide whether a team thrives with AI or resists it.
- The 4 Ds (the work): delegation (what to hand off, what to keep), description (asking clearly), discernment (judging what comes back), and diligence (doing it responsibly, safely).

**Measurement first.** We measure before we touch anything. Your number, not ours. Baseline and post-build numbers come from your real environment, and you own the data.

**EU AI Act & EU data.** Based in Berlin. We don't dodge the EU questions. The governed, approval-gated, audit-trailed layer we build is the compliance architecture, not an afterthought. From August 2, 2026 the EU AI Act reaches how you use AI, not just who builds it: Article 50(1) chatbot disclosure and 50(2) watermarking bind the provider, while as a deployer you own Article 4 (a team trained to use AI properly) and Article 50(4) (a human-perceivable label on deepfakes and AI-generated content). Systems already on the market before that date have until December 2, 2026 for the 50(2) marking rule. The high-risk regime lands later: Annex III on December 2, 2027, Annex I on August 2, 2028. We classify the workflow's risk tier and build the Article 9/11/12 artifacts as part of the deliverable. All processing stays in EU residency.

## FAQ

**Q: Is PromptMetrics a SaaS tool?**
A: No. We killed our SaaS in 2026. We're a consultancy; the skills registry is open source as Operator Stack.

**Q: Do you only work with EU teams?**

**Q: What does "governed AI" mean?**
A: Every skill has a human approval checkpoint, an audit trail, and plain-text markdown instructions. Nothing ships without sign-off, and nothing is a black box.

**Q: How is this different from hiring an AI agency?**

## Field notes

- [It's not the tools. It's the shape](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/it-s-not-the-tools-it-s-the-shape): AI didn't just make work faster — it changed what work is. The bottleneck is no longer the model; it's the shape of the workflow you wrap it around. Teams that redesign the shape first get durable gains; teams that bolt AI on top get month-three failures.
- [Why We Killed Our SaaS to Open-Source LLM Observability for the EU](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/open-source-eu-llm-observatory): Paid observability tools hold customer data hostage and struggle with EU residency. We moved the registry to open source so teams can self-host, audit, and maintain their own governance layer.
- [The 4 AI Loops of Death That Kill EU Startups Before Series A](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/4-ai-loops-killing-eu-startups): Hidden cost, drift, compliance, and trust loops destroy startup margins before the EU AI Act deadline. The fix is not more tools — it's explicit human gates, token budgets, and measurement before and after.
- [Prompt Engineering as Code: Why "Magic Strings" Kill AI Reliability](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/prompt-engineering-as-code): Hardcoded prompts in source code create the same technical debt as hardcoded SQL did. Moving prompts to versioned, tested, CI-governed artifacts prevents regressions and makes EU AI Act traceability possible.
- [Claude Code Agent Teams vs. Subagents: Is the 7x Token Cost Worth It?](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/claude-code-agent-teams-vs-subagents-cost-analysis): Agent Teams can multiply velocity, but the token premium only pays off when the problem has real parallel workstreams. For most workflows, subagents or well-structured single-agent loops are cheaper and easier to reason about.
- [The 5 Most Common Problems with Agentic AI in Production — And How to Solve Them](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/common-problems-with-agentic-ai-in-production-and-how-to-solve-them): Cost spirals, silent failures, compliance gaps, and human-overhead are the normal failure modes. The common fix is a governed loop with a measurable baseline and a mandatory review step.
- [7 EU AI Act Architecture Traps for SaaS CTOs (And How to Fix Them)](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/eu-ai-act-architecture-traps-saas): Logging, risk classification, human oversight, and deletion are architectural decisions, not legal afterthoughts. SaaS CTOs should map them before choosing models, not after the first audit.
- [Your AI Agent Can't Explain Itself: Why LLM Observability Fails EU AI Act Compliance](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/llm-observability-vs-eu-ai-act-compliance): Standard tracing proves the model ran; it rarely proves why the output was accepted. Article 12 requires a decision audit, which means a human gate plus a structured justification, not just a log.
- [How to Build a Production LLM Observability Stack in 2026](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/production-llm-observability-guide): The winning stack has three layers: tracing (Langfuse), cost control (LiteLLM), and governance (Operator Stack / PromptMetrics). Pick one tool per layer and integrate them at the prompt/eval boundary.
- [The AI Builder's Guide to Building Skills for Claude Code](https://www.promptmetrics.dev/blog/the-ai-builder-s-guide-to-building-skills-for-claude-code): A SKILL.md file is a contract: it tells the agent what it may do, what data it can touch, and what the review step is. Good skills are scoped, testable, and written for the human who will maintain them.
