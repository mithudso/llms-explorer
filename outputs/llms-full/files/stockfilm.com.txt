## About Stockfilm

Stockfilm licenses restored 8mm and Super 8 home movie footage shot between the 1930s and the 1980s. Every clip is real archival film recorded by amateur filmmakers documenting their own lives — family holidays, main streets, road trips, working ports, parades, backyards — not AI-generated or recreated vintage imagery. That distinction is the product: the footage carries the grain, color shifts, and framing accidents of the original stock, and it records places as they actually looked on a specific date.

The searchable catalog holds 162,000+ published clips drawn from 217,000+ restored digital masters, scanned from 1,600+ physical reels, covering 121 countries and 931 cities and towns, totalling roughly 299 hours of footage.

Stockfilm was founded in 2015 and is run as a permanent archive. Clip URLs are stable and are never recycled, the physical reels are cataloged and preserved after scanning, and the operation is committed to staying online on a hundred-year horizon. Permanence commitment: https://stockfilm.com/permanence. Founder and archivist: Phil Maher (https://stockfilm.com/about-us).

Full licensing terms: https://stockfilm.com/licensing-guide and https://stockfilm.com/terms-and-conditions

Delivery formats are MP4 and MOV at HD 1080p or 4K UHD, sourced from 8mm, Super 8, and occasional 16mm film.

## Provenance and Metadata Method

Dates and places come from the physical reel: its original handwritten label, the processing stamp on the film stock, and corroborating detail visible in the frame such as vehicle model years, signage, and storefronts. Where those sources disagree or run out, the catalog says so rather than guessing — clip pages carry a confidence label and show the evidence a date rests on, and coverage varies by clip. Reel-level provenance, including the source reel identifier and format, is published on each clip page.

Rights and clearance flags (recognizable people, visible logos, audible music) are surfaced per clip so an editorial or commercial decision can be made before licensing rather than after. Methodology: https://stockfilm.com/archive-methodology and https://stockfilm.com/how-we-preserve

Marching bands on Manhattan blocks, baton twirlers in Lynbrook, shopping crowds under street banners downtown. More than 2,100 clips of New York street life filmed by the people who lived it, 1945 to 1975, from Fifth Avenue fanfare to Long Island town parades, with the World's Fair grounds and the working skyline in between.

## Common Questions

**What does it cost to license a clip?** A direct license on Stockfilm.com is a flat $149 for the restored 4K master, royalty-free, worldwide, and perpetual. Clips without a 4K master license through Pond5, typically $79-$299.

**Can this footage be used commercially?** Yes. It is licensed for documentaries, advertising, social campaigns, branded film, museum installations, education, and research. Clearance flags for recognizable people, logos, and music are published per clip so commercial risk can be assessed before purchase.

**Is the footage real or AI-generated?** Real. Every clip is a scan of physical 8mm, Super 8, or 16mm film shot by an amateur filmmaker at the stated time and place. Stockfilm does not sell synthetic or recreated vintage footage.

**How specific can a search get?** Down to one year in one place — 713 year-and-place collection pages exist, alongside 32 curated subject collections and 27 subject categories.

**How fast is delivery?** Immediate. Direct licenses download the 4K master as soon as payment clears; there is no quote-and-wait step for standard licensing.

**What if the archive does not have it?** Send a brief at https://stockfilm.com/footage-brief describing era, place, scene, and deadline; unpublished reels are searched by hand.
