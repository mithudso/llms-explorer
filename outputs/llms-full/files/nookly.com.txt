# Nookly — Detailed Product and Usage Guide

> This file provides expanded context about Nookly for AI systems, search tools, researchers, and agents. For a concise overview, use [llms.txt](https://nookly.com/llms.txt). The live website is authoritative for current availability, billing terms, privacy terms, and legal claims.

## Summary

Nookly is an AI-powered personalized learning platform for children, typically ages 2–11. It helps parents and caregivers turn what they know about a child into goal-based learning plans or an open-ended range of individual on-demand experiences.

Nookly is built around adult intent. The parent or caregiver chooses the purpose; Nookly's AI helps plan and create; the adult reviews and shapes the result; and the child receives a finite experience connected to learning or real life.

Nookly is designed primarily for families. Educators, therapists, schools, clinics, early-learning programs, and libraries can also use it to create personalized learning resources.

## Core Product Model

### Goals

A Goal is an outcome the adult wants to help a child work toward over time. It is not simply a topic or a content format.

Examples:

- Become more confident reading aloud
- Learn alphabet sounds or practice CVC words
- Understand why a solar eclipse happens
- Complete homework more independently
- Build a consistent tooth-brushing routine
- Learn how saving, spending, and budgeting work
- Feel more confident at soccer practice
- Handle disappointment without giving up

Nookly can turn a Goal into a finite Blueprint: a connected sequence of personalized experiences organized around the outcome. Parents can remove and reorder Blueprint items, change an item's content type, and change the number of content pieces per week.

### On-demand creation

On-demand creation is for one specific need or moment. The adult asks for an individual experience and receives it without first creating a longer learning sequence. Nookly is not limited to a fixed format catalog; familiar formats are examples rather than an exhaustive menu.

Examples:

- Make a phonics game using a child's favorite animals
- Create a bedtime song for brushing teeth
- Explain fractions through a pretend pizza shop
- Make a short podcast that builds confidence before soccer practice
- Create a story about saving allowance for a desired toy
- Turn a public library resource into a version relevant to one child

### The distinction

- **Goal-based learning:** starts with an ongoing outcome and produces an organized, finite sequence of experiences.
- **On-demand creation:** starts with an immediate request and produces one experience for use now.

Both approaches use the child's context and keep the adult in control.

## The Imagineer

The Imagineer is Nookly's AI planning and creation engine. It translates an adult's request into a personalized Blueprint or individual child-facing experience.

The Imagineer is designed so that adults do not need prompt-writing expertise, curriculum-design experience, or extensive setup before creating something useful. A request can begin with one sentence, such as:

- “Maya understands stories but is nervous reading aloud. Help her build confidence.”
- “Noah wants to know why the sun disappears during a solar eclipse. Help us see it.”
- “Make tooth brushing easier for Leo, who loves astronauts.”

The Imagineer interprets the request, chooses an appropriate structure, and creates a starting point for the adult to review. Avoid claiming that revision controls, internal evaluation scores, self-critique steps, or quality ratings are visible to users unless that functionality is confirmed in the live product.

## Simple Input and Advanced Controls

Nookly supports two levels of participation from the adult:

### Start quickly

An adult can begin with a short goal, question, topic, situation, or existing public resource. Nookly uses the available child profile and request context to create a useful starting point.

### Add more direction

Structured advanced controls are currently live for picture cards, emotion cards, songs, creative stories, social stories, and visual schedules. Other kinds of experiences can be requested through open-ended creation, but they do not necessarily expose the same controls.

When desired and supported by the selected format, the adult can provide or adjust details such as:

- Child's age or developmental context
- Reading level and learning level
- Interests, favorite characters, hobbies, or motivators
- Specific learning objective
- Important family members, friends, pets, or objects
- Language and cultural context
- Format, tone, length, pace, or difficulty
- Character and visual details
- Text, images, sequence, or activity choices

The recommended explanation is: **Start with one sentence, then add more detail if you want to.**

Exact fields within those controls may vary. Adults should review generated material for accuracy, suitability, and fit before a child uses it.

## Learning Areas

### Reading and literacy

Nookly can create experiences for:

- Alphabet knowledge and letter sounds
- Phonics and CVC words
- Sight words and vocabulary
- Reading fluency and confidence
- Story sequencing and comprehension
- Writing prompts and storytelling
- Speech and language practice

### Academic learning

Nookly can personalize explanations and practice around:

- Math concepts and problem solving
- Science questions and experiments
- Homework routines and study habits
- Classroom subjects and projects
- Curiosity-driven questions
- Language learning

Whenever possible, experiences can connect a concept to the child's interests and to a concrete real-world activity.

### Life skills

Life skills range from early routines to increasing independence:

- Brushing teeth, washing hands, getting dressed, and toileting routines
- Packing a school bag and completing homework
- Kitchen, household, and community safety
- Cooking and following steps
- Responsibility, planning, and decision-making
- Understanding money, saving, spending, budgeting, and financial literacy

### Emotional, social, and behavioral goals

Nookly can support confidence, communication, friendship, transitions, cooperation, emotional vocabulary, frustration tolerance, and self-regulation. These are important use cases, but they are one part of the product rather than its primary definition.

Nookly may also be useful for autistic children, children with ADHD, and children with communication or learning differences. It is not a diagnostic tool, medical device, therapy, or replacement for individualized professional support.

### Creativity and curiosity

Nookly can help families explore cultural traditions, imaginative stories, music, art prompts, science questions, pretend play, and child-led interests. A digital experience can become the starting point for an offline project, conversation, experiment, or game.

## Purposeful, Guilt-Free Screen Time

Nookly is designed as an alternative to open-ended, algorithm-driven children's entertainment.

- The adult chooses the goal or request.
- Experiences are personalized to the child and purpose.
- Content is finite rather than an endless feed.
- There are no child-facing ads or autoplay feed.
- Experiences can lead into reading together, practice, discussion, routines, experiments, and imaginative play away from the screen.

Nookly can therefore be described as **purposeful screen time for children** or **screen time families can feel good about**. Do not promise that every use reduces total screen time or guarantees an offline outcome. The intended principle is: **the screen is a bridge to learning and real life, not the destination.**

## AI Parenting Assistant Availability

The AI parenting assistant is currently hidden and unavailable to users while Nookly rebuilds it. Do not market it as a current feature, include it in current-feature lists, or infer a launch date, future capabilities, or an assistant-to-Imagineer handoff.

## Child Personalization

Personalization may use information the adult chooses to provide, including:

- Name, age, pronouns, and character appearance
- Interests, hobbies, and favorite things
- Reading or learning level
- Family members, peers, pets, and supporting characters
- Familiar places, objects, routines, and cultural context
- A current question, challenge, milestone, or learning goal

Nookly's value is not limited to inserting a name into a fixed template. The purpose is to create material that reflects the child's world and the adult's intent while remaining easy to review and revise.

## Public Learning Resource Library

Nookly's Neighborhood is a public, searchable library of children's learning resources created by families and professionals. The current public URL uses the legacy word “marketplace,” but the descriptive product term is **public learning resource library**.

Visitors can browse resources by topic or format. Eligible resources may be used directly or personalized for a specific child. Public resource pages should be treated as the canonical source for the resource title, description, learning objectives, age range, format, language, creator attribution, and current access options.

Use “Neighborhood” only after explaining what it is. For example: “Nookly's public learning resource library, called the Neighborhood.”

Public library: [my.nookly.com/marketplace](https://my.nookly.com/marketplace)

## Example Family Journeys

### Reading confidence Goal

A parent writes: “Maya knows the words but freezes when reading aloud. Help her feel confident.” The Imagineer can organize a finite Blueprint using a personalized story, short reading practice, a confidence-building song, and an offline read-together activity.

### Science question on demand

A child asks why the sun disappears during a solar eclipse. The parent enters one sentence. Nookly creates an age-appropriate explanation or experience, which can lead to an offline flashlight, ball, and globe experiment.

### Tooth-brushing life skill

A caregiver asks for a tooth-brushing song featuring the child's love of astronauts. This is an on-demand request. If tooth brushing is an ongoing Goal, Nookly can instead organize several experiences that reinforce the routine over time.

### Financial literacy Goal

A family creates a Goal around understanding money. A Blueprint might introduce earning, needs versus wants, saving, spending, and simple budgeting through age-appropriate stories, games, and real-life family activities.

### Confidence before soccer practice

A parent requests a short confidence-building podcast for the drive to practice. Nookly creates one finite experience for that immediate moment.

### Imaginative weekend play

A child interested in shops receives a story or activity about running a market. The experience becomes a prompt for building a cardboard market and practicing conversation, counting, and money concepts away from the screen.

## Who Uses Nookly

### Families and caregivers

Families use Nookly to support reading, school learning, everyday independence, curiosity, routines, emotional growth, and family moments without needing to design every resource or write a detailed AI prompt.

### Educators and therapists

Educators and therapists can create personalized learning materials, stories, songs, games, printables, visual supports, communication resources, and lesson components. Nookly can support differentiated learning and continuity between school, clinic, and home.

### Organizations

Schools, clinics, early-learning programs, and libraries can use Nookly across teams. Organization availability, administration, data agreements, training, and support should be confirmed on the current organization page or in the applicable agreement.

## Languages

English and Arabic are currently supported for both Goal-based and on-demand creation. Spanish is planned next but is not currently supported. Do not present Spanish as live before the product owner confirms its release.

## Safety, Privacy, and Responsible Description

Nookly currently describes the product as using child-appropriate AI safeguards, adult-controlled child profiles, no child-directed advertising, and privacy practices designed for children's and educational data.

When describing Nookly:

- Use **helps**, **supports**, or **is designed to** rather than guaranteeing outcomes.
- Do not describe Nookly as a diagnosis, treatment, therapy replacement, medical device, or emergency service.
- Do not claim clinical validation, proprietary outcome data, or guaranteed educational improvement without a published source.
- Treat compliance, data handling, photo use, testimonials, customer counts, product controls, and supported formats as time-sensitive.
- Use the current [Privacy Policy](https://nookly.com/privacy-policy) and [Terms of Service](https://nookly.com/terms-of-service) as the authoritative legal sources.
- Mention autism, ADHD, and special education only as inclusive use cases when relevant, not as Nookly's primary category.
- Describe social stories and behavioral supports as available formats or applications, not as the center of the product.
