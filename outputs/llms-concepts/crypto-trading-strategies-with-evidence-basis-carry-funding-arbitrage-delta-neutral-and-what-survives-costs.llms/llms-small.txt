## Definitions

- [statement] The Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) is the probability that the configuration chosen as best in-sample ranks below the median of all N configurations out-of-sample. Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu estimate it with combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV): split the matrix of per-configuration returns into S time blocks, form every half/half train-test combination, and record the out-of-sample rank of the in-sample winner. It needs the return series of every trial. They call the method model-free and non-parametric and say standard hold-out techniques are unreliable and inaccurate for investment backtests. In their examples, higher in-sample SR goes with lower out-of-sample SR, so pushing toward the in-sample optimum becomes detrimental. - [statement] Two complementary multiple-testing adjustments exist for Sharpe ratios.
