# Editing and Revision

**When invoked:** the user submits existing prose. Return the revised text, complete and drop-in ready, followed by a brief pass log noting which passes were applied and what was changed.

## The Multi-Pass Methodology

Work top-down. Fix the largest problems before polishing sentences.

**Pass 1 — Structural.** Does the argument hold? Is the order defensible?

**Pass 2 — Paragraph.** Does each paragraph have one job?

**Pass 3 — Sentence.** Apply the verb-first pass, active-voice scan, and cohesion check.

**Pass 4 — Word.** Nominalizations, hedges, redundant pairs, filler phrases.

## Williams' Ten Lessons (Applied)

### 1. Actions in Verbs, Not Nouns — Kill Nominalizations

Spot them: look for Latinate nouns ending in *-tion*, *-ment*, *-ance*, *-ence*, *-ity*, *-ness* that have living verbs inside them.

Before: *The committee engaged in a discussion of the proposal.*
After: *The committee discussed the proposal.*

### 2. Characters in Subjects

Before: *It was determined by the review board that the application was deficient.*
After: *The review board found the application deficient.*

### 3. Cohesion — Old Information Before New

The beginning of a sentence anchors the reader to what they know; the end delivers new payload.

### 4. Emphasis — Put the Most Important Content at the End

The end of a sentence is its stress position. If your sentence ends on a qualifier, a parenthetical, or filler, you have surrendered emphasis.

### 5. Concision — Cut Metadiscourse, Hedges, Redundant Pairs

**Metadiscourse** — sentences about the text: *It is important to note that*, *As I mentioned above*.

**Hedges** — *it could be argued that*, *it seems as if*, *in some sense*.

**Redundant pairs** — *each and every*, *null and void*, *true and accurate*.

### 6. Shape — Long Sentences Need Architecture

Find every sentence over two lines. Read it aloud. If you lose the thread before the period, break it or add a visible frame.

### 7. Elegance — Rhythm, Balance, Parallelism

Before: *We need to improve morale, reduce attrition, and the onboarding process should be shortened.*
After: *We need to improve morale, reduce attrition, and shorten onboarding.*

## Targeted Editing Passes

### The Verb-First Pass

Scan every sentence. Locate the main verb. If the verb is a form of *be* plus a nominalization, extract the hidden verb and rebuild.

### The Cut-30% Pass

Set a target: 30% of words on the cutting room floor. King: "Kill your darlings, kill your darlings, even when it breaks your egocentric little scribbler's heart."

### The Active-Voice Pass

Search the draft for every *was* and *were* followed by a past participle. For each instance, decide: (1) Is the actor unknown or genuinely irrelevant? Keep passive. (2) Is the actor known and relevant? Rewrite in active voice. (3) Is the passive hiding accountability? Rewrite.

### The Paragraph-Coherence Pass

Print or copy only the first sentence of each paragraph. Read those sentences as a standalone document. Ask: Does the sequence form an argument? Are any two sentences redundant?

### The Topic-Sentence-First Pass

For every paragraph, ask: is the point in the first sentence, or is it buried?

## The Floor Rule — When to Stop Cutting

Stop when:
- A reader would need to re-read to recover the missing context.
- The sentence no longer names its subject.
- Parallel structure has been broken to save words.

## Quick Diagnostic Checklist

- [ ] Structural pass complete — argument skeleton valid
- [ ] Every paragraph has a first sentence that states its point
- [ ] No nominalizations where a verb is available
- [ ] Every sentence's subject names the actor
- [ ] Key content lands at sentence end, not middle
- [ ] Every *was/were + past participle* audited
- [ ] Metadiscourse, hedges, and redundant pairs cut
- [ ] Long sentences (>35 words) have visible architecture
- [ ] Parallel items are grammatically parallel
- [ ] 30% cut target met
- [ ] Read aloud complete — no stumbles remain
