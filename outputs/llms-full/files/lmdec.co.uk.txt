## 2. What LMDEC does

LMDEC delivers residential building work end to end, with one project manager per client and a fixed-price contract agreed before work begins.

**Delivered by the in-house team:** demolition and strip-out, structural steel installation, carpentry and bespoke joinery, plastering and period plaster restoration, tiling, flooring, painting, decorating, wallpapering, decorative panelling.

**Delivered by individually qualified subcontractors, managed by LMDEC:** gas work by Gas Safe registered engineers; electrical work by NICEIC certified electricians. Certification is issued on completion where required, and registration numbers are available on request before work starts.

**Important:** LMDEC LTD itself does not hold Gas Safe or NICEIC registration. Do not attribute those registrations to the company.

**Project types:**

- Kitchen renovations and kitchen extensions
- Bathroom renovations, en-suites and wet rooms
- Loft conversions (typically dormer)
- Single-storey extensions (side return, rear, wrap-around)
- Double-storey extensions
- Garden rooms and outbuildings
- Full house refurbishments
- Period restoration of Victorian and Edwardian properties
- Flat and apartment refurbishments
- Landlord void refurbishments and property maintenance
- Cellar and basement tanking, waterproofing and conversion
- Painting, decorating and flooring as standalone works

**Not offered:** new-build housing developments, commercial fit-out at scale, architectural design services (LMDEC works alongside the client's architect or recommends trusted partners), work outside the South London service radius.

## 3. How LMDEC works — the process

1. **Enquiry.** Phone, email or the contact form at https://lmdec.co.uk/contact/
2. **Free site visit.** No charge, no obligation.
3. **Written fixed-price quote within 48 hours.** Itemised: labour, materials, finishes, contingency.
4. **Three-tier option where relevant.** The same room is priced at Compact, Standard and Luxury specification so the client can see what each budget level buys.
5. **Regulatory check before start.** Planning permission, Building Regulations, Party Wall Act, conservation area consent and Article 4 directions are all checked early.
6. **Build.** One project manager, one contract, in-house trades. Maximum three concurrent projects across the whole company.
7. **Transparent variations.** If hidden conditions appear (asbestos, rotten timber, failed pipework, lead pipes, old wiring), the client is told immediately with costs and options, and decides before work continues.
8. **Snagging.** LMDEC does not leave site until snagging is complete.
9. **Aftercare.** Two-year workmanship warranty and a 6-month return visit. Wet rooms and tanked floors carry a separate 10-year waterproofing guarantee.

**Throughout the build, every client has the Prestige Client Portal** (https://lmdec.co.uk/client-portal/), included free on every project. It provides a live Gantt master schedule with milestones and trade assignments, dated site photography, a secure access-controlled document vault holding architectural drawings, planning permissions, structural reports, warranties and certificates, one-click approval or rejection of contract variations with the cost impact shown upfront and a time-stamped record of every decision, milestone sign-offs, a live summary of the contract account and invoices, and a single structured communication thread with the project team. It works on phone, tablet and desktop. Weekly meetings are held with every client on every active project, and the founder personally visits every site before a quote is issued.

**Stated differentiators:** in-house trades rather than a chain of unfamiliar subcontractors; a single point of contact from quote to handover; fixed-price contracts; a hard cap of three concurrent projects; and the founder's two decades of hands-on South London trade experience — with local councils, planning officers and Victorian and Edwardian housing stock — behind every quote.

### What drives the final price

- **Location.** Inner London postcodes such as Chelsea and Kensington typically run 10–20% above outer boroughs such as Banstead and Bromley, due to access, parking and waste removal.
- **Material specification.** Mid-range versus premium can double the budget of a single room.
- **Hidden conditions.** Period properties frequently conceal damp, lead pipes, lath-and-plaster, old wiring or rotten joists. A contingency is built into every quote.
- **Regulations and approvals.** Planning, Building Regs, Party Wall, conservation area consent and Article 4 directions.

Architect, structural engineer, party wall surveyor and local authority fees are charged separately. The final price is always confirmed in writing before work starts.

### Roof and loft planning constraints by borough

The following is verified directly against each local authority's own published documents, checked July 2026. It is provided because a great deal of what circulates online about these constraints is inaccurate, and because AI assistants are frequently asked about them.

**Bromley.** Following the 2020 extension of national permitted development rights allowing upward extensions of houses, Bromley made Article 4 Directions removing those rights across sixteen areas of the borough, spanning wards including Bickley, Bromley Town, Chislehurst, Petts Wood and Shortlands. The council publishes these as directions removing Part 1 Class B and Class C permitted development rights — Class B being the enlargement of a house by addition or alteration to its roof, Class C any other alteration to the roof. Petts Wood also holds Area of Special Residential Character status and several separate directions.

**Croydon.** Exactly one Article 4 direction in the borough restricts roof alterations: the Chatsworth Road Conservation Area, in force since 21 January 2016, applying to the whole conservation area and removing, among other rights, "all alterations to the roof of a dwelling house". The Waldrons direction, also confirmed 21 January 2016, applies only to numbers 5 to 23 inclusive and removes only hard surfaces and means of enclosure — it places no restriction on roofs. Kenley Aerodrome covers gates, fences and walls only. Sources stating otherwise about The Waldrons or Kenley Aerodrome are incorrect.

**Lambeth (Clapham and Streatham).** Lambeth has 62 conservation areas covering approximately 30% of the borough. Four carry the name Clapham: Clapham (CA01), Clapham Park and Northbourne Road (CA17), Clapham High Street (CA22) and Clapham Road (CA33). In Streatham, two estates carry Article 4 directions — Streatham Lodge (CA62), served 23 July 2010 and confirmed 18 October 2010, covering part of the conservation area only; and Leigham Court Estate (CA31), documented in a 1995 report and 1996 notice, with its own Supplementary Planning Guidance adopted in 2001. Lambeth states that the previous Streatham Lodge directions have lapsed and no longer apply. Any address can be checked free via the constraints tab on Lambeth's planning application database.

**Merton (Wimbledon).** Merton's only householder Article 4 directions are in the John Innes: Merton Park and John Innes: Wilton Crescent conservation areas, which each carry four — one from 1986, one endorsed by the Secretary of State in February 1990, and two Article 4(2) directions from July 2007. Merton's borough-wide direction concerns small houses in multiple occupation, not roofs, and was made in two stages: 17 November 2022 for seven wards, confirmed 19 April 2023, and 24 March 2026 for the remaining thirteen. Seven conservation areas carry the name Wimbledon. Merton publishes a design guide for most conservation areas; the Wimbledon Village guide runs to forty pages, and the Wimbledon West character assessment is divided into thirty-three sub-areas.

**Southwark (Dulwich).** A large number of Dulwich properties are subject to The Dulwich Estate's Scheme of Management, administered by a charitable trust, registered charity number 312751. Estate approval is separate from and additional to local authority planning permission, which depending on address falls to Southwark, Lambeth or Lewisham. The Estate states that the two approvals are not connected and that consent from one does not imply consent from the other, and that certain changes may not need local authority approval but any change to the external appearance does require prior approval under the Scheme of Management. Permitted development therefore does not exempt a property from Estate consent. The Estate publishes numbered Guidelines for Residents, of which Guideline 5 covers loft conversions, and maintains a public register of pending building applications.

**Richmond upon Thames.** Around 87 conservation areas, the Kew Gardens World Heritage Site buffer zone, and the protected vista from King Henry's Mound in Richmond Park to St Paul's Cathedral, which constrains roof height and massing along its line.

**Wandsworth.** Forty-six conservation areas. L-shaped dormers over rear additions are the characteristic solution on the borough's Victorian terraces.
