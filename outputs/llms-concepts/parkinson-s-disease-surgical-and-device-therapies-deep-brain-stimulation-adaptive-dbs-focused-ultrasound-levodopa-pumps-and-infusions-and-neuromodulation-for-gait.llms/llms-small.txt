## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] Oehrn and colleagues reported improved motor symptoms and quality of life versus clinically optimized continuous DBS in a blinded randomized crossover feasibility trial involving four male patients. Their personalized feedback markers were stimulation-entrained gamma oscillations in the subthalamic nucleus or motor cortex; the authors called for larger trials. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11826929/
- [statement] ADAPT-PD enrolled 68 participants previously stable on continuous DBS and medication. Its comparison of adaptive versus continuous DBS was open-label; randomization and blinding compared the two adaptive modes in participants tolerating both. The study therefore does not establish blinded randomized superiority of adaptive over continuous DBS. (low confidence) — https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2839117
- [statement] In ADAPT-PD, the post hoc primary performance threshold was met by 91% receiving dual-threshold and 79% receiving single-threshold adaptive DBS. This measured preservation of good on-time relative to continuous DBS rather than a superiority endpoint. Single-threshold treatment reduced total electrical energy delivered by 15%; this was not a measured 15% battery-longevity extension. (low confidence) — https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2839117
- [statement] A nine-patient double-blind randomized crossover trial using consecutive one-month treatment periods and beta-band dual-threshold control reported no statistically significant adaptive-versus-continuous DBS differences; the authors reported between-patient heterogeneity and called for adequately powered follow-up. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765432/
- [statement] In a randomized double-blind crossover assessment of directional versus omnidirectional stimulation, directional stimulation widened the therapeutic window in 183 of 202 evaluable participants (90.6%), with a mean increase of 41%. Therapeutic window denotes stimulation amplitudes achieving symptom relief without side effects; the study enrolled 234 subjects. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34047410/
- [statement] A separate 19-patient randomized directional-versus-omnidirectional crossover trial found no significant differences in primary rigidity or secondary clinical motor outcomes. Its discussion reported 30% lower stimulation intensity with directional stimulation, while chronic motor performance could worsen under the tested directional settings. Wider therapeutic windows should not be equated with universally superior motor outcomes. (low confidence) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00812-0
- [statement] Both STN and GPi are effective motor-symptom targets; the CNS guideline supports either target for motor treatment rather than a universal target preference. (medium confidence) — https://www.cns.org/guidelines/congress-of-neurological-surgeons-systematic-revie/ · keywords: Parkinson's disease surgical and device therapies: deep brain stimulation, adaptive DBS, focused ultrasound, levodopa pumps and infusions, and neuromodulation for gait · also: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536714/
- [statement] The randomized unilateral COMPARE trial reported no significant STN-versus-GPi difference in its co-primary mood and cognitive outcomes at optimal settings. It also reported adverse mood effects with ventral stimulation in both targets and greater letter-fluency deterioration in nonoptimal STN conditions; these setting-specific findings should not be generalized into inevitable global cognitive decline. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2692580/
- [statement] A 2022 Bayesian network meta-analysis associated LCIG and DBS with greater six-month OFF-time and quality-of-life improvement than apomorphine infusion or best medical therapy, with no significant LCIG-versus-DBS difference. Its indirect comparison mixed randomised and non-randomised studies, and carried three-month LCIG/apomorphine trial outcomes forward to six months. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9712309/
- [statement] A 2019 meta-analysis of five cohort studies involving 257 patients found no significant LCIG-versus-STN-DBS difference in UPDRS-III motor scores or adverse events; UPDRS-IV improvement favored STN-DBS, with substantial heterogeneity. Lack of significance does not establish equivalence. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6718716/
- [statement] In the 12-week active-controlled phase 3 foslevodopa-foscarbidopa trial, the incremental gain in ON time without troublesome dyskinesia versus oral immediate-release levodopa-carbidopa was 1·75 h; the incremental OFF-time reduction was 1·79 h. This was a comparison with oral therapy, not DBS or intestinal infusion. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36402160/
- [statement] In that foslevodopa-foscarbidopa trial, adverse events caused premature discontinuation in 16 of 74 infusion participants (22%) versus one of 67 oral-treatment participants (1%); infusion-site events were common. These trial-specific rates should not be used as a cross-device safety league table. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36402160/
- [statement] A 2026 systematic review pooling subcutaneous levodopa and foslevodopa regimens found clinically meaningful OFF-time improvement versus oral levodopa, but patient-experience gains were statistically rather than clinically significant, with more infusion-site adverse events; these findings do not compare the pumps directly with DBS. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12853972/
- [statement] The 2024 review associates intestinal levodopa therapies with tube displacement, wound problems or infections, abdominal pain and polyneuropathy. It describes apomorphine infusion-site nodules, nausea, somnolence and possible impulse-control disorders; these are therapy-specific risks rather than a single common device-risk profile. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11502575/
- [statement] In the 27-patient pilot randomized thalamotomy trial, the on-medication treated-hand tremor subscore improved a median 62% after ultrasound versus 22% after sham at 3 months; this is a score reduction, not a percentage of patients cured. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5822192/
