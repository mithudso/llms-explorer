## Original study: Cross-asset transfer — do the top five survive a change of asset?

> **Historical evidence.** This is an archival research report from August 2026, not a current performance claim or a recommendation to trade. Matching a saved output does not reproduce an experiment. The experiments were not rerun after later fixes to accounting, borrowing, metrics and selection. Results below retain the original study's assumptions and limitations.
>
> **Source availability.** References to `research/`, `backtester/` and result files identify the original research checkout. Those files and its market-data cache are not bundled with this article. Commands describe that historical environment; they are not a complete installation guide for a fresh checkout. Statements about tests passing describe the original study unless an explicit later check is identified.

## Why this test, and why it is the one that mattered

[the sol strategy rankings study](/blog/research/sol-strategy-rankings/) measures PBO at **0.700** at the daily horizons against a 0.500
noise line, which means in-sample rank is anti-informative and the SOL leaderboard cannot
certify itself. Checks outside the original ranking can test additional failure modes, though none alone certifies an edge.
[the sol robustness top five study](/blog/research/sol-robustness-top-five/) used two — the CPCV path distribution and ±10% parameter perturbation
— and explicitly listed what it did **not** establish: that any of it transfers to another
asset.

This is that test. Parameters are **not refitted**: every configuration runs the SOL-tuned
parameters from `research/sweep.py`. Refitting would answer "can this mechanism be made to fit
DOGE", which at PBO 0.700 the search cannot answer honestly. Holding parameters fixed answers
the narrower question that actually matters: *do the choices made on SOL transfer?*

**A poor DOGE or ZEC number tests the transfer of these settings to that asset and historical window. It does not establish a general property of the asset.**

## Two verification gates recorded by the original study

1. **Harness gate.** The script reproduces `cpcv_all25_btc_eth_1d.csv` — a file quoted in
   [the sol robustness top five study](/blog/research/sol-robustness-top-five/) and two coin spokes but which no committed script previously
   re-derived — to a **max absolute difference of 5.0e-07** across all 50 rows. That closes a
   real reproducibility gap as a side effect. *(This tolerance is printed at runtime by
   `--self-test` and is deliberately not stored in a CSV: a stored gate result would be an
   assertion about the past. These historical gates were not rerun for this publication and do not certify the current engine.)*
2. **Composite gate.** Run on SOL, the `--top5` path reproduces all five published medians
   (+1.345, +1.290, +1.054, +0.774, +0.696) to a **max absolute delta of 0.0004**, with
   matching path fractions and returns. The composite wiring is therefore the same wiring that
   produced the recommendation.

## Result 2 — the SOL ordering did not carry across assets

The two configurations ranked **1st and 2nd** on SOL are among the worst transfers. The two
ranked **3rd and 4th** are the only ones positive on all three assets.

- **#2 is the sharpest single finding in this file.** On the **same 15 paths**, it is **100%
  positive on SOL** (median +1.290, +68.9% return) and **0% positive on DOGE** (−0.706, −35.5%).
  Not one path in fifteen. A flawless path fraction on one asset told us nothing about the next,
  and because the path count is identical the comparison cannot be explained away by evaluation
  geometry.
- **#1**, the top-ranked configuration overall, goes **negative on ZEC** with only 24% of
  paths positive.
- **#3 and #4 survive**: positive median and positive path majority on all three assets. #4
  `obv_trend_60` has the smallest degradation of all ten transfers (−0.199 on DOGE).

The within-SOL PBO warning and the observed transfer changes both caution against interpreting the SOL ordering as a durable ranking. The transfer comparison does not calibrate PBO as a cross-asset forecast or prove a general ranking inversion across assets.

## Result 3 — direction transfers, reversion does not

All 25 singles, medium horizon, five assets. **Eight are positive on all five**, and the
`family` column is the repo's own `FAMILY` taxonomy from
`backtester/core/strategies/__init__.py`, not a label applied here:

| Strategy | Family | SOL | BTC | ETH | DOGE | ZEC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `adx_trend` | regime-filter | +0.336 | +0.575 | +0.495 | +0.188 | +1.037 |
| `bb_breakout` | breakout | +0.481 | +0.075 | +0.486 | +0.452 | +0.394 |
| `breakout` | breakout | +0.078 | +0.358 | +0.035 | +0.341 | +0.576 |
| `keltner` | breakout | +0.213 | +0.692 | +0.625 | +0.243 | +0.585 |
| `ichimoku` | trend | +0.023 | +0.825 | +0.433 | +0.194 | +0.304 |
| `macd` | trend | +0.321 | +0.496 | +0.253 | +0.424 | +0.317 |
| `ts_momentum` | momentum | +0.025 | +0.687 | +0.739 | +0.057 | +0.375 |
| `obv_trend` | volume-flow | +0.189 | +0.102 | +0.205 | +0.363 | +0.434 |

**Not one of the eight is from a reversion family**, and the converse holds too: **no
reversion-family strategy clears all five assets.** The four least consistent — positive on
only 2 of 5 — are `grid`, `bb_reversion`, `vwap_reversion` (all `mean-reversion`) and
`stochastic` (`oscillator-reversion`). Every `mean-reversion` member scores 4 or worse
(`ou_reversion` 4, `zscore` 3, the other three 2). The split falls along the repo's own family
lines and was not designed for: the sweep ran every registered strategy without regard to
family.

Two cautions before this is read as an endorsement of trend-following. First, the magnitudes:
several of the eight clear zero by margins (+0.023, +0.025, +0.035, +0.057) for which no uncertainty interval or significance test was supplied, so the defensible claim is about **sign consistency across assets**, not
size of edge. Second, four of the five assets rose over much of the window, and a directional
rule is advantaged by that in a way a reversion rule is not — this is evidence about these five
series, not a law about mechanisms.

## Result 4 — sign counts vary markedly across the tested series

Positive median path Sharpe, out of 25 singles, same parameters throughout:

|---|---|---|---|
| BTC | **25/25** | **24/24** | `ou_reversion` (+1.657, rank 1) has **6 trades** — below the floor |
| ZEC | **24/25** | 24/25 | only `dual_momentum` failed |
| ETH | 18/25 | **18/24** | `hurst_switch` has 4 trades, median exactly 0.000 |
| DOGE | 16/25 | 16/25 | |
| SOL (daily) | 13/25 | 13/25 | matches [the sol strategy rankings study](/blog/research/sol-strategy-rankings/)'s medium-horizon count |
| SOL (hourly) | **0/25** | 0/25 | `cpcv_all25_1h.csv`; timeframe, not asset |

**The "Rankable" column applies the project's own 10-trade evidence floor**, and it is not
cosmetic on BTC. `cpcv_evaluate` marks a configuration `insufficient` below `min_total_trades`
and prints it DROPPED, but **still returns a full path count** — so a filter on `n_paths > 0`
counts rows the engine has just refused. BTC's *top-ranked* strategy is one of them: `ou_reversion`
medians +1.657 on **six trades**, which is not evidence at any useful confidence. So "every
registered strategy cleared zero on BTC" survives as 24 of 24, but **the row a reader would quote
first does not qualify to be ranked.** `btc-bitcoin.md` had independently flagged "effective n on
BTC is 24" as an open item for a different reason (two byte-identical rows); both point the same
way. SOL, DOGE and ZEC have no below-floor rows — their minimum is 16 trades — and every top-5
configuration in Result 1 has 38 or more, so nothing in the transfer conclusion moves.

The identical strategy set ranges from 0/25 to 25/25 depending only on which series it is
pointed at. This is a descriptive contrast, not a variance decomposition: asset, calendar coverage and market regime are confounded. A related caution is the conclusion [the sol strategy rankings study](/blog/research/sol-strategy-rankings/) reached from BTC/ETH, now with three more columns and no
weakening.

## Result 5 — buy-and-hold beat all five on ZEC

| Asset | `buy_and_hold` median | Singles beating it |
|---|---|---|
| SOL | +0.534 | **1 of 24** |
| ZEC | +0.659 | **2 of 24** |
| BTC | +0.619 | 10 of 24 |
| ETH | +0.303 | 9 of 24 |
| DOGE | −0.041 | 16 of 24 |

**On ZEC, the best of the five recommended configurations (#2, +0.492) does not reach
zero-parameter buy-and-hold (+0.659)** — and loses on return too, +17.6% against +20.2%. On
SOL, where the recommendation was built, exactly one single beat holding the asset. Where
buy-and-hold did badly (DOGE, −0.041) most things beat it, by losing less.

Two honest caveats. First, `buy_and_hold` needs no warm-up, so it is evaluated on all 8 blocks
and 28 paths, while warm-up costs the slower configurations a block or two (21 or 15 paths). The
comparison is still the right one — needing no warm-up is a genuine property of the benchmark,
not an artifact — but the path counts are not identical and the CSV records them per row.

Second, **this comparison crosses horizons.** `buy_and_hold` here is the medium-horizon run,
while four of the five configurations (#1, #3, #4, #5) are long. Only #2 is medium, so only that
one row is strictly horizon-matched — and it is the row the sentence above names, because it is
also the best of the five on ZEC. The heading's "all five" is true numerically (the highest
top-5 ZEC median is +0.492 against +0.659) but is not a like-for-like comparison for four of the
five. No long-horizon ZEC `buy_and_hold` exists in the committed results, so there is nothing to
substitute; `buy_and_hold` takes no parameters, so its horizon label refers only to the cost and
block settings, not to any signal tuning.

## What this does not establish, and two confounds not corrected

- **Two assets are not a universe.** DOGE and ZEC are one more pair, not a general result.
  DOGE correlates +0.76 to +0.78 with BTC across every window measured in
  `research/coin-intelligence/doge-dogecoin.md` (that spoke is the source; no results CSV here
  carries a correlation column), so it is closer to a second BTC column than to an
  independent draw.
- **Calendar windows differ**, so asset is confounded with period: SOL/BTC/ETH span
  2021-06-17.., DOGE 1,890 bars from 2021-06-03, ZEC 2,043 bars from 2021-01-01. Block *i* is
  not the same weeks across assets. Aligning windows would discard real history and still
  leave the regimes unmatched, so the confound is disclosed rather than corrected.
- **Usable blocks differ per configuration.** Warm-up drops slow configurations from 28 paths
  to 21 or 15. Fewer paths is a weaker estimate; `n_paths` and `usable_blocks` are columns in
  both CSVs so no reader has to assume 28.
- **Nothing here is refitted, and nothing here should be.** The transfer numbers are not an
  upper bound on what these mechanisms could do on DOGE or ZEC with their own parameters. They
  are a measurement of whether SOL's choices carry — and mostly they do not.
- **No PBO for the new assets.** PBO needs a configuration population; this run evaluated 25
  singles plus 5 named configurations, not the 1,121-row retained CPCV search that made PBO meaningful.

## Consequences for the existing documents

1. **[the sol robustness top five study](/blog/research/sol-robustness-top-five/) finding 2 — "a volatility- or Hurst-based regime gate is the
   second most productive partner" — is contradicted by transfer and is downgraded.** The two
   configurations containing `vol_regime` (#1 and #5) suffered the largest degradations, and
   `vol_regime_60_0.5` goes negative on **both** new assets. As a medium-horizon single,
   `vol_regime` is positive on only 3 of 5 assets and one of those is +0.019.
2. **`obv_trend` is promoted to the transfer-supported mechanism.** It is the only member
   appearing in both surviving configurations (#3 and #4), and as a single it is positive on
   all five assets. Its path fractions on the new assets are **86% on DOGE — the highest of the
   25 — and 90% on ZEC, third of 25** behind `rsi` (100%) and `keltner` (95%).
3. **The ordering 1–5 should not be read as a preference ranking.** #3 and #4 transferred; #1
   and #2 did not. The document already ordered by checks cleared rather than by Sharpe;
   transfer is now a third axis and it reorders the set again.
