## The finding

In this dated sample, a missing 1m candle often was **not** a quiet minute. Across 31 gap runs sampled
over five assets, **26 (84%) had trades the candle endpoint dropped**.

| asset | bars | gap runs | missing min | sampled | dropped | est. dropped minutes (95% CI) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| SOL | 44,635 | 5 | 6 | 5 of 5 | 4 | 4 (2–5) |
| BTC | 44,640 | 1 | 1 | 1 of 1 | 1 | 1 (0–1) |
| ETH | 44,639 | 1 | 2 | 1 of 1 | 1 | 2 (0–2) |
| DOGE | 40,380 | 3,739 | 4,260 | 12 of 3,739 | **11** | **3,905 (2,752–4,196)** |
| ZEC | 43,579 | 987 | 1,062 | 12 of 987 | 9 | 796 (496–967) |

Intervals are Wilson score intervals on the sampled gap-run proportion, extrapolated to the
missing-minute count. This assumes the sampled run proportion applies to missing minutes, although runs have different lengths. The intervals are not design-based confidence intervals for individual minutes. DOGE and ZEC are **sample-based estimates**, not censuses: only
1 gap run per asset was actually repaired, while **12 sampled runs per asset were classified**. The other gaps were not repaired; the unsampled gaps were not exhaustively classified.

**SOL census versus the historical estimate:** all five SOL gap runs were inspected. The four traded runs span **2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 5 missing minutes**, and the repair added five bars. The table’s **4 (2–5)** is the original run-proportion extrapolation, not the observed minute count: it applies 4/5 to six missing minutes (4.8 before the source’s integer display). For SOL, use the observed **5 traded missing minutes**; its conditional extrapolation interval is not uncertainty about that complete observed census.

Three properties make this worse than a simple hole in the data:

1. **The gaps persisted in repeated requests.** Re-requesting an affected range narrowly, weeks later, still
   omits the minute and still returns the same wrong neighbouring bar. Refetching is
   not a repair for the observed ranges; the study used ticks to reconstruct the sampled repairs.
2. **Neighbouring bars can disagree with ticks even when present.** A missing-timestamp check alone does not flag them.
   DOGE 2026-07-06 01:37 reports volume 112.9 against **11,630.4** from ticks — a
   100x under-report in a bar that looks perfectly normal.
3. **It propagates to coarser bars, including prices.** The SOL 5m bar at 01:35 is
   short 254.782 SOL (9.6% of tick-reconstructed volume) and reports a low of 81.91 against a tick-reconstructed
   81.86. A backtest using bar lows for stops or liquidation would read that discrepancy.

### Not every diff is candle error

Some diffs are boundary attribution, not loss: at SOL 07-31, 23:30 is -0.135896 and
23:31 is +0.135896 — exactly zero-sum, one trade on the minute boundary assigned to
different sides by the two surfaces. Sub-0.01 price shifts paired with zero-sum
volume are attribution noise. Treat them as such rather than as recovered data.

### What the neighbour diffs do and do not show

In the two isolated single-minute drops the neighbour is too big by *exactly* the
dropped minute's volume (07-26 07:27 by 5.3562; 07-31 23:28 by 0.2402), consistent
with the dropped minute being folded into its neighbour. That does not extend to the
2026-07-06 01:38-01:39 outage, where neighbours are too *small* instead (BTC 01:37 by
12x, DOGE 01:37 by 100x). Two exact matches are a pattern; they are not a mechanism.

### The 2026-07-06 01:38-01:39 event

SOL, BTC, ETH, DOGE and ZEC are **all** missing 01:38 (most also 01:39). This is consistent with a
shared data-surface problem; the observations alone do not identify its cause.

## Reproducing

```bash
python3 research/candle_gap_audit.py --assets SOL,BTC,ETH --sample 5 --repair \
    --out-dir data/repaired --report research/CANDLE-GAP-AUDIT.md
python3 research/candle_gap_audit.py --assets DOGE,ZEC --sample 12 --seed 7 \
    --repair --max-runs 1 --out-dir data/repaired
```

Repaired series are mixed-provenance (candle bars except in repaired windows) and
live in gitignored `data/repaired/`, so they are regenerable rather than tracked.
