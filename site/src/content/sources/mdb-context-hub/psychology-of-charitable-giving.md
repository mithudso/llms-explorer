The empirical psychology of charitable giving — why people give (or don't). Distinct from fundraising-and-donor-psychology (which covers what to DO given they might give) and from psychology-of-charitable-giving being about the underlying motives.

Core motives: Andreoni's warm-glow / impure-altruism model (people give partly for the utility of giving itself, not only for the outcome).

The identifiable victim effect and singularity: Small, Loewenstein, and Slovic's work — one identifiable child gets more donation than statistics on many. Compassion fade / psychic numbing / pseudoinefficacy — help drops as the number of victims rises, because "helping any one of them feels like a drop in the bucket."

Empathy-altruism vs egoism debate: Batson's empathy-altruism hypothesis vs Cialdini's negative-state-relief counter-account.

The martyrdom effect (Olivola and Shafir — willingness to give rises when the pledge requires personal suffering).

Overhead aversion: donor rejection of gifts that pay for overhead rather than programs (Gneezy, Keenan, and Gneezy).

Moral identity (Aquino and Reed) and parochial altruism (in-group preference).

Prosocial framing: EPPM applied to donation, narrative vs statistics message framing, emotion appeals.

Nudge caveats: Mertens 2022 meta-analysis and the Maier et al. Bayesian reanalysis; the choice-architecture literature is real but overstated in some meta-analyses.
