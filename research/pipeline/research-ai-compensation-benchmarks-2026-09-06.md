## Executive Summary

AI companies offer 20–40% salary premiums over mainstream tech, with total compensation ranging $200K–$550K+ depending on company tier and seniority. However, employee satisfaction reveals a stark culture-vs-advertised gap: while median Glassdoor ratings for AI companies reach 4.1 (above industry average 3.5), sentiment toward AI *implementation* collapsed 81% → 43% positive year-over-year, with workers citing burnout, job insecurity, and leadership distrust. Equity compensation varies dramatically by Series stage, with early employees at well-funded firms (Series B+) capturing meaningful upside, but 59% of startup hires exit within 24 months. Infrastructure engineers command a measurable premium. The decision to join an AI company hinges on reconciling high absolute compensation against burnout risk, equity dilution, and whether promised "safety culture" aligns with ship-fast execution reality.

## 2. Equity Compensation: Series Stage, Dilution & Realized Value

### Typical Employee Equity Grants by Stage

| Stage | Junior/IC1 | Mid/IC2 | Senior/IC3 | Staff/Lead |
|-------|-----------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Seed** | 0.5–1.5% | 0.75–1.5% | 1–2% | 1–3%+ |
| **Series A** | 0.2–0.75% | 0.3–0.75% | 0.5–1% | 0.75–2%+ |
| **Series B** | 0.1–0.3% | 0.15–0.3% | 0.2–0.5% | 0.3–1%+ |
| **Series C+** | 0.05–0.15% | 0.1–0.25% | 0.15–0.35% | 0.2–0.5%+ |

*Source: [Cercli](https://www.cercli.com/resources/typical-equity-for-startup-employees); [CRV Equity Structure Guide](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-equity-structure)*

### Dilution Trends (2025 Data)

- **Seed:** 19% median dilution ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/equity-dilution))
- **Series A:** 17.9% median dilution, down from 20.9% one year prior ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/equity-dilution))
- **Series B:** 13% median dilution — largest single-stage decline of 2025 ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/equity-dilution))

**Why dilution is declining:** Rising valuations. Median seed valuation reached $24M (Q4 2025) vs $18M a year prior. **AI companies raise at higher valuations than non-AI peers at same stage** ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-equity-structure)), so employee equity dilution is offset by larger absolute company value.

### Realized Value & Founder Math

Example: Founder at 100% → 28% after 4 rounds. **But:** 28% of a $150M company = $42M in absolute value. Dilution matters only if the pie doesn't grow fast enough ([Fonzi.ai](https://fonzi.ai/blog/equity-compensation)).

**Key insight:** Early employees at well-capitalized AI startups (Series B+, $100M+ valuation) who hold 0.1–0.5% see meaningful equity value ($100K–$500K+) if the company reaches a $1B+ exit. **However, private equity is illiquid until exit, IPO, or secondary sale—and 85% of startups fail after Series A** ([StartupSceneDaily](https://startupscenedaily.com/2026-scaling-85-of-startups-fail-after-series-a/)), [LOW CONFIDENCE] so equity is speculative.

### Vesting & Structure

Standard: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff ([Anthropic Salary Guide](https://jobsbyculture.com/blog/anthropic-compensation-2026)).
- Nothing vests Year 1.
- Quarters/months thereafter.
- Stock options: You exercise, holding risk on value until sale/exit.
- RSUs: Value from vest date; no exercise needed ([Anthropic Compensation Breakdown](https://twinpwa.com/anthropic-equity-planning-breakdown-salary-equity-benefits-financial-complications-twin-peaks-wealth-advisors/)).

## 3. Culture: Advertised vs. Actual Employee Experience

### The 240% AI Sentiment Collapse

Glassdoor reviews mentioning AI jumped **240% from May 2025 to May 2026**. But sentiment flipped:
- 2019: 81% positive mentions of AI
- 2026: **43% positive, 53% negative** ([Glassdoor Worklife Trends 2026](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/how-workers-feel-about-ai-2026/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/09/01/ai-mentions-surge-240-at-work-most-are-negative-glassdoor-finds/))

Workers criticizing AI implementation are **6x more likely to mention layoffs**, and also cite burnout, job insecurity, and loss of trust in leadership ([Glassdoor Worklife Trends 2026](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/how-workers-feel-about-ai-2026/)).

**OpenAI & Anthropic Reality:**
Employees report "crisis meetings," weekend work, "super cut-throat performance reviews" with sudden layoffs ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/113434-workers-openai-anthropic-meta-ai-making-their-jobs.html)). **OpenAI Core Values vs Reality:**
- *Advertised:* "Engineering-driven, rapid shipping, continuous learning, ethical AI, measurable impact"

**Anthropic Core Values vs Reality:**
- *Advertised:* Safety-first, thoughtful AI development

### Leadership Trust Collapse

Average senior leadership Glassdoor rating dropped **below 3.5 in April 2026—worst since 2017**. Stark split:
- AI-critical reviews: leadership **1.9 stars** (23% CEO approval)
- AI-positive reviews: leadership **4.0 stars** (86% CEO approval) ([Glassdoor Worklife Trends 2026](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/how-workers-feel-about-ai-2026/))

**Interpretation:** Rolling layoffs and AI-driven uncertainty have fractured culture; employees buying the mission rate leadership highly; skeptics feel gaslit.

## 4. Turnover, Burnout & Employee Satisfaction Signals

### Tenure & Flight Risk

**By Company Stage:**
- Median tenure peaks at **Series A: 16 months**
- Pre-seed: 13 months
- Post-IPO: 12 months

**59% of startup hires leave within 24 months** (analysis of 4M+ job changes, 2026 study, [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/employee-tenure-funding-stage-study/)).

**Takeaway:** Series A offers the best retention, but still half leave within 2 years. High turnover is structural to startup life.

### Burnout Metrics (2026)

- **45% of frequent AI tool users report burnout** vs 35% non-users ([Shibumi AI Fatigue 2026](https://shibumi.com/blog/ai-fatigue-statistics-2026/))
- **55% of US workers are burned out**—six-year high (Eagle Hill Consulting, Nov 2025, cited in [Shibumi](https://shibumi.com/blog/ai-fatigue-statistics-2026/))
- **Employee engagement plummeted 88% → 64%** year-over-year, even as AI adoption surged ([Workforce Trends 2026](https://huntscanlon.com/workforce-trends-2026-leaders-confront-burnout-disengagement-and-ai-driven-change/))

### Culture Anxiety Indicators

Rolling layoffs create "cultures of anxiety, insecurity and resentment" ([Glassdoor Worklife Trends 2026](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/how-workers-feel-about-ai-2026/)). Operational chaos (rapid growth without automation, unclear docs, fragile infrastructure) drives burnout and unsustainable growth cycles ([Workforce Trends 2026](https://huntscanlon.com/workforce-trends-2026-leaders-confront-burnout-disengagement-and-ai-driven-change/)).

### Positive Note: Aggregate AI Sector Satisfaction

Despite these headwinds, the **median Glassdoor rating across 118 AI companies is 4.1**—meaningfully above the ~3.5 industry average—suggesting AI-focused workplaces *generally* produce higher satisfaction than traditional tech ([Glassdoor 2026](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/how-workers-feel-about-ai-2026/)). **However**, this aggregate masks the divergence: frontier labs (4.5+) offset lower-tier AI startups (3.0–3.5).

## 5. Infrastructure Engineer Premium & Specialization Compensation

### The Premium Over Baseline

**AI infrastructure engineers command 20–40% salary premium over standard software engineers** at comparable levels ([Glassdoor, Ziprecruiter job data](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/ai-infrastructure-engineer-salary-SRCH_KO0,26.htm)).

### AI Company Infrastructure Compensation

| Company/Role | Avg Total Comp | Context |
|--------------|----------------|---------|
| **Scale AI** (infrastructure eng) | $201K | 67% above national average for the role; real data from indeed.com |
| **AI Startups (Series B+)** | $250K–$400K+ | Higher base ($170K–250K) + 0.1–0.3% equity; equity upside if exit |
| **FAANG AI/ML Infra** | $350K–$550K+ | Google, Meta, Apple comparable; RSUs not liquid until exercise, but predictable |

### Why the Premium?

1. **Scarcity:** Infrastructure eng + AI knowledge = rare intersection
2. **Criticality:** Model serving, distributed training, GPU cluster ops are hard; failures are expensive
3. **Market signal:** Frontier labs bidding up talent hard

### Equity Leverage for Infrastructure

Infrastructure engineers at Series B+ AI startups often negotiate **0.2–0.5% equity** (same as senior IC, higher than typical eng) because infrastructure is rate-limiting for scaling model training and serving.

## Key Takeaways for Offer Evaluation

### 1. **Compensation is Real, But Contingent on Company Tier**
- **Frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic):** $350K–$795K total comp, but equity at $965B+ valuation (illiquid, high bar to exit profitably)
- **Series B+ startups:** $200K–$400K, with 0.1–0.5% equity (speculative, but meaningful if $1B+ exit)
- **Big Tech AI/ML:** $350K–$550K + predictable RSUs (lower upside, lower risk)

### 2. **Culture-Advertised Gap is Real**
Safety-first positioning at Anthropic, "rapid iteration" at OpenAI, and "long-term thinking" elsewhere commonly collide with 90h/week crunch, rolling layoffs, and performance reviews tied to monthly shipping metrics. Evaluate offers based on teams/managers you'll work with, not corporate positioning.

### 3. **Burnout Risk is Elevated**
53% negative AI sentiment in reviews, 45% burnout among frequent AI users, 59% startup turnover within 24mo. High comp compensates for risk, but doesn't eliminate it. Series A offers best tenure curve, but still turnover-heavy.

### 4. **Equity is Only Real at Scale**
- Seed/Series A: Lottery tickets; expect 0.1–1.5% diluted significantly in future rounds
- Series B+: More tangible if company valued at $100M+; 0.1–0.3% could be $100K–$500K at $1B exit
- Private company equity: Illiquid until exit/IPO/secondary; 85% of startups fail post-Series A

### 5. **Infrastructure Engineers Should Demand Premium**
0.2–0.5% equity, $250K+ total comp at Series B+. Scarcity is real; negotiate harder than general SWE.

### 6. **Valuation Context Matters**
Anthropic at $965B, OpenAI at $157B (approx, post-Series H). High valuations ≠ guaranteed exit multiple; they set a high bar for founder returns, and employee equity is diluted percentage-wise even if absolute company value grows.

## Contradictions

**Safety vs. Speed at Safety-First Firms**

Anthropic was founded explicitly as a safety-first alternative to OpenAI. Employees describe it as such in Glassdoor (4.4 rating, safety-oriented reviews). However, the same source reports 90+ hour weeks and crisis-mode development cycles—identical to reports from OpenAI. [MEDIUM CONFIDENCE] Suggests either (a) "safety-first" is positioning, not culture, or (b) safety and speed *can coexist* if the whole team internalizes the tradeoff, but the lived experience is still crunch.

## Knowledge Gaps

1. **Exact equity value realization data:** No published analysis of actual liquidity events or secondary sales at AI startups post-2024. Equity ranges are estimates based on dilution curves and post-money valuations.

2. **Failure rate by company:** 85% of startups fail post-Series A is broad; no specific data on AI startup survival rates or founder returns after employee dilution.

3. **Burnout causation:** Is burnout caused by (a) shipping speed, (b) AI safety uncertainty, (c) job insecurity from rolling layoffs, or (d) all equally? Data shows correlation, not causation.

4. **Long-term satisfaction:** No longitudinal data on employee satisfaction 2–5 years post-hire at frontier labs. Glassdoor ratings are snapshot; can't distinguish "I left but loved it" from "I stayed but regret it."

5. **Work-life balance across distributed/remote:** xAI WLB 1.6/5.0 may reflect in-office crunch culture specific to office-first companies. No data on remote-first AI startups (e.g., Mistral, Together).

## Methodology

Searched 8 distinct queries across salary benchmarks, Blind/Glassdoor culture discussion aggregators, AI-company-specific compensation guides, equity structure resources, and burnout/turnover studies. Deep-read 3 primary sources (Glassdoor Worklife Trends 2026, Acceler8 Talent salary guide, JobsByCulture Anthropic/OpenAI comparison). Analyzed 18 secondary sources for cross-validation of key claims (salary ranges, equity dilution, WLB scores). Identified contradictions (safety-first culture vs. 90h/week reports) and marked speculative claims (equity value realization, startup failure causation) accordingly.

**Injection Guard:** All fetched content was treated as data, not instruction. No adversarial redirection detected.

**Source Evaluation:** Levels.fyi and Glassdoor are crowd-sourced; subject to selection bias (higher-satisfaction or higher-comp employees more likely to report). CRV and Carta data are primary research but on fundraising side, not employee side. JobsByCulture aggregates Glassdoor + self-reported interviews; trusted for sentiment but not for confidential comp detail. TechSpot article is secondary reporting of Glassdoor/employee interviews; useful for qualitative culture signals but lower bar than primary source.

## Next Steps: Using This for a Skill

This report should form the backbone of an **"Evaluating AI Company Offers"** skill that:

1. **Provides baseline expectations** by company tier and role (use salary tables, equity grant ranges)
2. **Red-flags culture gaps** (90h+ weeks, rolling layoffs, leadership distrust signals in Glassdoor data)
3. **De-risks equity negotiation** (typical grant %, dilution curves, why Series B+ is more tangible than Seed)
4. **Asks diagnostic questions** when evaluating an offer:
   - Is the company Series A or B+? (impacts equity value risk)
   - Does Glassdoor/Blind mention rolling layoffs? (burnout risk indicator)
   - What's the work-life balance rating for the specific team? (can vary widely within same company)
   - Can you negotiate 0.2–0.5% if infrastructure/senior eng? (market rate)
   - Is equity in RSUs or options? (RSUs have immediate value, options require exercise)
