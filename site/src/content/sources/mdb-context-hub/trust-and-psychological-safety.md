# Trust, Rapport & Psychological Safety

Three distinct but interlocking literatures govern professional relationships. **Trust** is dyadic willingness to be vulnerable. **Rapport** is the moment-to-moment relational tone. **Psychological safety** is a group-level climate belief. They are not the same thing — build all three deliberately.

This skill is about trust **between people**. For trust in *machines/automation* (over-reliance, algorithm aversion, calibration), use `human-ai-interaction-psychology`.

**Meta-finding across all three:** trust and safety are slow to build and fast to destroy (Slovic). Match every move to that asymmetry.

Deep detail, the repair decision aid, the full 7-item scale, and source citations live in `references/trust-rapport-safety-detail.md`. Load it when you need specifics.

## 1. Interpersonal trust models

| Construct | Core idea | Operator application (customer + team) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ABI model** (Mayer, Davis & Schoorman 1995) | Trustworthiness = **Ability** + **Benevolence** + **Integrity**; trust = willingness to be vulnerable. | Integrity + ability read fast; benevolence is earned over time. New account/teammate: prove competence and keep promises now; don't fake care. |
| **Cognitive vs affective trust** (McAllister 1995) | Head-trust (track record, competence) vs heart-trust (emotional bond). Cognitive usually precedes affective. | Earn the customer's *cognitive* trust with reliability before expecting *affective* trust; a peer relationship needs both. |
| **Trust Equation** (Maister 2000) | (**Credibility** + **Reliability** + **Intimacy**) / **Self-Orientation**. | Highest-leverage lever = **lower self-orientation** (customer outcome over your quota). Reliability compounds; intimacy differentiates. |
| **Swift trust** (Meyerson, Weick & Kramer 1996) | Temporary teams import trust *presumptively* from role/credentials, then verify. Fragile. | First TAM engagement, new escalation bridge: borrow credibility from role/references/standards; protect it — one early miss does outsized damage. |
| **Stages of trust** (Lewicki & Bunker 1996) | Calculus-based → knowledge-based → identification-based (most resilient). | Early customer = calculus stage, breakable. Strategic, long-tenured accounts reach identification trust that survives an outage. |

## 2. Trust violation & repair

| Construct | Core idea | Operator application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Violation type → repair** (Kim et al. 2004) | **Competence** breach → **apology** beats denial. **Integrity** breach → **denial** beats apology *only when you're innocent*. | Blown SLA / wrong rec (competence) → apologize + show the fix. Honesty/commitment question (integrity) → if innocent, explain/deny with evidence; deny only if true. |
| **Reticence** (Ferrin, Kim et al.) | Silence is worse than a clear apology *or* a clear denial. | Never go quiet after a trust hit — unresolved suspicion festers. |
| **Asymmetry principle** (Slovic 1993) | Trust is built slowly, destroyed in an instant; one bad event erases many good ones. | Over-invest in prevention and *rapid, visible* recovery. You cannot average your way back. |

See the 6-step repair decision aid in the reference file.

## 3. Rapport & relationship dynamics

| Construct | Core idea | Operator application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3 components** (Tickle-Degnen & Rosenthal 1990) | Rapport = mutual attentiveness + positivity + coordination; positivity leads early, coordination later. | First meeting: visible warmth + full attention. Long relationship: demonstrated sync. |
| **Similarity-attraction** (Byrne 1971) | Shared attitudes/values drive liking (proportion, not count). | Surface *genuine* common ground (tech philosophy, prior tooling) — never manufactured agreement. |
| **Mere-exposure** (Zajonc 1968) | Repeated positive contact increases liking; first exposures matter most. | A short, reliable weekly touchpoint can beat a rare big meeting for warmth. |
| **Chameleon effect** (Chartrand & Bargh 1999) | Unconscious mimicry/synchrony increases liking — but *deliberate* exaggerated mimicry backfires. | Let synchrony emerge; the safe deliberate move is matching pace/medium/formality, not posture-mirroring. |
| **5:1 ratio** (Gottman) | Healthy relationships run ~5 positive : 1 negative; high-performing teams run positive-heavy. | Audit your interaction ledger with a tense account/teammate; deliberately add positives. (Exact team number is heuristic — see caveat.) |
| **Active-constructive responding** (Gable & Reis 2004) | How you respond to *good* news predicts bond quality more than bad news. ACR (enthusiastic + ask to elaborate) is the only style that builds it. | Highest-ROI micro-skill: when a customer/teammate shares a win, celebrate and ask them to say more. |

## 4. Psychological safety (team-level)

| Construct | Core idea | Operator application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Team psychological safety** (Edmondson 1999) | Shared belief the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking → learning behavior → performance. | An internal-team lever, not a customer one. Make it safe to ask, admit errors, experiment. |
| **7-item scale** (Edmondson 1999) | 7-point Likert, 3 reverse-scored (held-against-you, reject-for-difference, hard-to-ask-help). | Measure team safety with the scale (full wording in refs). |
| **Psych safety ≠ trust** | Safety = group climate, near-term, "is it safe to be *me*?"; trust = dyadic, longer-term, "will *they* act well?". | Don't assume dyadic trust delivers team safety or vice versa — build both. |
| **Leader behaviors** (*The Fearless Organization* 2019) | Frame work as learning; model fallibility; invite participation; respond productively to bad news. | Erode it by blaming the messenger or punishing honest mistakes. |
| **4 stages** (Clark 2020) | Inclusion → Learner → Contributor → **Challenger** safety. | Diagnose *where* it breaks; most teams stall before Challenger safety, killing dissent/innovation. |
| **Project Aristotle** (Google 2012-15) | Psych safety = #1 of 5 dynamics of effective teams. | How the team interacts beats individual talent. |

**Safety is NOT niceness.** Pair high safety with high accountability/standards (Edmondson's learning zone). Never sell it as "be nice." And trust is NOT blindness — mature trust is calibrated by evidence.

---

## Caveats (knowledge gaps from the research)

1. **Edmondson 7-item exact wording** varies across secondary reproductions; the version in refs is the most common, but verify against the 1999 ASQ original before any formal measurement.
2. **Team positive:negative ratio** — the marital 5:1 is well replicated; the *team* threshold rests partly on Losada's math, later critiqued (Brown, Sokal & Friedman 2013). The direction (high performers run positive-heavy) holds; treat a specific team number as heuristic, not law.
