## Definitions

- [statement] An LST is a token for staked assets on a proof-of-stake chain that keeps earning staking rewards while staying usable in DeFi. An LRT is a token that makes a restaked position (staked tokens, LSTs, or native BTC tokens securing extra protocols) liquid. Lido's ReGOOSE post frames LRTs as the riskier, actively managed product and the LST as the commoditized one. ## Structure and components

- [statement] EigenLayer slashing is opt-in and isolated by design. AVSs group operators into Operator Sets, each with its own slashing conditions. An operator allocates part of its delegated stake as Unique Stake that only one AVS can slash. A staker is exposed only if their operator registers for a slashable set and allocates stake to it. Kiln adds that delegated assets follow the operator's chosen allocation percentages, so they become slashable at once. - [statement] Withdraw is limited to lamports above the account's still-staked amount plus its rent reserve. If the stake has not been deactivated, the staked amount is the full delegation, so nothing delegated can be withdrawn during warmup. Once the current epoch is at or after deactivation_epoch, the staked amount is the remaining effective stake, so lamports that already cooled can be withdrawn in part during a multi-epoch cooldown. Withdrawing the entire balance (closing the account) needs a staked amount of zero. The withdraw authority must sign. A lockup in force blocks withdrawal unless the lockup custodian signs, but it does not block delegating, deactivating or splitting. ## How it works

- [statement] In the Steward code (master branch), the Config account holds an admin key that can change the parameters and blacklist authorities, call SPL stake pool instructions that need the staker signature, and pause or reset the state machine. Scoring and rebalancing are permissionless: Jito says any cranker can run them.
