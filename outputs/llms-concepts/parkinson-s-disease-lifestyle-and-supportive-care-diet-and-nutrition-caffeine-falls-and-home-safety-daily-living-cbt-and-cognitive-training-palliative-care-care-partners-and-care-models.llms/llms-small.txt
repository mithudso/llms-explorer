## Measurements and reference values

- [statement] Courage-PD estimated OR 0.74 (95% CI 0.60–0.93) for PD per standard-deviation increase in genetically predicted smoking initiation. It found no significant coffee or alcohol association but cautioned that conclusions for those two exposures were limited by insufficient statistical power. MR interpretation depends on valid instrumental-variable assumptions. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9211765/
- [statement] In Café-PD, 121 people with established PD were randomized to caffeine 200 mg twice daily or placebo. Caffeine did not significantly improve the primary motor outcome at six months; early modest sleepiness improvement attenuated over time, and secondary outcomes included slightly worse cognitive scores and increased dyskinesia. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664303/
- [statement] Protein redistribution requires nutritional and medication monitoring: the 2023 practical review advises avoiding low-protein diets below 0.8 g/kg/day to prevent lean-mass loss, monitoring weight and dyskinesias, and considering levodopa dose adjustment when bioavailability improves. Its minimum is not a universal optimal protein prescription. (low confidence) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-023-00541-w
- [statement] The 2017 national trial randomized 195 people and added 4 virtual specialist visits over 1 year to usual care. It reported 98% completing at least one virtual visit, 91% of scheduled virtual visits completed, and median savings of 88 minutes and 38 miles per visit. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5595275/

## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] The 2020 caffeine meta-analysis reported a progression association from cohort endpoints such as dyskinesia, motor fluctuation and levodopa initiation, but explicitly noted that none of its included studies isolated pure caffeine tablets and that an optimal caffeine dose could not be defined. Its pooled progression association should not be treated as randomized evidence of disease modification. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7353179/
- [statement] In a one-year randomized trial in early PD, transdermal nicotine did not slow progression. Of 163 randomized participants, 101 contributed to the primary endpoint; discontinuation and adverse events were important causes of dropout, and application-site skin reactions were common. The result does not support prescribing nicotine to slow established PD. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38320207/
- [statement] A 2026 UK Biobank analysis reported no overall coffee–PD association, but associations differed by CYP1A2 genotype: below 5 cups/day was associated with lower risk in AA carriers, whereas at least 5 cups/day was associated with higher risk in AC and CC carriers. The authors explicitly describe these observational findings as hypothesis-generating and noncausal. (low confidence) — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-026-01519-0
- [statement] Lower PD incidence associated with coffee consumption should be distinguished from treating established PD: EPIC4PD observed an inverse risk association, while Café-PD found no clinically important motor benefit from caffeine treatment. ## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] Prospective observational evidence frequently associates habitual caffeine or coffee consumption with lower incident PD risk: the 2020 cohort meta-analysis reported HR 0.797 (95% CI 0.748–0.849), and EPIC4PD also reported an inverse association. These are risk associations rather than intervention effects. (medium confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7353179/ · keywords: Parkinson's disease lifestyle and supportive care: diet and nutrition, caffeine, falls and home safety, daily living, CBT and cognitive training, palliative care, care partners and care models · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11175631/
- [statement] In the 2024 EPIC4PD analysis of 184,024 participants, the highest coffee-intake category versus nonconsumption had adjusted HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.46–0.88). Its nested study of 351 incident cases and 351 controls associated higher prediagnostic caffeine, paraxanthine and theophylline with lower subsequent PD risk; adjustment for smoking and alcohol did not substantially change results. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11175631/
- [statement] In Café-PD, caffeine 200 mg twice daily did not improve the six-month motor outcome versus placebo in established PD. Sixty participants received caffeine and 61 placebo; the between-group MDS-UPDRS-III difference was −0.48 points (95% CI −3.21 to 2.25). This treatment result does not test primary prevention. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664303/
- [statement] The 2023 correction to Hong’s caffeine meta-analysis fixed citation numbers in tables and figures; the authors stated that the scientific conclusions were unaffected. It should be consulted alongside the original meta-analysis rather than described as a retraction. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9919867/
- [statement] Two Mendelian randomization studies reported inverse smoking-related associations with PD, but for different exposures: Courage-PD found an association for genetic liability to smoking initiation, whereas Dominguez-Baleon and colleagues found an association for smoking continuation (current versus former smokers). These estimates do not identify nicotine as the responsible component. - [statement] Nicotine has not demonstrated clinical benefit as a PD treatment: a randomized early-PD trial found that one year of transdermal nicotine did not slow progression, and a 2025 meta-analysis found no significant pooled short- or long-term motor improvement. The meta-analysis includes the trial and is corroborating synthesis rather than an independent replication. (high confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38320207/ · keywords: Parkinson's disease lifestyle and supportive care: diet and nutrition, caffeine, falls and home safety, daily living, CBT and cognitive training, palliative care, care partners and care models · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12384040/
