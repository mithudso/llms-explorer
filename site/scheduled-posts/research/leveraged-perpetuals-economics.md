## 1. The result that reframes the question

Write P&L on collateral at leverage `L`:

```
pnl%_collateral  = L·move − (fee_rt·L + rate·util·hours·L)
break_even_move  = fee_rt + rate·util·hours          ← L cancels
```

**Leverage cancels from this model’s break-even equation.** Under its linear fee and carry assumptions, a 2× and a 250× position need the same price move to break even before liquidation. This does not imply equal risk or execution costs.

The model also changes **how close the absorbing barrier sits** and **how fast carry consumes collateral**. Real margin rules, price impact and liquidation can break the simplified comparison.

## 2. The four costs, and what each one kills

| Cost | Magnitude | What it kills |
|---|---|---|
| **Round-trip fee** | 12 bps notional (6 each way) | **Frequency** — multiplied by L *and* by trade count |
| **Borrow carry** | ≤0.016%/hr SOL custody (~140% APR); ≤0.003%/hr stable (~26.3% nominal annualized at constant full utilisation) | **Duration** — never negative, never pays you. Pure theta. |
| **Liquidation assumption** | **100% of remaining collateral in this model** | **Being eventually right** — path-dependent |
| **Liquidation drift** | Carry accrues against collateral | **Fixed stops** — the barrier walks toward you |

The borrow fee is the one most often misread. It is **not** a funding rate: both sides
always pay, it is never negative, and nobody is ever paid to wait. Longs borrow the
volatile custody, shorts the stable one — which is where the asymmetry in §5 comes from.

## 3. Break-even move by holding period

Utilisation 50%. Leverage-independent, per §1.

| Hold | Long (SOL custody) | Short (stable custody) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 h | 0.128% | 0.121% |
| 4 h | 0.152% | 0.126% |
| 1 day | 0.312% | 0.156% |
| 3 days | 0.696% | 0.228% |
| 1 week | 1.464% | 0.372% |
| 1 month | **5.880%** | 1.200% |

**Carry overtakes the round-trip fee at ~15 h for longs, ~80 h for shorts** (7.5 h / 40 h
at full utilisation). That is the hinge. Below it you are in a fee-dominated regime where
trade *count* is the enemy; above it a carry-dominated regime where *time* is. They
reward opposite behaviour, and a strategy that straddles the hinge pays both.

## 4. The number that should govern leverage choice

Time until carry **alone** zeroes the collateral, with the price perfectly flat:

| Leverage | util 50% | util 100% | Adverse move to liquidate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2× | 260.4 d | 130.2 d | 50.00% |
| 5× | 104.2 d | 52.1 d | 20.00% |
| 10× | 52.1 d | 26.0 d | 10.00% |
| 20× | 26.0 d | 13.0 d | 5.00% |
| 50× | 10.4 d | 5.2 d | 2.00% |
| 100× | 5.2 d | 2.6 d | 1.00% |
| 250× | **2.1 d** | **1.0 d** | **0.40%** |

At 250× and full utilisation, this model exhausts collateral in about a day through carry alone, on a flat chart. And a **0.40%**
adverse move exhausts the modelled collateral before any additional margin or liquidation rule is considered.

These are the *optimistic* readings: they show when collateral reaches zero, before the
liquidation penalty and before price impact.

## 5. Which strategies survive

### High cost hurdles under these assumptions

**Grid and market-making.** Fee cost is `12 bps × L × round-trip count`:

| Round trips | 1× | 5× | 10× | 20× |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 1% | 6% | 12% | 24% |
| 50 | 6% | 30% | 60% | 120% |
| 100 | 12% | 60% | **120%** | 240% |
| 500 | 60% | 300% | 600% | 1200% |

**100 round trips at 10× cost 120% of initial collateral in fees alone.** Gross trading gains would have to exceed that cost before carry and other charges. The arithmetic establishes a high cost hurdle, not that every possible spacing or payoff is impossible. Values in the table are rounded to whole percentages; 10 round trips at 1× cost 1.2%, shown as 1%. It also compounds the closed-trade
illusion already measured for grid unlevered — closed P&L stays positive and green while
the account drains, and leverage accelerates the account line without touching the
closed-trade line.

**Mean reversion and averaging down.** The absorbing barrier removes "eventually right,"
and averaging down raises exposure to that assumed barrier. This model does not establish an optimal strategy ranking.

**Long-horizon holds at high leverage.** 5.88%/month break-even for longs at 50%
utilisation, before any price impact.

### What the structure actually favours

- **Few trades, short holds** — under the ~15 h hinge, where fees dominate and carry is
  still small.
- **Convex payoff with a hard stop well inside the liquidation price.** Dying at your stop
  is a partial loss; dying at liquidation is a total one. Those are different events, and
  the distance between them is the entire risk budget.
- **Moderate leverage**, because the liquidation price drifts toward you as carry accrues —
  a stop that was safe at entry converges on the barrier while you hold.
- **Shorts are ~5× cheaper to carry in the stated rate assumptions** (stable vs volatile custody). The original report also quoted **85–90% long** open interest. No dated primary observation for that estimate is retained here, so it is not a verified current market fact or an input to these calculations.

## 7. The caveat that outranks everything above

**This is cost structure, not edge.** Across 48 out-of-sample months of prior measurement,
every strategy family tested lost to buy-and-hold *unlevered*, and the parameter sweep's
Deflated Sharpe called every winner indistinguishable from best-of-N luck.

Leverage rescales an edge that already exists. It does not create one. Applied to a
negative edge it simply reaches zero faster — and now with an absorbing barrier, so the
*path* decides the outcome rather than the average.

## 8. Honest limits of this model

- Per-custody rates are **Gauntlet's May-2024 recommendation**, not a live read.
  Utilisation moves continuously; these are order-of-magnitude frames, not quotes.
- **Price impact is not modelled.** The original author lacked sufficient validated parameter documentation to calculate it here. This does not establish that current public data could never support such a calculation.
- Also unmodelled: keeper request-to-fill drift, priority fees and Jito tips, failed
  transactions, and the liquidation penalty itself.
- Additional nonnegative fees add cost. Keeper request-to-fill drift can help or hurt a particular fill; its direction was not established by this model.
- Venue parameters change. Consult current official Jupiter documentation before using a venue parameter; the historical assumptions here are not executable quotes.
