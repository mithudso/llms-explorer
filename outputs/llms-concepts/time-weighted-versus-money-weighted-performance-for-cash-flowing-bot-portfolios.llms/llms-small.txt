## How it works

- [statement] Time-weighted return (TWR) seeks to neutralize external cash-flow timing to evaluate the underlying investment management. Money-weighted return (MWR) reflects the investor’s experience, including the amount and timing of deposits and withdrawals as well as investment performance. The GIPS handbook, Fidelity help and IBKR white paper make this distinction. ## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] In the GIPS handbook, IRR is a commonly used method for MWR, defined in prose as the rate equating present values of inflows and outflows. An MWR calculated by IRR uses a single IRR over the measured period, rather than linking sub-period IRRs. Fidelity’s performance help also describes its personal money-weighted return as an internal rate of return. This does not make every reported MWR an exact IRR calculation. - [statement] The GIPS handbook defines fair value using an orderly arm’s-length sale between willing parties, prioritizes an observable unadjusted quote for an identical investment in an active market when available, and includes accrued income. This is scoped valuation guidance for marking held assets; a bot comparison still needs an explicit inventory-pricing policy rather than substituting only closed-trade proceeds for portfolio valuation. (low confidence) — https://www.gipsstandards.org/standards/gips-standards-for-firms/gips-standards-handbook-for-firms/
- [statement] The GIPS handbook allows beginning-of-day or end-of-day external-flow assumptions but emphasizes establishing and consistently applying the policy.
