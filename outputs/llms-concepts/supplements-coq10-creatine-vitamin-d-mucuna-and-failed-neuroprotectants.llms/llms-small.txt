## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] Two coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) trials in early Parkinson disease reported conflicting results: an eighty-subject dosage-ranging trial (Arch Neurol 2002) reported less functional decline at 1200 mg/d than with placebo (P =.04) and concluded that CoQ10 appears to slow deterioration pending confirmation in a larger study, while the phase III QE3 trial (600 participants; JAMA Neurol 2014) was terminated after a prespecified futility criterion was reached and showed no evidence of clinical benefit; a 2026 review suggests the discrepancies might stem from different sample sizes and study durations. (high confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12374491/ · keywords: Supplements: CoQ10, creatine, vitamin D, Mucuna and failed neuroprotectants · also: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24664227/, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442935/
- [statement] Two published meta-analyses of randomized trials of coenzyme Q10 in Parkinson disease reported no significant benefit over placebo: a 2016 analysis of five RCTs (981 patients) found that the overall effect did not favor either group for total UPDRS, and a 2017 analysis of eight studies (899 patients) found a pooled WMD of 1.02 for UPDRS part 3 (p = 0.54) and stated that routine CoQ10 treatment of PD could not be recommended. (high confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26553164/ · keywords: Supplements: CoQ10, creatine, vitamin D, Mucuna and failed neuroprotectants · also: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27830343/
- [statement] A 2014 Cochrane review (searches to 10 November 2013; two RCTs, 194 patients) reported no clear evidence of an effect of creatine on motor function (MD -0.26; 95% CI -4.39 to 3.88; low quality evidence) and judged the evidence base limited by risk of bias, small sample sizes and short duration, and a 2017 BMC Neurology meta-analysis (five RCTs, 1339 participants) reported no significant differences in total, mental, activities of daily living or motor UPDRS scores, with an improvement in Schwab & England Scale scores, and concluded that creatine has no observed benefit in PD. - [statement] Published conclusions on isradipine in early Parkinson disease differ: the STEADY-PD III trial (336 patients; 5 mg of immediate-release isradipine twice daily or placebo for 36 months) reported that long-term treatment did not slow clinical progression (treatment effect -0.27 point, P = 0.85), whereas a re-analysis of STEADY-PD II data (10 mg/day extended-release isradipine) reported significantly smaller UPDRS scores than placebo at the end of the 52-week trial, with post hoc adjustment for symptomatic therapy diminishing the statistical significance, and suggested that clinically attainable brain exposure to isradipine may slow early-stage progression. ## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] In the SURE-PD3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (298 participants with early PD at 58 US sites; inosine titrated to raise serum urate to 7.1-8.0 mg/dL, n = 149, versus placebo, n = 149, for up to 2 years), the study closed early after a prespecified interim futility analysis and MDS-UPDRS progression rates did not differ significantly (11.1 points per year for inosine versus 9.9 for placebo; difference 1.26, 95% CI -0.59 to 3.11; P = .18); more kidney stones occurred with inosine (7.0 versus 1.4 stones per 100 patient-years) and the authors concluded the findings do not support inosine as a treatment for early PD. (high confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8441591/ · keywords: Supplements: CoQ10, creatine, vitamin D, Mucuna and failed neuroprotectants · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13442935/
- [statement] In a double-blind, randomized, controlled, crossover study (Neurology 2017) of single-dose intake in eighteen patients with advanced Parkinson disease, Mucuna pruriens (MP) powder from roasted seeds at a high dose containing 17.5 mg/kg of levodopa induced greater motor improvement at 90 and 180 minutes, longer ON duration and fewer dyskinesias than dispersible levodopa plus benserazide (3.5 mg/kg), and the authors concluded that single-dose MP met all noninferiority efficacy and safety outcome measures; a 2025 systematic review likewise states that this trial found MP significantly reduced dyskinesia at both 90 and 180 min compared to levodopa plus a decarboxylase inhibitor. (high confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5539737/ · keywords: Supplements: CoQ10, creatine, vitamin D, Mucuna and failed neuroprotectants · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12377966/
- [statement] Sources differ on adverse events with Mucuna pruriens (MP) in Parkinson disease: a 2025 systematic review (5 clinical trials, 108 participants; one study rated high quality, one with some concerns and three low quality) found MP associated with fewer adverse events and no dyskinesia reported while calling current clinical evidence limited, whereas a 12-month open-label phase 2 randomized trial in thirty-two untreated patients reported more frequent adverse events with MP than with levodopa plus a decarboxylase inhibitor (56% vs. 37.5%, p = 0.48; not statistically significant) and stated that larger double-blind trials with extended follow-up of 24–36 months are needed.
