## How it works

## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] The Camp Lejeune result cannot uniquely attribute the association to TCE: exposure was inferred from residence, diagnostic data were limited to VHA/Medicare users, and PCE, vinyl chloride, benzene or mixtures could have contributed. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10186205/
- [statement] Air-pollution findings differ across settings: a Taiwan nationwide study reported positive associations for several pollutants that persisted in multipollutant models, while a Northern Ireland study found no overall association for medium-term PM₂.₅ or NO2 exposure and medication-defined PD onset; its under-50 subgroup showed a PM₂.₅ association. These exposure windows and outcome definitions limit direct comparison. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12322442/ · keywords: Epidemiology and environmental risk factors: paraquat, TCE, air pollution · also: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12521372/
- [statement] The same Global Burden of Disease analysis reported male-to-female ratios of age-standardised prevalence rates of 1·40 (95% UI 1·36-1·43) in 2016 and 1·37 (1·34-1·40) in 1990, and estimated 211 296 deaths (95% UI 167 771-265 160) due to Parkinson's disease in 2016. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30287051/
- [statement] The Global Burden of Disease investigators concluded that the global burden of Parkinson's disease more than doubled over the past generation as a result of increasing numbers of older people, with potential contributions from longer disease duration and environmental factors. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30287051/
- [statement] A 2023 narrative review in J Parkinsons Dis (Dorsey and colleagues) reports that trichloroethylene (TCE) was first linked to parkinsonism in 1969, that four case studies involving eight individuals have linked occupational exposure to TCE to PD, and that a small epidemiological study associated occupational or hobby exposure with a 500% increased risk; the authors postulate that TCE contributes to the global rise of PD and state that further research is necessary to examine this hypothesis. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041423/

## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] The Camp Lejeune cohort study found higher odds of PD among veterans stationed at the contaminated base than among Camp Pendleton veterans (OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.39–2.07). Exposure concerned water containing TCE and other volatile organic compounds, rather than an isolated TCE exposure. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10186205/
- [statement] The critical review by Atterling Brolin and colleagues argues that converging epidemiological and experimental evidence supports stronger environmental policy action; this is the review authors’ assessment of causality and prevention, rather than proof that every exposure causes PD. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11832802/
- [statement] A UK paraquat-manufacturing workforce mortality study found no evidence of increased PD mortality (SMR 0.67, 95% CI 0.18–1.72). It assessed death-certificate outcomes rather than incident PD and disclosed Syngenta funding. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157632/
- [statement] In the Global Burden of Disease analysis, 6·1 million (95% uncertainty interval 5·0-7·3) individuals had Parkinson's disease globally in 2016, compared with 2·5 million (2·0-3·0) in 1990; age-standardised prevalence rates increased by 21·7% (95% UI 18·1-25·3) over the same period. (low confidence) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30287051/

## Facts and statements

- [statement] Goldman and colleagues’ discordant-twin study associated TCE exposure with PD (OR 6.1, 95 percent CI 1.2–33). The very wide interval makes the effect size imprecise. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3366287/
- [statement] Xie and colleagues’ 2025 meta-analysis estimated a pooled OR of 1.10 (95 percent CI 1.03–1.17) for PD with each 5 μg/m3 increase in long-term PM2.5 exposure. This is an observational pooled association. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11861764/
- [statement] The 2025 Northern Ireland cohort found no adjusted overall association between medium-term PM₂.₅ or NO2 exposure and medication-defined PD onset, but reported a positive PM₂.₅ association in participants under 50. Its findings therefore do not support a uniform association across age groups. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12521372/
- [statement] A nationwide Taiwan cohort reported positive associations between air-pollutant exposure and PD incidence that persisted in multipollutant models; this observational result does not establish a universal exposure effect. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12322442/
- [statement] In the same projection, population ageing was the largest contributor to the projected growth in cases, ahead of population growth and changes in prevalence. (low confidence) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11881235/
