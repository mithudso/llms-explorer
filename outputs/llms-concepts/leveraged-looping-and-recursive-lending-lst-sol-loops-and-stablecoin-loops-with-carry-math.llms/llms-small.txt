## Measurements and reference values

None of the three quantifies the points, so whether a negative-carry loop stays open depends on a term these sources do not measure. ## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- [statement] Fixed-rate borrowing turns B into a known input for the term, at a price. Kamino says fixed-rate borrowing 'allows locking the borrow cost for the full term of a position, converting a variable-spread strategy into a predictable one', at the cost of 'typically paying a small premium over the variable rate at time of entry'. Notional charges an upfront fee on entry and on exit before maturity, and warns that its fixed borrow maturities 'may not align exactly with PT expirations': when fixed debt matures it 'converts to a variable rate', which reintroduces rate risk until the PT expires. (medium confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/how-it-works · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://docs.notional.finance/notional-v3/product-guides/leveraged-pendle-pts
- [statement] The execution route changes both the realised entry leverage and the cost. Kamino's developer docs say manual looping takes '8-10 separate transactions', is 'Exposed to price changes between each step' and takes 'Slippage on each swap individually'. They add that if borrow rates spike mid-loop 'you may end up with a worse position than intended — or fail to complete the loop entirely'. Atomic Multiply uses one flash-loan transaction with a 'single consolidated slippage check' and a 0.001% flash-loan fee, charged on open, adjust and close. The SENTRA Fluid example models six swaps in and six out. A quoted Net APY assumes the target L and the quoted B hold at entry; the same Kamino table lists no price exposure between steps for the atomic route and exposure at each step for the manual route. (medium confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/build/borrow/multiply/leverage-concepts · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://hackernoon.com/fluid-stablecoin-looping-mechanics-part-1-opening-and-closing-costs, https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/concepts
- [statement] Kamino documents the net APY equation as (Collateral Yield x Leverage) - (Borrow Rate x (Leverage - 1)), states that a negative spread has no floor, and warns that borrow rates are variable by utilization, so a positive spread can flip negative with no action by the holder. Kamino's strategies page names sustained SOL borrow rates above LST staking yield as the primary risk for SOL LST Multiply. (high confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/concepts · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/strategies
- [statement] Loopscale and Kamino both offer fixed-rate borrowing for loops. Loopscale's loops use fixed-rate loans and earn only while the LST yield exceeds the fixed rate. Kamino's docs say a fixed rate removes borrow-rate risk but typically costs a small premium over the variable rate at entry. No current fixed SOL borrow rate was found for either venue, so fixed-rate carry cannot be quantified from the sources read. (medium confidence) — https://docs.loopscale.com/protocol-concepts/loops · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/how-it-works
- [statement] Kamino-run auto-deleverage is a last-resort tool initiated by its Risk Council under extreme systemic stress: positions get a 72-hour warning, it targets the largest leveraged positions, and Kamino says it has never been triggered. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-11-06) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/risks
- [statement] Kamino applies a borrow factor to debt: borrow capacity = collateral value x Max LTV / borrow factor. A factor of 2.0 doubles the effective risk-adjusted LTV for the same real debt, so a position can be closer to liquidation than the raw market-value LTV suggests. A liquidation price computed from raw values needs the borrow factor applied to debt. (low confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/borrow/concepts
- [statement] Reserve caps and available liquidity bound how much looping a market can absorb, whatever its LTV. Aave's supply and borrow caps 'limit the total amount of a token that can be supplied and borrowed from a reserve'. Kamino curators can set a borrow limit per collateral inside each elevation group and a separate limit outside groups. Baude et al. add the liquidity constraint x*(l - 1) <= S - B (a loop cannot borrow more than the pool holds) and note that rates explode near full utilization, so it rarely binds. (medium confidence) — https://aave.com/docs/aave-v3/concepts/reserve · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://kamino.com/docs/curators/markets/elevation-groups, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14005v1
- [statement] Kamino states the Jito Market (SOL) has an interest-rate cap mechanism that bounds rate spikes for JitoSOL/SOL strategies. Kamino describes this as a deliberate limit on the primary risk of its highest-leverage LST product. This is a single vendor claim and the cap parameters were not verified. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-11-06) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/risks

## Comparisons and alternatives

- [statement] Higher eMode LTVs shrink the gap between operating LTV and the liquidation threshold, so at near-max leverage a modest adverse move in the yield spread (staking yield minus SOL borrow rate) matters more. Kamino names sustained borrow rate above LST yield, and LST smart-contract exploits, as the main liquidation paths for SOL-LST Multiply rather than SOL price moves. (medium confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/curators/markets/elevation-groups.md · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://kamino.com/docs/curators/markets/risk-parameters.md
- [statement] For SOL-denominated LST loops (JitoSOL, mSOL, bSOL, JupSOL against SOL debt) Kamino prices the LST by stake rate, LST price = SOL_staked / LST_minted, not by DEX price. Jupiter Lend pegged vaults price on an on-chain exchange or redemption rate, and its docs add that a drop in that rate (slashing, or a loss in the underlying strategy) can also bring a position to liquidation. ## Facts and statements

- [statement] The formula rearranges to Net = Y + (Y−B)×(L−1): unlevered yield plus the yield spread earned on each borrowed dollar. Kamino writes it as 'Net APY = Collateral Yield + Yield Spread × (Leverage − 1)'. SODAX writes it as effectiveNet = effectiveSupply + leverage × spread, where its 'leverage' is the borrowed amount per unit of deposit (that is, L−1), so the two forms are algebraically identical. Check which convention a calculator uses before comparing numbers. (high confidence) — https://kamino.com/docs/products/multiply/concepts · keywords: Leveraged looping and recursive lending: LST SOL loops and stablecoin loops with carry math · also: https://docs.sodax.com/developers/packages/foundation/sdk/functional-modules/leverage_yield/leverage_yield_apr
- [statement] Yield-side inputs in calculators may be trailing averages. SODAX takes the LSD staking yield from DefiLlama's latest apy value (a trailing 7-30 day window, per the SODAX doc) and falls back to a hardcoded value flagged stale:true if the fetch fails, so a calculator's Y can lag the live rate. (low confidence) — https://docs.sodax.com/developers/packages/foundation/sdk/functional-modules/leverage_yield/leverage_yield_apr
- [statement] The formula rearranges to Net = Y + (L−1)×(Y−B): the unlevered yield plus the spread earned on each borrowed unit. Kamino writes 'Collateral Yield + Yield Spread × (Leverage − 1)'; CryptoTicker writes 'result = yield + borrowed units × (yield − borrow rate)'. SODAX writes effectiveNet = effectiveSupply + leverage × spread, but its 'leverage' is debt ÷ equity = targetLTV/(1−targetLTV) = L−1; its code comment says this is 'Not total exposure' and that a UI 'Leverage' label shows 1 + that value. Check whether a calculator's 'leverage' means L or L−1 before comparing numbers.
