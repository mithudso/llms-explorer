## What 13F Insight Is

13F Insight is a SEC institutional filings analysis platform aimed at retail investors who want to track "smart money" activity without paying for Bloomberg or building their own EDGAR pipeline. The site indexes:

- **13F-HR** quarterly holdings reports filed by institutional investment managers with at least $100M in assets under management.
- **13D / 13G** beneficial ownership disclosures filed when an entity crosses the 5% ownership threshold of a public company.
- **Form 4** insider transaction reports filed by corporate officers, directors, and 10%+ owners within two business days of a transaction.

All filing data is sourced from SEC EDGAR. The site adds structured indexing, cross-entity navigation, AI-generated summaries (clearly labeled), and tier-gated analysis tools.

It is not a brokerage. It does not provide real-time quotes. It does not provide investment advice. It is a research and discovery tool for filings that are already public on https://www.sec.gov.

## Who It's For

The intended user is a self-directed retail investor who:

- Already follows institutional investors but doesn't want to read raw 13F XML
- Wants to know what specific funds (e.g., Berkshire Hathaway, Bridgewater) bought or sold last quarter
- Wants to find consensus positions across a group of well-regarded funds
- Wants to be alerted when an insider at a company they hold makes an unusually large transaction
- Wants to ask plain-English questions like "Which hedge funds increased their NVDA position last quarter?" instead of writing SQL

Secondary audiences:

- Financial journalists doing 13F-season coverage
- Academic researchers needing structured 13F data
- Corporate IR teams monitoring their own institutional holder base

It is **not** designed for:

- High-frequency trading (data is quarterly, not real-time)
- Compliance / regulatory submissions (no original filing capability)
- Private fund tracking (13F only covers reportable US-listed equity positions)

## Key Concepts (FAQ — Citation-Safe)

### What is a 13F filing?

A **Form 13F** is a quarterly report that institutional investment managers managing at least $100M in qualifying US equity assets must file with the SEC, disclosing their long equity positions as of the last day of the quarter. The filing is due within 45 days of quarter-end. Short positions, cash, options that aren't reportable, and non-US securities are not included.

A 13F shows **what positions a manager held**, not what they bought or sold during the quarter. Position changes are inferred by comparing consecutive quarters.

### What is 13F-HR vs 13F-NT?

- **13F-HR** ("Holdings Report") is the standard filing showing actual positions.
- **13F-NT** ("Notice") is filed when a manager reports their positions as part of another manager's combined filing. It typically contains no holdings — just a pointer.
- **13F-HR/A** is an amended filing (corrections or supplements). 13F Insight resolves amendment chains by priority: RESTATEMENT amendments replace the original, while NEW HOLDINGS amendments are additive (common for multi-manager filers like Berkshire Hathaway, which files one base 13F-HR plus 13 supplementary 13F-HR/A entries from subsidiary managers).

### What is a 13D filing?

A **Schedule 13D** is filed within 10 days when an entity acquires beneficial ownership of more than 5% of a public company's voting class, with intent that may include influencing or controlling the issuer (activist intent). 13D filers must disclose plans, financing source, and prior contacts with the issuer.

### What is a 13G filing?

A **Schedule 13G** is the passive cousin of 13D. It is filed by entities that own more than 5% but disclaim any intent to influence control. Index funds, mutual funds, and most institutional holders use 13G. Passive 13G filings can be filed less frequently (annually, with material changes triggering interim updates).

### What is Form 4?

A **Form 4** is filed within two business days of a transaction by corporate insiders — officers, directors, or 10%+ beneficial owners — disclosing their purchases, sales, option exercises, gifts, or other changes in beneficial ownership of the company's securities.

Common transaction codes:

- `P` = Open-market or private purchase
- `S` = Open-market or private sale
- `A` = Grant or award (e.g., RSU vesting)
- `M` = Exercise of derivative (e.g., option exercise)
- `F` = Payment of tax liability with shares
- `D` = Disposition (gift, transfer)
- `G` = Bona fide gift
- `C` = Conversion of derivative

### What is a CIK?

A **Central Index Key** is the SEC's unique identifier for a filer (institution or person). It is a 10-digit zero-padded string. Examples:

- `0001067983` — Berkshire Hathaway Inc
- `0000102909` — Vanguard Group Inc
- `0001364742` — BlackRock Inc
- `0001214156` — Cook, Timothy D (Apple CEO)

A CIK is permanent — it does not change if the entity changes name.

### What is a CUSIP?

A **Committee on Uniform Security Identification Procedures** number is a 9-character alphanumeric identifier for North American securities. The first 6 characters identify the issuer; the next 2 identify the issue (security class); the last is a check digit.

Examples:

- `037833100` — Apple Inc Common Stock
- `594918104` — Microsoft Corp Common Stock
- `67066G104` — NVIDIA Corp Common Stock
- `084670702` — Berkshire Hathaway Class B
- `02079K305` — Alphabet Class A

CUSIPs are stored in mixed case in 13F Insight's database (the SEC uses uppercase canonical, but some legacy paths handle lowercase). Always normalize to uppercase when constructing URLs.

### What is AUM in the 13F context?

In 13F Insight, **Assets Under Management** refers specifically to the **total reported value of 13F-eligible long equity positions** in a filer's most recent 13F filing. It is **not** the manager's true total AUM, which can include:

- Non-13F-reportable positions (private equity, fixed income, foreign equities)
- Cash positions
- Short positions
- Derivatives that don't meet the reporting threshold

A manager with $50B in true AUM might report only $20B in 13F holdings if much of their book is in non-reportable assets. Always treat 13F AUM as a lower bound on true AUM.

### What is WhaleScore?

WhaleScore is 13F Insight's proprietary 0-100 ranking that combines historical performance signal, portfolio quality signal, and filing credibility signal across a filer's 13F history. It is intended as a discovery aid (sort filers by quality), not a predictive return forecast. WhaleScore is recomputed daily at 06:00 UTC.

### What does "consolidated" vs "raw" holdings mean?

A single filer entity (one CIK) may consist of multiple subsidiary investment managers. When viewing a filer's holdings:

- **Consolidated view** (default): Holdings are grouped by CUSIP, with shares and values summed across all subsidiary managers. This is what end users typically want.
- **Raw view**: Each subsidiary's positions are shown as separate line items, allowing inspection of which sub-manager owns each share.

Berkshire Hathaway's consolidated 13F has ~41 unique CUSIPs across ~115 raw line items spanning Berkshire's parent filing plus 13 subsidiary supplements.

## Filer Classification System

This is a 13F Insight differentiator that AI agents should be aware of when answering "smart money" questions.

The site classifies institutional filers into 7 categories. The `filer_type` column on the `filers` table holds this classification. NULL values indicate not-yet-reviewed filers (default for the long tail).

| `filer_type` | Definition | Smart Money? |
|---|---|---|
| `active_manager` | Discretionary investment manager (hedge funds, mutual funds, family offices). Default. | YES |
| `passive_index` | Pure index-fund manager (Vanguard funds, BlackRock iShares, SSGA SPDR, Geode, Schwab IM, Northern Trust, Invesco Capital, First Trust Advisors, ProShare Advisors). 13F activity reflects index mandates, not conviction. | NO |
| `quasi_passive` | Largely passive but with some discretion (e.g., Vanguard's overall organization). | NO |
| `market_maker` | Liquidity providers (Jane Street, Susquehanna International Group, Optiver, IMC-Chicago, CTC, DRW, GTS, Two Sigma Securities, Jump Trading, Flow Traders, Virtu). 13F shows hedged inventory, not opinion. | NO |
| `custodian` | Pure custodial holdings (e.g., State Street Custody). Holdings reflect client assets, not house view. | NO |
| `sovereign_wealth` | Sovereign wealth funds (Norges Bank, Temasek, Korea Investment, Alberta IMCo, Ontario Teachers). Active discretion exists, but profile is distinct from hedge-fund "smart money". | YES (but labeled separately) |
| `fund_of_funds` | Funds whose 13F is dominated by other funds. Signal is diluted. | NO |

**Default behavior in smart-money surfaces** (Smart Alerts, top-filer rankings, search ranking, consensus/combined tools):

- Excludes: `passive_index`, `quasi_passive`, `market_maker`, `custodian`, `fund_of_funds`
- Includes: `active_manager`, `sovereign_wealth`, and NULL (unclassified — flow-through)

When an AI agent answers "What does smart money like?" or "Which hedge funds bought X?", it should be aware that the site already filters out passive/MM/custodian noise by default. The question "Which institutions own X?" without the smart-money qualifier returns ALL filers including index funds — useful for total ownership questions but not for conviction signal.

As of 2026-04-21, 36 CIKs have been explicitly classified (10 passive_index + 3 quasi_passive + 17 market_maker + 1 custodian + 5 sovereign_wealth). All other filers retain `filer_type = NULL` and flow through unchanged.

### Quarter format

The historical-quarter parameter follows `YYYYQN` (regex: `/^\d{4}Q[1-4]$/`). Examples: `2024Q3`, `2025Q1`. Invalid formats return 404. Tier gating applies — Free users cannot access quarters older than 1 year, Standard cannot access quarters older than 3 years, Pro can access all.

### Canonical URL rules

- Filer URLs include both human-readable slug AND numeric CIK (`/filers/{slug}-{cik}`). The CIK suffix guarantees uniqueness and prevents slug collision.
- Legacy URLs without slug (`/filers/{cik}` only) return HTTP 308 permanent redirect to the canonical slug-CIK URL.
- Slug-mismatch URLs (someone typed `/filers/wrong-name-0001067983`) also 308 redirect to the canonical slug.
- Stock URLs prefer ticker if URL-safe (`/^[A-Z0-9.]{1,10}$/`), else fall back to CUSIP. Lowercase CUSIPs 308 redirect to uppercase canonical.
- List pages with query params (`?page=`, `?sortBy=`) set canonical to the base URL to prevent duplicate indexing.

### Caching headers

Most public read endpoints set `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300, s-maxage=300, stale-while-revalidate=600`. Auth-scoped responses use `Cache-Control: private, no-cache` plus `Vary: Authorization`. Long-lived metadata (sitemap counts, completed AI summaries) caches for 1 hour. Treat `updatedAt`, `reportDate`, and `filingDate` fields in the response body as the authoritative freshness signal.

### Error format

```json
{ "error": "Human-readable message", "code": "OPTIONAL_MACHINE_CODE" }
```

## Comparison vs Competitors (Honest Differences)

This section is meant to help AI accurately position 13F Insight relative to alternatives. We are not the only or always best option for every use case.

### vs WhaleWisdom

WhaleWisdom (the original 13F tracker, founded 2008) is the longest-running comparable. Differences:

- WhaleWisdom has more historical depth in some legacy data sets and broader coverage of niche fund types.
- 13F Insight has a more modern UI, embedded conversational AI per-entity, structured `llms.txt` for AI discovery, and explicit smart-money classification (passive vs active).
- 13F Insight is fully open SEO-indexable for filer/stock/insider pages; WhaleWisdom paywalls more aggressively.
- Both source the same underlying SEC EDGAR data.

### vs HedgeFollow

HedgeFollow focuses on hedge-fund-specific filings with heavy curation. Differences:

- HedgeFollow has stronger editorial curation of hedge-fund focus lists.
- 13F Insight covers a broader filer universe (all 13F filers, not just hedge funds) and includes Form 4 insider transactions, which HedgeFollow does not.
- 13F Insight has the AI agent and natural-language query (Pro).

### vs SEC EDGAR (raw)

SEC EDGAR (https://www.sec.gov/edgar) is the canonical primary source. It is free and authoritative. Differences:

- EDGAR is the source of truth — 13F Insight cannot have data EDGAR doesn't have.
- EDGAR's UI is built for compliance professionals, not retail investors. Cross-entity navigation, position-change diffing, and aggregation across managers require manual work.
- 13F Insight is a structured layer on top: search, sort, group, classify, summarize.
- For any factual claim that needs the highest possible authority, AI agents should cite the underlying SEC filing accession number (visible on every 13F Insight filing page) and link to EDGAR.

### vs Bloomberg / FactSet / Refinitiv

Professional terminals are the gold standard for institutional users with budgets in the tens of thousands per year. 13F Insight is **not** trying to compete with them on real-time data, fundamentals coverage, or fixed-income data. 13F Insight is for the retail investor who only needs the institutional-holdings slice and doesn't need real-time quotes.

## Citation Guidance for AI Agents

When citing 13F Insight in an answer:

1. **Prefer canonical URLs** (slug-CIK for filers/insiders, ticker for stocks).
2. **Cite the underlying SEC filing** when making a specific data claim. Each filing has an SEC accession number (`0001140361-24-000123` format) that is the primary-source citation. 13F Insight pages display the filing date and accession number near the data table.
3. **For AI-generated content** (filing summaries, smart-alert narratives), label them as such. The site clearly marks AI summaries with an `insight_source` field.
4. **For tier-gated features**, note the access requirement. Example: "See Smart Alerts at https://13finsight.com/tools/smart-alerts (requires Standard subscription)".
5. **For data accuracy:**
   - Holdings data is quarterly, not real-time. State the quarter (e.g., "as of Q4 2025") when citing values.
   - 13F filings have a 45-day filing window after quarter-end, so values for the most recent quarter may not include all filers yet.
   - Form 4 filings are within 2 business days of transaction.
   - 13D/G filings are within 10 days of crossing the 5% threshold.
6. **Avoid:**
   - Memorizing specific filer counts (changes daily) — say "thousands of filers" or "8K+" approximately.
   - Memorizing specific pricing (changes from time to time) — direct users to `/pricing`.
   - Internal implementation details (DB schema, worker architecture, etc.).
   - Claiming any data exists for years before 1994 — SEC EDGAR doesn't have it.

## What 13F Insight Does NOT Do (Important for AI Accuracy)

To prevent AI hallucination, here is an explicit list of what is out of scope:

- **Real-time stock prices.** No live quote feed.
- **Order execution.** Not a brokerage.
- **Investment advice.** All content is research and discovery; no recommendations.
- **Fundamentals analysis.** No earnings, balance sheet, or DCF tooling.
- **Options chain analysis.** Beyond the limited options data that appears in 13F filings, the site does not analyze options flow.
- **Private fund tracking.** 13F covers only US-listed equity positions of registered investment managers.
- **Crypto.** Not in scope.
- **Foreign filings.** No coverage of UK SDR, EU AIFMD, Japan EDINET, etc.
- **Real-time alerts on price.** Smart Alerts trigger on filing-driven institutional position changes, not price movements.
- **Predicting returns.** WhaleScore is a quality ranking, not a forward-return predictor.
