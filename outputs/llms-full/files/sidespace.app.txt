## Why using Vertical Tabs

**Say Goodbye to Tab Clutter**

Vertical tabs provide a smart solution for managing your browser space, reducing the chaos of a crowded tab bar while freeing up more visible on-screen area.

**Quickly Spot the Tabs You Need**

When your tab bar is overflowing, horizontal tabs shrink to the point where their labels are unreadable. Vertical tabs solve this issue by offering ample room to display tab titles clearly, making it easier and faster to locate the one you need.

**Optimize Your Screen Layout**

Most monitors are wider than they are tall, which makes top-to-bottom space more limited. Shifting your tabs to a vertical layout takes advantage of the extra width on your screen, allowing you to make better use of the available space.

## Quick Methods: How to Restore Chrome Tabs Immediately

### Method 1: The Universal Keyboard Shortcut

The fastest way to **restore chrome tabs** is using the keyboard shortcut:

- **Windows/Chrome OS**: `Ctrl + Shift + T`
- **Mac**: `Cmd + Shift + T`

This shortcut reopens recently closed tabs one by one in reverse chronological order. You can press it multiple times to restore several tabs, and it works even after restarting Chrome (as long as your browsing history is intact).

### Method 2: Right-Click Context Menu

If you prefer using your mouse:

1. Right-click on an empty space in the tab bar
2. Select "Reopen closed tab" from the context menu
3. The most recently closed tab will reopen instantly

### Method 3: Chrome Menu Navigation

For bulk restoration:

1. Click the three-dot menu (⋮) in the top-right corner
2. Navigate to **History → Recently Closed Tabs**
3. Select individual tabs or entire sessions to restore
4. Look for "X Tabs" entries to restore complete browsing sessions

## Advanced Recovery: When Basic Methods Don't Work

### Chrome History Recovery

When keyboard shortcuts fail, your browsing history becomes your lifeline:

1. Press `Ctrl + H` (Windows) or `Cmd + Y` (Mac) to open full history
2. Use the search function to find specific websites
3. Look for time-stamped entries from your lost session
4. Click individual pages to reopen them

### Session File Recovery (Technical Method)

For advanced users, Chrome stores session data in specific files:

**Windows Location**:

`C:\\Users\\[Username]\\AppData\\Local\\Google\\Chrome\\User Data\\Default\\`

**Mac Location**:

`~/Library/Application Support/Google/Chrome/Default/`

Look for files named "Current Session," "Current Tabs," "Last Session," or "Last Tabs." However, this method requires technical expertise and doesn't always succeed.

### Chrome Crash Recovery

When Chrome crashes, it typically shows a "Restore pages? Chrome didn't shut down correctly" prompt. If you miss this prompt:

1. Immediately use `Ctrl + Shift + T` upon browser restart
2. Check `chrome://crashes` in the address bar for crash reports
3. Avoid opening new tabs or browsing until you've attempted recovery

If you work across a laptop, desktop, and phone, tab syncing isn’t a “nice to have”, it’s how you keep projects moving. But when **sync tabs on Chrome** suddenly stops working, you’re left re-opening research, losing context, and breaking your workflow.

## How to Sync Tabs in Chrome Across Devices (Official Method)

Chrome can sync open tabs (and more) when you’re signed in and Sync is turned on. Here’s the cleanest way to set up **tab sync for Chrome** on desktop.

### Step-by-step: enable Chrome Sync for open tabs

1. **Open Chrome** on your computer.
2. Click your **Profile icon** (top-right).
3. Select &#x2A;*Turn on sync…**
4. **Sign in** with the Google Account you want to use everywhere.
5. Confirm **Yes, I’m in** (or similar prompt).
6. Go to **Settings → You and Google → Sync and Google services** (wording may vary).
7. Under **Sync**, ensure it’s **On** and your data types include what you need (especially **Open tabs** / **History** depending on your setup).

> Tip: Use **separate Chrome profiles** if you use multiple Google accounts (work vs personal). Mixing accounts is one of the most common causes of “tabs not syncing.”

### How to sync tabs on Chrome across devices

1. Sign into Chrome on every device using the **same Google account**.
2. Turn on **Chrome Sync**.
3. Verify **Open tabs/History** is included in Sync settings.
4. Check **Tabs from other devices** in History (UI varies by Chrome version).

### Where to find your synced tabs in Chrome

Once Sync is running, you can access **synced tabs on Chrome** from other devices in a few common places (UI varies by version), such as:

- History menus (often includes “Tabs from other devices”)
- Search/address bar suggestions tied to synced history
- Your Google account activity tied to Chrome Sync

If you’ve done the steps above and still see **chrome sync tabs** missing, move to troubleshooting.

## Why Chrome Tabs Sometimes Don’t Sync

Knowing the “why” helps you fix it faster. Most Chrome tab sync failures come from:

- **Sync isn’t actually enabled** (signed in ≠ syncing)
- **Wrong Google account** on one device
- **Sync settings misconfigured** (tabs/history not included)
- **Network or sign-in issues** (captive portals, VPN quirks, expired auth)
- **Outdated browser version** causing compatibility or caching weirdness
- **Sync conflicts or corrupted local cache** (rare, but real)

## What to Do If Chrome Tabs Won’t Sync

### 1) Confirm you’re signed into the same Google account

**Symptom:** You swear sync is on, but nothing matches across devices.

**Fix:**

- On every device, open Chrome → Profile icon
- Verify the **exact same email** is logged in
- If it’s different, switch accounts or create a separate Chrome profile

### 2) Make sure Sync is ON (not just “signed in”)

**Symptom:** Bookmarks or passwords might sync, but tabs don’t, or nothing syncs.

**Fix:**

- Chrome Settings → **You and Google** → **Sync**
- Turn Sync **On**
- If there’s a prompt to confirm, accept it

### 3) Check Sync data types (tabs/history)

**Symptom:** Bookmarks sync, but **chrome sync tabs** doesn’t.

**Fix:**

- In Sync settings, review what’s included
- Enable**Open tabs** (or enable**History** if your Chrome version ties tab discovery to history syncing)

### 4) Turn Sync off → restart Chrome → turn Sync back on

**Symptom:** Sync looks on, but it’s “stuck.”

**Fix:**

- Turn Sync **Off**
- Close Chrome completely (quit the app, don’t just close the window)
- Reopen Chrome
- Turn Sync **On** again

### 5) Update Chrome on every device

**Symptom:** One device syncs, another doesn’t; behavior is inconsistent.

**Fix:**

- Update Chrome on all devices
- Restart after updating

### 6) Rule out network and sign-in issues

**Symptom:** Sync works sometimes, fails on certain networks.

**Fix:**

- Try another network (mobile hotspot is a quick test)
- Temporarily disable VPN/proxy extensions
- Confirm you can access Google services normally (account sign-in, Gmail, etc.)

### 7) Check for extension interference (quick isolation test)

**Symptom:** Tab sync breaks after installing privacy/security extensions.

**Fix:**

- Open an Incognito window with extensions disabled (or temporarily disable suspect extensions)
- Re-test syncing behavior

### 8) If Google open tabs won’t sync on Chrome: wait + verify account health

**Symptom:** Everything is configured correctly, but tabs lag or never appear.

**Fix:**

- Sync can be delayed, give it a short window
- Make sure your Google Account isn’t prompting security verification on one device
- Re-authenticate if needed (sign out/in to Chrome)

### Sync tabs on Chrome: the fastest fix

If **Chrome tabs won’t sync**, do these in order:

1. **Confirm the same Google account**is signed in on every device
   - Chrome → Profile icon → check the email address.

   - Chrome Settings → *You and Google* → *Sync* → **Turn on**.
3. **Enable the right sync items**
   - In Sync settings, ensure **Open tabs** (and/or **History**) is enabled.

   - On desktop: Quit Chrome completely, then reopen.

7. **Test extension interference**
   - Temporarily disable privacy/security extensions and retry.

   - If Sync is “stuck,” turning it off/on often re-initializes syncing.

# Side Copilot | Introducing Group Guard Settings - Customizable URL Grouping

## Revolutionizing Your Browsing Experience with Group Guard Settings

We're excited to announce a powerful new feature for the Side Copilot extension: Group Guard Settings. Group Guard Settings is a new functionality that allows users to define their own rules for grouping URLs. With this feature, you can create custom categories based on your unique browsing habits and preferences. This function empowers users to take control of their browsing experience by customizing how URLs are grouped and categorized. This feature complements our AI Grouping function. It's designed for users who want to prioritize specific grouping rules or aren't fully satisfied with the AI Grouping results. With Group Guard Settings, you can fine-tune your groupings to achieve a more perfect organization.

Let's dive into what this means for you and how it can transform the way you organize your online world.

## How to Use Group Guard Settings

Setting up your custom groups is straightforward:

1. Access the Side Copilot extension settings.
2. Navigate to the new "Group Guard Settings" section.

![Access the Side Copilot extension settings.](https://www.sidecopilot.com/blog/blog_009_01.webp)

1. Click "Add Group" to create a custom category.

![Click "Add Group" to create a custom category.](https://www.sidecopilot.com/blog/blog_009_02.webp)

1. Name your group (e.g., "Work", "Social Media", "Research").
2. Define the URL pattern for this group using [match pattern structure](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/match-patterns){rel="&#x22;nofollow&#x22;"}:

match patterns consist of three parts: *scheme*, *host*, and *path*. The scheme and host are separated by `://`.

`<scheme>://<host><path>`

a. The *scheme* component may take one of two forms:

| Form                                                                          | Matches                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `*`                                                                           | Only "http" and "https" and in some browsers also ["ws" and "wss"](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebSockets_API){rel="&#x22;nofollow&#x22;"}. |
| `http`, `https`, `ws`, `wss`, `ftp`, `data`, `file`, or `(chrome-)extension`. | Only the given scheme.                                                                                                                                           |

b. The *host* component may take one of three forms:

| Form                                    | Matches                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `*`                                     | Any host.                                 |
| `*.` followed by part of the hostname.  | The given host and any of its subdomains. |
| A complete hostname, without wildcards. | Only the given host.                      |

**\*Note that the wildcard** `*`&#x2A;*may only appear at the start.**

c. The *path* component must begin with a `/`.

After that, it may subsequently contain any combination of the `*` wildcard and any of the characters that are allowed in URL paths or query strings. Unlike *host*, the *path* component may contain the `*` wildcard in the middle or at the end, and the `*` wildcard may appear more than once.

1. Save your settings and watch as Side Copilot automatically categorizes your tabs!

## Match pattern examples

1. `*.google.com` **or** `*.google.com/*`

Matches any URL that is on a google.com host, with any path. Examples of matches include `http://mail.google.com` or `https://chromewebstore.google.com` or `https://www.google.com/chrome/ai-innovations/`

2. `https://*.google.com/search?*ai*`

Matches any URL using the `https` scheme, on a google.com host, with a path that starts with `search` and contains the keyword `ai`. Examples of matches include `https://www.google.com/search?q=ai`

3. `https://*/*ai*`

Matches any URL using the `https` scheme, on any host, with a path that contains `ai`. Examples of matches include`https://www.google.com/search?q=ai` and  `https://www.bing.com/search?q=ai` and `https://www.baidu.com/s?wd=ai`.

## How to Combine Group Guard Settings with AI Grouping

To better categorize your tabs, start by customizing rules with our Group Guard Settings feature. Then, use the AI Grouping Settings to automatically categorize the remaining tabs that don't match your custom rules.

To avoid conflicts between custom rules and AI Grouping results, you can enable a button `Filter out the grouped tabs`in the AI Grouping Settings. This ensures that when you use the AI Grouping feature, it won't affect or move tabs that are already categorized by your custom rules.

# Side Copilot | Customized Shortcuts in Side Copilot

## What Are Customized Shortcuts?

Customized Shortcuts let you save your **frequently used instructions** or **prompts as reusable commands** inside Side Copilot.

Instead of retyping the same message every time, you can **trigger a saved shortcut instantly**, streamlining your workflow and keeping your focus on what matters.

For example, you can create shortcuts like:

> 🧠 &#x2A;"Summarize this page"* – quickly generate a concise overview of your current tab.

> 🧹 &#x2A;"Close inactive tabs"* – tidy up your workspace in one click.

> 🔍 &#x2A;"Find similar articles"* – let Side Copilot research related content automatically.

## How to Add Customized Shortcuts in Side Copilot

There are **three easy ways** to create customized shortcuts:

### 1. From the Configuration Page

**Open Side Copilot → Settings → Shortcuts Templates.**

You'll see a list of official pre-built shortcut templates.

- You can **copy and modify a template**, or
- Click &#x2A;*"Add New Shortcut"** to create one from scratch.

In the creation dialog, fill in:

- **Shortcut Name** – how it will appear in your list.
- **Instructions** – the prompt or command the AI should perform.
- **(Optional) Under Advanced**, select an icon for easier recognition in your sidebar.

### 2. From the Chat Input (Slash Command)

While chatting with Side Copilot, type &#x2A;*"/"** (slash) in the message box.

You'll see a list of all existing shortcuts.

At the bottom of that list, click &#x2A;*"Add New Shortcut"** to create one directly from the chat window — perfect for when you come up with a new idea mid-conversation.

### 3. From Chat History

You can also turn past conversations into shortcuts:

- In your **chat record**, find a message or command you often reuse.
- Click &#x2A;*"Add to Shortcuts."**
- Give it a name, and that's it!

Now you can reuse it anytime without retyping the full instruction.

## How to Use Shortcuts

Once you've created shortcuts, there are **2 ways** to use them:

- Type &#x2A;*"/"** in the chat input to quickly access and trigger any saved shortcut.
- Click the Shortcut in the **Shortcuts Templates** settings page, choose a shortcut from **My Shortcuts** and then click **execute shortcut**.

When triggered, the shortcut will instantly run your saved prompt.

You can also **edit**, **hide** or **delete** shortcuts anytime from the **Shortcuts Templates** settings page.

## How to Hide a Shortcut?

All Shortcuts created in **My Shortcuts** will be listed in the quick list. If you don't want a shortcut appear in your quick shortcuts list, just click the **hide button** on the top right of the shortcut that you want to hide.
