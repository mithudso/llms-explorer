# Antigravity MongoDB scout audit

Version: 1.0.0. Verified: 2026-11-06. Delta: initial representative audit.

Antigravity supplied useful discovery leads, but this sample does not demonstrate
net time or token savings. Keep bounded source seeds and independently verify
their contents. Preliminary `established` flags cannot qualify research claims.

## Population and reproducible sample

The inventory contains 440 bundles: 432 ready and eight in progress. They contain
1,603 concept occurrences (1,534 exact concept-parent pairs), 4,379 source records
(3,220 distinct URLs), and 1,569 frontier proposals (1,544 exact pairs).
Of 640 concept occurrences with preliminary assertions, 2,466 assertion records
include 1,278 marked established. All 2,466 mark `volatile=false`, including
pricing and changing product features. These are observed artifact fields;
they do not prove the scout's execution history or the assertions' correctness.

Use the fixed seed `antigravity-audit-20261106`. In each of the October 31,
November 1 and November 3 strata, select the two ready nonempty bundles with
the lowest SHA256 of `seed|run_id`. Inspect the first concept and all its sources.
Add the lowest-hash repository and blog source records, then the earliest
October 29 stratum's lowest-hash bundle's first concept's first two sources.
The October 29 supplement was added after the initial selection; the original
18 records remain in the sample.

The resulting sample contains 20 source records from nine bundles, seven dated
concept samples, seven frontier proposals and six preliminary assertion examples.
It covers dates and source types; its percentages are sample descriptions,
not weighted estimates for the population.

The Activity Feed export and mongosync state/lag leads have useful documented
foundations. The mongosync restrictions assertion cites its state page and a
forum rather than its discovered limitations page. Retain the latter as a seed
and bind its own clauses to its own readable passages.

The App Services pricing hypothesis conflicts with its cited current billing
content. The separate discovery baseline found the surviving Triggers billing
and App Services lifecycle documentation. Product lifecycle and pricing need
current applicability checks. [Triggers billing](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/billing/triggers/),
[App Services lifecycle](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/app-services-deprecation/).

The proposed requirement to recreate a cluster for built-in Stream Processing
failover conflicts with current architecture documentation. That documentation
describes checkpoint replication to failover processors. An active-active
proposal remains a design hypothesis; its source does not establish the proposed
architecture. [Stream Processing architecture](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/atlas-stream-processing/architecture/).

Both supplied Scheduled Trigger paths return 404. The current canonical page
provides cron and catch-up behavior, but does not support the asserted overlap
suppression. Absence of that support does not prove the opposite behavior.
[Scheduled Triggers](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/atlas/atlas-ui/triggers/scheduled-triggers/).

Both current trees already contain the sampled webhook HMAC concept under a
different parent. Activity Feed export is covered; mongosync, billing, failover
and scheduling overlap current nodes, children or pack text. Other proposal
names lack exact matches, but that does not establish semantic novelty.
Current coverage cannot disprove a historical novelty observation.

Vendor documentation, source repositories and integrated WiredTiger represent
one MongoDB organization. A firsthand forum report supports its author's
specific anecdote. Multiple URLs do not establish independent organizations.
Today's 404 responses cannot prove that historical accesses were fabricated.

## Effort comparison

| Measurement | Observed effort |
| --- | --- |
| Full initial audit report checkpoint | 493.8 seconds |
| Final audit including baseline and semantic-overlap supplement | 619.7 seconds |
| Separate no-scout source acquisition for the same six initial topics | 54 seconds; seven queries, nine official pages, ten open actions |
| Baseline including report preparation | 140 seconds |
| Original scout generation cost | Unavailable |
| Comparable input, output and cached token counts | Unavailable |

The audit cost more elapsed time than the bounded discovery baseline. Their
scopes differ: the audit checks seed accessibility, assertions, novelty and
provenance; the baseline only acquires useful primary sources. The comparison
is nonrandomized, and an incidental Explorer search snippet limited the
baseline's blind isolation. These measurements do not establish a causal
speedup, total research cost, or library-wide return on investment.

Using the final audit time, and ignoring already-paid generation and later
corrections, this sample would need to avoid more than 47.7 seconds of discovery
per retained source to break even. That is a conditional calculation, not an
observed saving. The audit includes its own inefficient unfiltered inventory
output, extraction repair and bookkeeping; those costs remain charged to it.

Use the scouts for bounded URL retrieval and candidate questions. Independently
read retained sources, reconcile current coverage, and apply normal claim and
whole-question gates. The sample supports discovery value; it does not support
net token savings or importing preliminary prose as accepted research.
