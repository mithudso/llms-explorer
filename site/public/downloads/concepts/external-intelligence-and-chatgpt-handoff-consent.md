# External intelligence and ChatGPT handoff consent

## How it works

- A June 2024 independent analysis republished by TechXplore listed whether every ChatGPT action required explicit consent as an unanswered question. Current Apple and OpenAI instructions now describe different consent rules by OS flow and request type, so launch-era summaries of one universal prompt are not a reliable current rule. (medium confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://techxplore.com/news/2024-06-apple-insists-chatgpt-users-privacy.pdf)
- In the standard Siri flow, an explicit request to use ChatGPT is treated as authorization and goes directly to ChatGPT; if Siri determines ChatGPT might help without an explicit request, it asks first. The user can disable that confirmation. Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence offer a separate choice to use the extension. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- When a user links a ChatGPT account, OpenAI's existing data preferences and privacy policies govern the handoff. Chats can be saved in account history, and model improvement depends on account choices. OpenAI says the device receives basic account fields for linking, including name, email, profile image, workspace names, and billing plan. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-19) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)

## Problems, failure modes and limitations

- The ChatGPT extension must first be enabled and is off by default. In the version 26 Siri flow, an explicit “Ask ChatGPT” request goes directly to ChatGPT; inferred handoff asks for confirmation by default, and users can turn off that prompt, but photos and files still require confirmation. With Siri AI (Beta), OpenAI says request confirmation cannot be disabled and users must explicitly invoke ChatGPT. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- OpenAI's current setup guide says Siri AI confirmation cannot be disabled and users must explicitly ask Siri to use ChatGPT before sending a prompt, photo, or file. For non-Siri-AI version 26, the guide says beginning a request with “Ask ChatGPT” skips the prompt for that request, while photos and files still require confirmation. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10269382-setting-up-chatgpt-with-apple-intelligence)

## Facts and statements

- Apple’s current 27.0 support page lists the ChatGPT extension among supported iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Apple Vision Pro features. OpenAI’s setup page separates that extension from Siri AI, which it labels Beta, and says confirmation controls depend on whether Siri AI is active. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115)
- A ChatGPT handoff can include the request, chosen attachments or document contents, and limited context such as time zone, country, device type, language, and the feature in use. Apple says it obscures the user’s IP address from ChatGPT while providing an approximate general location for fraud prevention and legal compliance. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-20) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- Without a connected ChatGPT account, OpenAI says it processes requests to provide a response and comply with law, does not receive the user’s IP address, does not store requests, and does not train on them. Apple says OpenAI receives no information tied to the user’s Apple Account and must not retain or train on the request except where law requires it. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-20) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- Signing in is optional, but it changes the data-policy boundary: OpenAI account settings and privacy policies then apply. OpenAI says conversations may be saved in ChatGPT history and used to improve services depending on account settings and use; Apple’s notice says OpenAI may log requests, attachments, and session history and may use signed-in data to improve or train models. OpenAI exposes an “Improve the model for everyone” control for model improvement. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-20) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- OpenAI says connecting the account makes the account’s name, email, profile image, workspace names, and billing plan available on the device to complete the link; its integration page says existing OpenAI data preferences apply to Apple-originated conversations. The pages describe these fields as device-level connection data and do not say they are sent to Apple. (medium confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10269382-setting-up-chatgpt-with-apple-intelligence)
- If a user continues an Apple-started conversation in the ChatGPT app or website, OpenAI says that continuation is processed under its Privacy Policy. TechCrunch reported the same policy boundary in its December 2024 coverage. (medium confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-20) — [source](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9737562-how-your-data-is-handled-when-you-use-chatgpt-through-apples-integrations)
- Apple collects limited request and response metadata, such as request counts or approximate sizes, to operate the extension and prevent fraud. If users opt in to Improve Siri and Dictation or Improve Siri & Apple Intelligence, Apple may also log request audio, transcripts, and response metadata; Apple says these records are not tied to the Apple Account. (low confidence) (verified as of 2026-09-14) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- Apple says the ChatGPT extension is off by default, must be enabled before use, and can be disabled at any time. OpenAI says a ChatGPT account is optional: users can set up the integration without signing in. (high confidence) (verified as of 2026-10-23) — [source](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/chatgpt-extension/)
- A 2024 research paper on AI assistants in end-to-end encrypted systems identifies AI handoff as a potential confidentiality boundary and recommends careful defaults and consent practices. The paper is a general technical and legal analysis, not an audit of Apple's ChatGPT integration. (low confidence) — [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20231)
