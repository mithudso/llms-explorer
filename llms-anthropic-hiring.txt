## Interview Stages

### Stage 1: Recruiter Screen (30 min)

**Assessment focus:** Background, specific motivation for Anthropic, logistics check, preview of role/team.

**What they want to hear:** Genuine engagement with Anthropic's published research. Reference specific paper ("Scaling Monosemanticity" or Constitutional AI), specific safety challenge you find compelling, or gap in field Anthropic addresses.

**What gets you cut:** Vague motivation ("I'm passionate about AI"), inability to explain last project, asking about compensation too early.

**Preparation:** Write "why Anthropic" answer, read 2+ recent Anthropic papers, prepare 3 questions about team's current research direction.

---

### Stage 2: Technical Assessment

#### For Software Engineers:
- Two 45-60 minute live coding rounds
- Medium-to-hard LeetCode difficulty
- Heavy emphasis on verbal communication, not just solution correctness
- Common topics: graph traversal, BFS/DFS, dynamic programming, string manipulation
- Failure mode: Silent coding (if you stop talking >90 seconds, interviewers assume poor communication)
- Preparation: 30-40 LeetCode problems at medium-hard level, PRACTICE NARRATING your approach

#### For Research Scientists / Research Engineers:
- Take-home problem set OR presentation (48-hour window if take-home)
- Take-home: Shows rigor on ambiguous problem; reviewers care about reasoning process, not polish
- Presentation: Paper or project you led; Q&A is adversarial but genuine curiosity ("What would falsify this?")
- Fewer live coding questions than SWE; deeper technical discussion on ML systems, model architecture
- Preparation: For take-home, walk through failed attempts and reasoning; for presentation, prepare for "what would falsify this" questions on every major claim

---

### Stage 3: System Design or Research Presentation

#### System Design (SWE/Infrastructure):
- 60 minutes, distributed ML infrastructure focus (NOT generic design questions)
- Example prompts: "Design inference serving for millions of requests with P99 <200ms" or "Design petabyte-scale dedup pipeline"
- What separates strong candidates: Ask clarifying questions before drawing boxes; make tradeoffs explicit
- Failure mode: Starting with solution before establishing requirements
- Preparation: Pick 5-7 real design problems, practice explaining tradeoffs out loud, practice handling mid-design requirement changes

#### Research Presentation (Research roles):
- 30-45 min presentation + 30 min Q&A
- Audience: 2-4 people, at least one has read the work
- Q&A is adversarial: "What falsifies this?" "Why that baseline?" "What would you do differently?" "What does this NOT tell you?"
- Strong answer to "I don't know" path: "Here's how I would find out"
- Preparation: Give presentation to colleague who pushes back on every claim; answer "what falsifies" for each finding

---

### Stage 4: Values and Mission Alignment Interview (45-60 min)

**Assessment focus:** Can you reason clearly about AI safety? Handle disagreement? Operate under uncertainty? Intellectual honesty vs. performing conviction.

**Weighted equally as all technical rounds — failing it = no offer.**

#### Common question types:

1. **Disagreement & Intellectual Honesty:** "Tell me about when you believed your team's direction was wrong. How did you raise it?"
   - Strong: Names specific technical belief, how you raised it (1-on-1, doc, team meeting), outcome, what you learned
   - Weak: Conflict-avoidance dressed as collaboration

2. **Uncertainty & Epistemic Humility:** "How do you decide when you lack data?"
   - Strong: Concrete decision from history, specific missing info, framework you used
   - Weak: Ideal conditions that never existed

3. **Safety Reasoning:** "Tradeoff between capability and safety in current AI systems?"
   - NOT testing agreement with Anthropic positions
   - Testing whether you reason from first principles
   - Weak: Cite Constitutional AI/RLHF without explaining why they address failure modes
   - Strong: Think through tradeoffs live, even if less polished

4. **Mission Alignment:** "Why Anthropic specifically?"
   - Must go past "I believe in safety"
   - Name something Anthropic works on that's underexplored
   - Name tension in AI development Anthropic addresses differently
   - Be honest about uncertainty
   - Example: "I follow mechanistic interpretability work, especially sparse autoencoders on superposition. Understanding feature geometry is tractable path to verification. Most labs invest in capability; I want interpretability built alongside frontier models."

**Preparation:** Write 3-5 stories (disagreement handled well, decision under uncertainty, changed mind based on evidence). Practice in 2 minutes each using STAR format. Be specific and honest about what happened, not what sounds best.

### Role-Specific Interview Loop Variations

| Role | Rounds | Timeline | Emphasis |
|------|--------|----------|----------|
| **Software Engineer** | Recruiter, 2 coding, system design, values | 3-5 weeks | Clean code, communication, no research presentation |
| **ML Engineer / Research Engineer** | Recruiter, 1 coding (lighter), 1-2 ML technical, take-home/presentation, values | 4-6 weeks | ML systems architecture, published work deep-dives |
| **Research Scientist** | Recruiter, take-home/presentation, research methodology, values (weighted heavier) | 4-7 weeks | Experimental design, how you think about evidence, autonomy readiness |
| **Product Manager** | Recruiter, product sense, cross-functional scenario (research+engineering+safety), data/metrics, values | 3-5 weeks | Must know Claude.ai, API, system prompt behavior |
| **Operations / GTM** | Recruiter, 1-2 ops-focused, values (weighted heavy) | 3 weeks | Customer/partner interface, safety positioning |

## Timeline and Logistics

**Typical Duration:** 3-6 weeks (SWE: 3-5 weeks, Research: 4-7 weeks)

**Delays Come From:**
1. Interviewer travel schedules (Anthropic researchers frequent conference attendees)
2. Headcount approval timing (even if you pass, role may lack formal headcount)
3. Team matching (some research/eng roles tied to specific team; if your team doesn't offer but another interested, adds 1-2 weeks)
4. Reference checks (done BEFORE offer, not after)

**Follow-up Rule:** If no contact within 5 business days after a round, polite follow-up to recruiter is appropriate.

**Reference Preparation:** Prep references BEFORE onsite loop, not after verbal offer.

## Compensation and Negotiation

**Base Salary + RSUs + Bonus Breakdown:**
- Software Engineer (L4/L5): $250K-$400K total; $180K-$230K base; 4-year RSU vest with 1-year cliff
- Research Scientist / Research Engineer: $300K-$600K (higher with strong publication record)
- Product Manager: $220K-$350K

**Negotiation Strategy:**
- First offer is rarely final
- Most effective leverage: competing written offer from OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta FAIR
- Negotiate total compensation as single number, let recruiter structure base/equity/bonus
- Use competing process timeline as deadline, not open-ended negotiation

## Preparation Checklist

### Recruiter Screen Prep:
- [ ] Write out "why Anthropic" with specific angle
- [ ] Read ≥2 recent Anthropic papers (Constitutional AI, mechanistic interpretability, sparse autoencoders)
- [ ] Prepare 3 questions about team's current research direction
- [ ] Prepare 1-2 min story of technically hard problem you solved

### Coding Rounds Prep (SWE/ML Engineer):
- [ ] Solve 30-40 LeetCode problems (medium-hard)
- [ ] Record yourself solving 10 problems; listen back to verbal narration
- [ ] Practice explaining approach BEFORE writing code
- [ ] Practice handling interrupter mid-solution with requirement changes

### System Design Prep:
- [ ] Pick 5-7 design problems from System Design Primer / Designing Data-Intensive Applications
- [ ] Practice explaining every tradeoff out loud
- [ ] Have someone interrupt with requirement changes; practice adapting cleanly
- [ ] Focus on: requirements before solutions, explicit tradeoffs, clean pivots

### Research Presentation Prep:
- [ ] Give presentation to colleague
- [ ] Colleague asks: "What falsifies this?" for every major claim
- [ ] You can say "I don't know" but then: "Here's how I'd find out"

### Values Interview Prep:
- [ ] Write 3-5 stories using STAR format (2 min each)
  - Disagreement handled well
  - Decision made under uncertainty
  - Moment you changed mind based on evidence
- [ ] Practice on AI mock interview tools with real-time feedback
- [ ] Answer "why Anthropic specifically" with specific research/person/gap, not generic safety answer
- [ ] Be honest about uncertainty; calibrated doubt > false certainty

## Common Failure Modes and Fixes

| Failure Mode | Fix | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Vague motivation** | Name specific paper, safety challenge, or gap Anthropic addresses | Every candidate says "I want to work on AI safety" — specificity is differentiator |
| **Silent coding** | Talk through every decision; narrate dead ends; >90 sec of silence = red flag | Communication is weighted equally with solution correctness |
| **Starting system design without requirements** | Ask clarifying questions on read/write ratio, failure tolerance, budget, team size | Interviewers watch how you think; wrong answer that followed good questions ranks higher than right answer guessed |
| **Performing false conviction** | Be honest when uncertain; say "I don't fully buy that" if true; explain your actual reasoning | Anthropic specifically hires for intellectual honesty; false certainty gets caught in values round |
| **Under-preparing values round** | Prep values with same rigor as coding | Values round is equally weighted; many candidates ace coding then fail values |
| **Generic interview questions** | Ask: "What's the hardest unsolved problem your team is navigating?" or "What research direction are you most uncertain about?" | Shows you're thinking like colleague, not just candidate |
| **Not asking questions at end of rounds** | Prepare 2-3 specific questions for each round (technical for tech rounds, mission for values) | Silence or generic "what's culture like?" signals low engagement |
| **Treating recruiter as obstacle** | Keep recruiter informed; be direct about timeline and competing offers; ask for feedback after rounds | Recruiter is your advocate; they move good candidates faster |

## Anthropic's Hiring Philosophy

**What Anthropic Values (Hiring Signals):**
- Intellectual honesty and epistemic humility
- Reasoning ability (over polished answers)
- Specific engagement with AI safety research
- Ability to disagree constructively
- Clear technical communication
- First-principles thinking vs. memorized knowledge
- Genuine curiosity about the field's hard problems

**What Anthropic Does NOT Value:**
- Memorization of perfect LeetCode solutions
- Performing conviction you don't have
- Generic motivation ("I love AI")
- Silence during problem-solving
- False certainty over "I don't know, here's how I'd figure it out"

**Company Context:**
- Founded 2021 by former OpenAI researchers (Dario & Daniela Amodei, Chris Olah, Sam McCandlish, others)
- Focus: AI safety, interpretability, constitutional AI approach
- Scale: ~300-400 employees (as of mid-2026)
- Funding: $5B+ (Salesforce, Google, others)
- Valuation: $350B+ (as of late 2025/early 2026)
- Products: Claude.ai, API, enterprise deployments
- Team structure: Research, research engineering, product, safety, GTM/operations
