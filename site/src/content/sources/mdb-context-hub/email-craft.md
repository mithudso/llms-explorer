# Email Craft

General-purpose professional email writing. Optimized for the 90% of work email that isn't sales, isn't customer support, and isn't an exec announcement.

## Core Concepts

### 1. The subject-line contract

Two-part structure: `[Topic]: [Purpose]`. The colon does the work.

- "Q3 forecast: need your number by Thu noon"
- "Onboarding doc: review request (10 min)"

Prefixes: `Action:`, `Decision needed:`, `FYI:`, `Question:`, `Reminder:`, `Intro:`. Avoid `Following up`, `Quick question`.

Length: 2-7 words wins.

### 2. BLUF: Bottom Line Up Front

The first sentence states the conclusion, the ask, or the decision needed.

```
Bad:  "Hey, hope you're well. I've been thinking about the Q3 forecast..."

Good: "I need your Q3 forecast number by Thursday noon. Context below."
```

### 3. One ask per email

If the email has two asks, it has zero asks. Split into two emails, each with its own subject line.

Test: can you summarize the ask in one sentence starting with a verb? "Please review X by Friday."

### 4. To / Cc / Bcc: a deliberate decision

- **To**: people who must act or reply.
- **Cc**: people who need to be informed but don't owe a reply. Use sparingly.
- **Reply-all**: default to No. Only when the answer materially changes what other recipients should do.

### 5. The "would this fit in Slack" test

Email is for:
- Anything you want a searchable, durable record of.
- External recipients.
- Long-form content (>3 paragraphs).
- Anyone whose Slack hours don't overlap yours.

### 6. The "phone vs email" judgment

Pick up the phone when:
- The topic is emotional, sensitive, or carries firing/conflict/HR risk.
- You're on email round 3+ on the same thread without resolution.
- Tone-sensitive feedback, layoffs, performance, breakups.

### 7. Follow-up cadence

- Day 0: original email
- Day 3-4: polite nudge, reply on the same thread
- Day 7-10: third nudge, change channel
- Beyond: stop. The non-answer is the answer.

### 8. The breakup email

```
Subject: Closing the loop on [topic]

Hi [name] — I haven't heard back on this, so I'll assume it's not a
priority right now and stop following up. If that changes, drop me a
line and I'll pick it back up. Thanks for considering it.
```

### 9. Send-time decisions

- Internal, ongoing relationship → send when drafted.
- External, senior, or first contact → schedule Tue-Thu 10am recipient-local.
- Drafted after 9pm → always schedule for 9am next workday.

### 10. Signature hygiene

A good signature is 4 lines or fewer:
```
First Last
Title, Team
Company  |  Phone (if relevant)
```

### The Five Cs of email — pre-send quality check

1. **Clear.** Can the reader understand the message on a single read?
2. **Concise.** Is every sentence earning its place?
3. **Complete.** Does the email contain everything the reader needs to act?
4. **Correct.** Spelling, grammar, names, titles, numbers, dates, attachments.
5. **Courteous.** Is the tone proportionate to the relationship and the stakes?

**The five things to never write:**
1. "Please be patient."
2. "We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused."
3. "Per my last email..." / "As I mentioned..."
4. "Unfortunately..."
5. "This is a known issue." (without immediately following it with a workaround)

## Templates

### Template: action-required ask

```
Subject: [Topic]: [verb] needed by [date]

Hi [name],

I need [the specific thing] by [date/time]. Here's the context: [1-3
sentences max]. Reply with [the shape of the answer you need].

Thanks,
[name]
```

### Template: breakup / close-the-loop

```
Subject: Closing the loop on [topic]

Hi [name] — I haven't heard back, so I'll assume [topic] isn't a fit
right now and stop following up. If that changes, just drop me a
line. Appreciate you considering it.
```

## Anti-Patterns

- **The wall of text** — five paragraphs, no whitespace, no BLUF.
- **The hidden ask** — buried in paragraph 4.
- **Reply-all "thanks!"** — multiplies inbox noise by N for zero value.
- **The midnight send** — signals burnout culture. Schedule for next morning.
- **Bcc'ing the boss as surveillance** — corrosive, almost always discovered.
